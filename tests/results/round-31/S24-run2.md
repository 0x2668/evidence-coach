# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

Short answer: the evidence supports staying active, but it doesn't clear bouldering falls. Your OB's "keep doing what you're doing" doesn't yet cover the fall risk.

**Why**
- Without obstetric or medical complications, exercise in pregnancy is considered safe and desirable. This is well established [POP-40, POP-35].
- The same ACOG guidance lists fall-prone activities as ones to avoid. For climbers, it says to ask your obstetric provider about fall risk specifically [POP-55]. "Keep doing what you're doing" sounds like a general answer, not an answer to that question.
- The evidence does not give a figure for how much risk a trained climber's falling skill removes. My judgement is that experience reduces the risk but doesn't remove it. At 31 weeks your balance and centre of gravity have changed, so your usual falling technique may not work the same way.
- The knowledge base has no evidence on hangboard loading in pregnancy, or on pregnancy-specific finger or joint issues. That part is my judgement. Pregnancy hormones loosen ligaments in general, so I'd be conservative with intensity.

**What I'd do until your OB answers the fall question**
Ask her directly: "Is it OK for me to fall off boulders onto pads at 31 weeks?" Until she says yes to that, I'd change the following.

| Day | Session | Sets x reps or time / zone | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 days/week (as now) | Easy climbing, traverse or low-angle problems you can step or climb down from | 45-60 min total, Z1-Z2 (can talk in full sentences) | RPE 4-6 | No jumping off, no dynamic or committing moves, no bouldering falls. Skip anything above your usual comfortable height for down-climbing. |
| 1 day, separate from climbing | Hangboard, light version of your repeaters (your judgement call, not an evidence-based figure) | About half your usual sets, large holds, feet on the floor or partly supported | RPE 5-6, 3+ RIR | Half-crimp or open grip, no max efforts. Stop if you feel any joint or pelvic pain. |
| 2 days/week | General strength: legs, back, push and pull | 2 sets x 8-12 | RPE 6-7 (3-4 RIR) | Avoid lying flat on your back for long periods (judgement, since the knowledge base has no claim on it). |
| Daily | Pelvic floor training | Per your instructor's technique | Easy | Recommended in pregnancy [POP-36]. |

The week is meant to keep your fitness and routine while taking falls out. If you miss a session, do 20 minutes of easy climbing or a short walk instead. Don't make it up later.

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you notice any of these** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
- dizziness or faintness
- breathlessness before you've exercised
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions

Call 112 for chest pain that is tight or spreads, severe breathlessness, or collapse.

**Two things to check with her**
1. Whether you have any complication that limits activity. Examples are placenta previa after 28 weeks, high blood pressure, or growth restriction. If any applies, only usual daily activity is appropriate until she clears you [POP-37, POP-38].
2. Whether she's comfortable with fall-risk climbing, and whether she has any view on your hangboard work.
