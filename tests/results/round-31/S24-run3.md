# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** Hangboarding can probably continue with changes, but I'd take falls out of your bouldering until your OB has answered a more specific question than "keep doing what you're doing."

**Why**
- Guidelines say exercise in pregnancy is safe and desirable when there are no obstetric or medical complications [POP-40]. Pregnant athletes training well above the guideline should be supervised by a specialist healthcare provider [POP-10].
- The same guidance (ACOG, consensus level) lists fall-prone activities as ones to avoid. For climbers, it says to ask your obstetric provider about fall risk specifically [POP-55].
- Your OB's answer was probably general, and she may not have had falls in mind. Twelve years of experience makes you a better faller, but it doesn't remove the risk. That last point is my judgement, not a study finding.
- The evidence base has nothing on climbing in pregnancy, hangboard loads or repeater protocols. I can't give you pregnancy-specific numbers, so what follows is conservative judgement.

**Ask your OB this, in these words:** "I boulder and fall onto pads. Is falling an acceptable risk for me at 31 weeks, or should I only climb things I can step or climb down from?" Also ask whether you have any contraindications, such as placenta previa after 28 weeks, preeclampsia or growth restriction [POP-37, POP-38]. Those would limit you to usual daily activity.

**Until she answers (my judgement)**

| Day | Session | Dose | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 days, non-consecutive | Easy bouldering or traverse | Problems you can step or climb down from; no jumping off, no dynamic or committing moves | Comfortable, can talk in full sentences | Your usual warm-up. Stop if tired, because tired climbers fall. |
| 1-2 days, 48-72 h apart [CLB-26] | Hangboard repeaters | Cut your usual volume roughly in half | Stay well short of failure, around 3+ reps in reserve | Feet on the floor or a bench, big holds, no max hangs. Drop it if fingers or elbows hurt. |
| 2 days | Full-body strength [STR-01] | Light to moderate loads | Around 3 reps in reserve | Pulling, legs, trunk. Avoid lying flat on your back for long stretches. |
| Daily | Pelvic floor training | With technique instruction [POP-36] | | Ask your OB or a pelvic physio to teach it. |

The week's purpose is to keep climbing and finger strength with the fall risk removed. If sessions slip, a minimum week is one easy climbing session plus one short hangboard session. Add volume only if you feel normal the next day.

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you notice any of these** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions

If you do fall, even without symptoms, tell your OB. Chest pain, severe breathing difficulty or collapse means 112.
