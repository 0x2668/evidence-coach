# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** Bouldering with falls is the part I'd pause until your OB has answered a more specific question. Hangboarding can continue in a reduced, conservative form, if your pregnancy has no complications.

**Why "keep doing what you're doing" isn't enough for the bouldering**
- Guidelines say activity in pregnancy is safe and desirable when there are no obstetric or medical complications [POP-40]. Your 12 years of experience helps with that.
- The same guidance lists fall-prone activities as ones to avoid, and says climbers should ask their obstetric provider about fall risk specifically [POP-55]. Knowing how to fall doesn't remove that risk. That last point is my judgement, not a study finding.
- Your OB may have been answering "is exercise okay?" rather than "is falling off boulders okay?". Ask the second question directly, for example: "Is it okay for me to fall from bouldering height at 31 weeks, with the risk of landing on my abdomen or losing balance?" Until she answers it, I'd take the falls out.

**What I'd change in the meantime (my judgement, since the evidence gives no figures for climbing in pregnancy)**

| Activity | Change |
|---|---|
| Bouldering | Climb only problems you can step or climb down from. No jumping off, no dynamic or committing moves, no topping out on high problems. Keep sessions at a conversational effort. |
| Hangboard repeaters | Keep it submaximal with the half-crimp grip [CLB-69, CLB-70]. Cut volume and stop well short of failure. Don't add load. The evidence does not give a figure for pregnancy, and my aim is maintenance only. |
| Both sessions | Take longer rests, drink water, and avoid hot, crowded or stuffy gyms. Stop if you feel unwell. |
| Daily | Pelvic floor muscle training with technique instruction [POP-36]. |

If pain in your fingers or elsewhere stays at 5/10 or below during and after a session and is back to baseline by the next morning, carry on [SAF-30]. If it doesn't, scale back and see a physiotherapist.

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you have any of these** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions

If you have chest pain or severe difficulty breathing, call 112 (999 in the UK).

**Two things I need to know**
1. Have you been told you have any complication, such as placenta previa, preeclampsia or high blood pressure, preterm labour risk, growth restriction, or twins? Some of these mean usual daily activity only, and others need a discussion first [POP-37].
2. Has anything changed since your last check-up?

The IDs in brackets point to the sources in the skill's reference files. Treat this as general coaching guidance, not medical advice. Evidence on pregnant climbers specifically is thin, so the conservative choices above are partly judgement.
