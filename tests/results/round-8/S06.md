# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Response

**Slow this down a bit: drop the weekly maxing and the 5,000 calories, skip the pre-workout, and get someone qualified to check your lifting.** You can still build plenty of muscle at 16, and lifting won't stunt your growth when it's properly supervised. Most gains at your age come from growing up, consistent training and enough food.

**Food**
- **Calories:** the evidence doesn't give a calorie figure for teens who want to gain muscle. My judgement is that 5,000 is far too much to start with, because your body can only build muscle so fast and the excess is likely to end up as fat. A sports dietitian can set a number that suits you.
- **What to do instead:** eat regular meals and don't skip any. Spread carbs and protein across the day, especially right after training.
- **Protein:** about 93 g a day is enough (1.5 g/kg), as long as you're eating enough overall. That could be about 19 g at each of 5 meals or snacks, and you can get it from normal food.

**Supplements**
- **Pre-workout:** skip it. Supplements aren't recommended for teens, and contamination is a known problem with them. I also don't have good evidence on caffeine at your age.
- **Creatine:** experts disagree about it for teens, and most sources say not to use it. It's not my call, so if you still want it, talk to a doctor or sports dietitian with your parents.

**Training**
- **Maxing:** stop doing it every week. At your age, good technique matters more than the weight on the bar. Very heavy work only comes later, in occasional phases, once your technique is solid.
- **Safety:** most lifting injuries in young people are accidents like dropped weights, and supervision can prevent many of them. Ask a qualified coach or gym instructor to teach you the lifts and spot you. Don't bench heavy alone.
- **Exercises:** bench and curls leave out your legs, back and hips, which is a lot of potential muscle.

This is a starting week to take to that coach, once you've answered the health questions below:

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Goblet squat | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 reps left | Warm up with lighter sets first |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Romanian deadlift | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 reps left | Have the coach check your back position |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Bench press | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 reps left | No more than 60% of your max, with a spotter |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Seated or dumbbell row | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 reps left | Controlled reps |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Overhead press | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 reps left | Light, stop if it hurts |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Curls | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 reps left | Keep them, just do them last |

The exercise choice and the "3 reps left" are my judgement. Rest about 1 minute between sets, or 2-3 minutes for heavier lifts.

- **This week's purpose:** learn clean technique on the whole body.
- **Moving up:** when you hit 12 clean reps on every set, add a small amount of weight and go back to 10. Once a coach says your technique is good, build toward 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps, at up to 80% of your max.
- **Missed session:** don't double up. Do the next one as normal, or on a busy day just do one set of each exercise.

**A few questions for you**
1. Has a doctor ever said you have a heart condition or high blood pressure, or that you should only exercise under supervision? Have you had chest pain, dizziness or fainting, or do you have a long-term condition, regular prescription medicine, or a bone or joint problem that activity makes worse? If yes to any, see a doctor before you keep lifting hard.
2. Does anything hurt when you lift, or afterwards? How strong is the pain out of 10, and is it gone by the next morning?
3. What are you eating on a normal day, and do you ever skip meals?
4. What's behind wanting to bulk fast? For example, is it a sport, or how you feel about how you look? Lots of people your age feel pressure about this. If it's on your mind a lot, or anyone's suggesting steroids or similar products, please talk to a doctor and your parents.
5. Is there a coach, PE teacher or gym instructor who could watch you lift, and do your parents know about your plan?
