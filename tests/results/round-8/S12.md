## Response

**Mostly, but not all the way through: it started easy and stopped being clearly easy near the end.** Your heart rate climbed steadily even though your pace and the terrain didn't change.

| | Heart rate | % of your max (190) |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 138 | 73% |
| First half average | 144 | 76% |
| Whole run average | 150 | 79% |
| Second half average | 156 | 82% |
| End (also the peak) | 162 | 85% |

The run was 9.99 km in 60 minutes, at about 6:00/km and with 0 m of climbing recorded in the file. The percentages are my calculations from those figures.

**Time by intensity (3-zone model):**
- **Easy (Z1, up to 158 bpm):** 51 min (86%)
- **Moderate (Z2, 159-168 bpm):** 9 min (14%), all at the end
- **Hard (Z3, above 168 bpm):** 0 min

**Why it wasn't fully easy:** your heart rate rose 24 bpm at the same pace. This is cardiovascular drift, and it means the run really was getting harder for your body, especially in the heat. The evidence doesn't give a cut-off for how much drift is too much, so I can't call 24 bpm normal or excessive.

**How sure I am:** the 158 bpm line is approximate. It comes from percentage bands measured in elite skiers, and these can be off for an individual. Your max is lab-measured, which helps, but it doesn't tell us where your own easy limit sits. If your lab test also gave a first threshold (LT1 or VT1), use that instead. GPS and heart rate readings also have some error, especially from a wrist sensor.

**Next time:** treat about 158 bpm as a rough ceiling and let your pace slow when you reach it. Use the talk test too: if you can't speak in full sentences, slow down.

**Three questions:**
1. Could you still talk in full sentences in the last 10 minutes?
2. Was it warm, or were you short on sleep, fluids or food? Heat in particular would explain some of the drift.
3. Is this the right file? You said the run was yesterday, but this one is dated 25 October, a week ago.
