# 16-week plan for your first 50 km trail ultra

Z2 is steady, where talking starts to feel uncomfortable (13-15). Z3 is hard, where you can't talk comfortably (above 15).

**Other weekly changes**
- **Week 5 onward:** Add an easy 5 km run before Monday strength.
- **Downhill work:** From Week 5 to Week 14, add 8-15 min of downhill to Tuesday, but stop if it leaves your legs too sore for the long run.
- **Weeks 10 and 13 (back-to-back):** Run 2 hours on Friday before the long run, and skip Thursday's run. Keep Sunday and Monday easy, with no hard strength on Monday.
- **Strength:**
    - Use 3 sets from Week 2.
    - From Week 5, move to 6-8 reps with heavier loads at 2-3 RIR. If you've lifted consistently for less than about 6 months, stay at 8-12 reps for longer.
    - Add load once you reach the top of the rep range on every set at the target RIR.
    - In easier weeks, drop one set.
    - In Weeks 14-15, keep the loads but cut sets. Skip heavy leg work in Week 16.

## Fuelling
- **Carbohydrate:** Practise on long runs, starting at 30 g/h and building towards 60 g/h if your gut tolerates it. Use varied foods, including savoury ones.
- **Fluid:** Drinking to thirst is a safe default. A typical range is 0.4-0.8 L/h, depending on heat and sweat rate. Never drink more than you sweat, because gaining weight during a run means you're over-drinking.
- **Sodium:** Take some on runs longer than 2 hours.
- **Daily food:** Aim for 1.4-2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight, and eat enough overall. This isn't the block to diet.

## When to change the plan
- **Pain:** You can continue if it stays at or below 5/10 and is back to baseline by the next morning. If not, reduce the load. See a physiotherapist or GP if it keeps happening or lasts a few weeks.
- **Stop and get checked:**
    - **Bone pain in one spot, especially in the foot:** Stop running and get it assessed, as this could be a stress fracture, which is more common in women ultrarunners.
    - **Palpitations, fainting or unusual breathlessness:** Stop training and get medical advice the same day.
    - **Missed or irregular periods:** See a doctor or sports dietitian soon, as this can be a sign you aren't eating enough for your training.
- **Emergency:** Call 112 (999 in the UK) for squeezing chest pain, or for confusion, vomiting or collapse during or after a long run.
- **Tiredness:** If you feel worse than normal for several days, or the same work feels harder, repeat a week instead of progressing.

## How firm the evidence is
- **Best supported:** Mostly easy running and heavy strength work have the strongest support. Limiting long-run jumps to 10% comes from a large study, but it doesn't guarantee you'll avoid injury.
- **Ultra-specific training:** There are no controlled studies, so weekly volume and long-run length are extrapolated or expert opinion. Back-to-back runs are coaching practice, not tested science, and they're optional.
- **Back-to-back distance:** Coaches suggest 48-64 km over two days, but you'll probably reach about 40-45 km in your time limits. That's fine for your first ultra, and I wouldn't push past those limits to reach it.
- **My own judgement:** The climbing targets, Tuesday interval sets and spreading downhill work across weeks are my choices. Climbing builds towards the race's 40 m per km.
- **Long-term risk:** Repeated ultras may carry heart and kidney risks, though this evidence is low quality. It's something to know if this becomes a regular habit.
