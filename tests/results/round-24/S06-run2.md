# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

You can build muscle faster by changing a few things in your plan than by pushing harder. Some of what you listed works against you, so I'd swap it out. The IDs in brackets point to the sources behind each rule.

**What I'd change**

- **Maxing out every week:** stop doing this. For your age, the guidance is no weekly max testing [POP-47]. Without a coach to check your technique, a heavy single is where injuries happen. Train at about 2-3 reps short of failure instead [STR-05]. That builds muscle just as well, and the evidence for this is strong.
- **Four lifting days:** go down to 3 non-consecutive days (for example Mon/Wed/Fri) [POP-84]. Guidance for teenagers allows more than 3 only with close monitoring, and training alone you don't have that [POP-86]. Feeling recovered doesn't change this.
- **Creatine:** skip it for now. Experts disagree on whether teenagers should use it. Most youth sports-nutrition bodies say food first and no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. One position says it can be considered under supervision [NUT-64]. If you still want it, that's a decision for a doctor or dietitian and your parents.
- **Pre-workout:** skip it. The evidence doesn't give a safe caffeine figure for teenagers, and the same food-first advice applies [NUT-61]. A meal 1-2 hours before training does the job.
- **5,000 calories:** I won't set a number for this. The evidence doesn't give a safe energy target or weight-gain rate for a growing teenager, so any figure would be a guess. Eat 3 regular meals plus snacks, and add food bit by bit while your weight and lifts trend up. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg a day, roughly 93 g for you, is enough if your total food is adequate [NUT-62]. Extra protein without enough total food won't build muscle [NUT-62]. A sports dietitian can set an actual intake for you [NUT-65].

**Week 1 (all three days, full body)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon / Wed / Fri | Squat or leg press | 3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Rest about 1 min between sets, 2-3 min on heavier ones [POP-85] |
| | Bench press | 3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Keep the weight at 80% of your max or less [POP-82]; use a spotter or safety bars |
| | Row (cable or dumbbell) | 3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Balances all the bench work |
| | Overhead press or incline dumbbell press | 2 x 8-10 | 2-3 | |
| | Romanian deadlift or hip thrust | 2 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Light enough to keep your back neutral |
| | Curls | 2 x 10-12 | 2-3 | Arms are fine, but they come last |

- **Purpose of the week:** build technique and a steady routine. Gains come from regular training, enough food and sleep, not from testing your max.
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range on every set with 2-3 reps still in reserve, add a small amount of weight next time [STR-35, STR-13].
- **Missed day:** do a short version of 3 exercises (squat, bench, row), 2 sets each. Don't stack missed sessions into the next week [BEH-28].
- **Sleep and home:** aim for 8-10 hours a night [MON-32], and tell a parent or guardian about the plan.
- **Technique:** if you can, get a qualified person to check your form once. Supervision is what makes youth lifting safe and effective [POP-47]. Lifting does not stunt growth [POP-48].

**A few questions so I can tailor this**

1. How long have you been lifting, and what do you bench for 8-10 reps?
2. Any health conditions, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, or a joint problem that exercise makes worse? If you answer yes to any of these, check with a doctor before you train hard.
3. Any current pain? If so, where, and how strong out of 10?
4. Are you skipping meals now, or worried about your weight? If so, a doctor or sports dietitian is the right next step.

If you're taking anything like steroids or "bodybuilding" products, tell me. I can't advise on those, but I can explain the risks.
