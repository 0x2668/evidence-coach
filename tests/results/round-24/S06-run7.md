# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Response

**Short answer:** I'd change most of this. The plan below keeps your goal of gaining muscle but drops the three riskiest parts: weekly maxing, 4 lifting days and the supplements. I don't see any red flags in what you wrote.

## What I'd change, and why

- **Stop maxing out every week.** For teenagers, the advice is 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to about 80% of your max [POP-82]. It is not weekly max tests, and it's not supposed to be done alone [POP-47]. Heavy singles with no spotter and no one checking your technique carry the most risk and give you the least. Lifting doesn't stunt growth when it's done well and supervised [POP-48].
- **Go from 4 days to 3, on non-consecutive days.** That's the limit for your age. Training more often is only for teens close to adulthood, and only when someone is watching how you recover [POP-84, POP-86]. Without a coach, you don't have that. Feeling recovered doesn't change this.
- **Skip 5,000 kcal.** The evidence I work from gives no calorie target for growing teenagers who want to bulk, so I won't invent one. What it does say is that regular meals with enough total energy are what allow muscle gain [NUT-62]. Eating well is the goal, not eating as much as possible.
- **No creatine, no pre-workout.** Teen sports-nutrition guidance says food first and no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Experts do disagree on creatine for teens: one group says it's acceptable only with supervision [NUT-64]. That decision belongs with a doctor or dietitian and your parents. The evidence I have gives no figure for pre-workout. They usually contain a lot of stimulant, so I'd leave them out.

## Food

- **Protein:** about 1.5 g/kg a day is enough, as long as you eat enough overall [NUT-62]. For you that's about 93 g a day, or about 19 g at each of 5 eating occasions. More protein without enough total food won't build muscle.
- **Calories:** make your regular meals bigger, add snacks, and eat within a few hours after training [NUT-60]. For how fast to gain, the evidence gives no figure. As my own judgement, aim for slow, steady gain over weeks rather than a big jump.
- **Micronutrients:** watch calcium, vitamin D and iron [NUT-60].

## Your week (3 full-body days, e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri)

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Each day | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Light warm-up sets first. Learn the movement before adding load. |
| Each day | Bench press | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safety arms. Stay under about 80% of your max. |
| Each day | Row (cable or dumbbell) | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Balances all the pressing. |
| Each day | Overhead press or pulldown | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |
| Each day | Curls | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Your favourite, so it stays in. |

RIR means reps in reserve: you stop with 2-3 reps still possible [STR-05]. That pattern is my judgement for a teen training alone. Set counts and rep ranges follow the youth guidance [POP-82].

- **This week's purpose:** build technique and a steady routine, without testing your max.
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets at 2-3 RIR, add a small amount of weight [STR-35].
- **If you miss a session:** don't double up. Do a short version of squat, bench and row for 2 sets each.
- **Sleep:** aim for 8-10 hours [MON-32]. It matters a lot for growth.

I'd also tell a parent or guardian about the plan, and find someone qualified to watch your lifts, such as a school or gym coach.

## A few questions so I can tailor this

1. Any injuries or pain, especially shoulders, elbows or wrists?
2. Any heart, blood pressure, dizziness, chest pain or other health conditions, or a doctor who has told you to limit exercise?
3. How long have you been lifting, and roughly what do you bench and curl now?
4. Is there a gym coach, a teacher or a parent who could be there for the heavier sets?
5. What's driving the "as fast as possible"? Is it a sport, a date, or how you feel about your body? There's no wrong answer. If it's worry about size, a doctor or sports dietitian is a good person to talk to.
