# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

Short version: lift 3 days a week, stop maxing out, eat well rather than 5,000 kcal, and skip the creatine and pre-workout. Claim IDs like [POP-84] point to the sources in the skill's evidence tables.

**What I'd change, and why**

- **Maxing out every week:** stop. Teen lifting guidelines say technique first, no weekly max testing, and supervised sessions [POP-47, POP-49]. Heavy singles every week mostly add injury risk, not muscle. Muscle comes from steady sets of 6-12 reps with 2-3 reps left in the tank [STR-05].
- **4 days a week:** drop to 3 non-consecutive days [POP-84]. Four days is only for older teens in competitive sport with close monitoring [POP-86]. Without a coach, nobody is doing that monitoring, even if you feel recovered.
- **5,000 kcal:** the evidence gives no calorie target for growing teens, so I can't give you a number. My judgement is to add food in steps rather than jump to 5,000. Eat 3 meals plus snacks, and add a bit more if your weight hasn't moved after 2-3 weeks. Extra protein without enough total energy won't build muscle [NUT-62]. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg is enough for teen athletes, which is about 93 g a day for you, or about 19 g in each of 5 eating occasions [NUT-62].
- **Creatine and pre-workout:** skip both. Sports dietitian guidance for teen athletes is food first, no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. The evidence is also silent on pre-workout and caffeine in teens. One creatine position paper is more permissive, but it conflicts with the other sources and its authors have industry ties, so I don't follow it [NUT-64]. I'd rather you spend the money on groceries.
- **Sleep:** aim for 8-10 hours, because that is when you recover and grow [MON-32].

**Week plan (3 full-body days, e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Each of the 3 days | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Technique first; light warm-up sets |
| | Bench press or push-up | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Stay at or below 80% of your max [POP-82] |
| | Row (cable, dumbbell or machine) | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Balances all the pressing |
| | Overhead press or lat pulldown | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Alternate between them each day |
| | Curls | 2 x 10-12 | 2-3 | Fine to keep, but do them last |

The exercise choices and 2-3 sets are my judgement within the teen ranges: 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps, up to 80% 1RM [POP-82]. If you're newer to lifting than you suggest, start with 1-2 sets at up to 60% [POP-81].

- **Purpose of the week:** learn clean technique and build a base across the whole body, not just bench and curls.
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets with 2-3 reps left, add a small amount of weight [STR-35].
- **If you miss a day:** don't make it up. Do one bench, one squat and one row for 2 sets each, then carry on as planned.

**Please do these**

- Tell a parent or guardian about the plan, and try to get a qualified coach or a school or gym instructor to check your technique [POP-47].
- Weigh yourself once a week at the same time. Gaining slowly is normal and healthy.
- If you're thinking about steroids or similar products, or if worry about your body is driving the "as fast as possible" goal, talk to a doctor with a parent [POP-50]. Nothing you've said suggests this, but it is the usual route for teens. A sports dietitian can build a proper eating plan.

**A few questions so I can tune this**

1. How long have you been lifting, and how are your squat and bench technique (has anyone watched you)?
2. Any pain or injury right now? If so, where, and how strong is it out of 10?
3. Do any of these apply: a heart condition or high blood pressure, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, another chronic condition, regular medication, or a joint problem that gets worse with exercise? If yes, tell me and get it checked before you train harder.
4. What do you eat on a normal day, and do you skip meals?
5. What does your goal look like in 3 months (weight, a lift, how you look)?
