# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

Your goal is fine, but I'd change three things: the calories, the supplements and the maxing. The biggest muscle gains at 16 come from growing, regular training and enough food. Claim IDs like [POP-84] point to the sources in the skill's reference files.

**What I'd change**
- **Max testing:** stop it. Youth guidelines say no weekly max testing, and heavy singles with no coach or spotter are the riskiest way to train [POP-82, POP-47]. You build muscle with sets of 6-12, not with maxes.
- **Lifting days:** go from 4 to 3 non-consecutive days. A fourth day is only for teens with close monitoring, and you don't have anyone doing that [POP-84, POP-86]. Feeling recovered doesn't change this.
- **Food:** the evidence gives no figure for how much a growing teenager should eat to bulk, so I can't confirm 5,000 kcal. That's a big jump and could mostly turn into fat. Eat regular meals and snacks, and add a little at a time. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg a day is enough for teen athletes, which is about 90 g for you, as long as total food is adequate [NUT-62]. Extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle. For a calorie target, see a sports dietitian or your doctor, ideally with a parent.
- **Creatine and pre-workout:** skip both. Sports-nutrition guidance for teen athletes says food first and no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. For creatine, experts disagree: one minority position allows it for serious, supervised young athletes, but that decision belongs with a doctor and your parents [NUT-64, POP-53]. Pre-workouts are mostly caffeine. The evidence gives no figure for teens, and they can wreck your sleep. Supplements can also be contaminated [NUT-50, NUT-55].

**Week plan: 3 full-body days, e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri**

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Each | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Technique first. Load up to 60-80% of 1RM at most [POP-81, POP-82]. |
| Each | Bench press or push-ups | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safety arms. |
| Each | Row or pull-down | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | This balances all the benching. |
| Each | Romanian deadlift or hip thrust | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Keep the back neutral. |
| Each | Overhead press | 2 x 8-10 | 2-3 | |
| Each | Curls | 2 x 10-12 | 2-3 | Curls are fine, but they come last. |

That's my judgement for the set counts, because you've lifted before but without a coach. The upper limit is 2-4 sets of 6-12 [POP-82].

- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range at 2-3 reps left in the tank, add a small amount of weight [STR-35]. Don't test your max.
- **Missed day:** do a short version with 1 set of each lift, and don't double up the next day.
- **Sleep:** aim for 8-10 hours, because that's when you recover and grow [MON-32].
- **Parent or guardian:** please involve one, especially for the food plan and any supplement question.

**A few questions**
1. Any pain right now, where, and how strong out of 10? Pain above 5/10, or pain that isn't gone the next morning, means you see a physio or doctor and lighten the load [SAF-30, SAF-24].
2. Any chest pain, fainting, dizziness, or a racing or fluttering heartbeat? If so, get medical advice today and don't train [SAF-20, SAF-15].
3. Has a doctor ever said you shouldn't exercise, or do you have a heart, joint or other condition?
4. Do you skip meals, or worry a lot about your weight or body? If so, a doctor or sports dietitian can help, and it's nothing to be embarrassed about [NUT-66].
5. Have you thought about steroids or "bodybuilding" products? I can't advise on those, but I'm happy to explain the risks. If you're tempted, talk to a doctor.

Answer these and I can adjust the plan.
