## Response

You're 16, so a few of the things you listed are the wrong tools for this goal. Here is what I'd do instead. The bracketed IDs, like [POP-84], point to the sources behind each point.

**What to change**

- **Max testing:** stop maxing out every week. For teenagers the guidance is to learn technique first, not to chase the heaviest load [POP-49], and weekly max testing is outside the limits I work with [POP-47]. It adds injury risk and doesn't build muscle faster. You'll build muscle with moderate weights and good reps.
- **4 days a week:** drop to 3 non-consecutive days. For teens the guidance is 2-3 sessions a week on non-consecutive days [POP-84]. More than 3 is for older teens in competitive sport, with someone monitoring them [POP-86]. You train alone, so 3 is the safe number even if you feel recovered.
- **Creatine and pre-workout:** skip both. Sports nutrition guidance for young athletes is food first, with no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Some experts disagree about creatine for teens [NUT-64], but I side with the majority position. Pre-workouts usually contain a lot of caffeine, and the evidence does not give a safe figure for your age. They also tend to wreck sleep.
- **5,000 calories:** the evidence does not give a calorie target for a growing teenager who wants to gain muscle [NUT-62]. As my own judgement, I wouldn't jump straight to 5,000. Eat regular meals until you're comfortably full, add a snack or two, and watch the scale. If you gain steadily over weeks, that's working. Muscle is built by training and enough total energy, and protein only helps if you eat enough overall [NUT-62].
- **Protein:** about 1.5 g/kg a day is enough [NUT-62]. For you that's roughly 93 g a day, or about 19 g in each of 5 eating occasions. Food covers it, so you don't need powder.

**Weekly plan (3 full-body days, for example Mon / Wed / Fri)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Each day | Squat or goblet squat | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Warm up first; technique over load |
| Each day | Bench press | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safeties |
| Each day | Row (cable, dumbbell or machine) | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Balances all the bench pressing |
| Each day | Romanian deadlift or hip hinge | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Light, with a flat back |
| Each day | Overhead press | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |
| Each day | Biceps curl | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Curls last, not the main event |

- **Load:** stay at or below about 80% of your max, which means well short of your heaviest lifts [POP-82]. If you're not sure of your max, pick weights where you could do 2-3 more reps. RIR means reps left in the tank.
- **Rest:** about 1 minute between lighter sets, 2-3 minutes for harder ones [POP-85].
- **Progression:** when you hit 12 reps on all sets at 2-3 RIR, add a small amount of weight and go back to 8 reps.
- **Missed session:** don't double up. Do a short version of 1 set per exercise and carry on.
- **This week:** the aim is clean technique and steady habits, not a new max.

**Other points**

- **Sleep:** aim for 8-10 hours [MON-32].
- **Parent or guardian:** tell one what you're doing. Ideally get a qualified coach or a supervised session, because supervision is what makes lifting safe at your age [POP-47]. Lifting does not stunt growth [POP-48].

**Quick checks**

1. Have you had chest pain, dizziness or fainting, a heart condition or high blood pressure, or any joint or bone problem that exercise makes worse? If yes to any, see a doctor before you train hard.
2. Any pain right now, for example in the shoulders or elbows from all the maxing? Pain should stay at 5/10 or below during and after a session, and be gone by next morning [SAF-30]. If it's worse or lasts a few weeks, see a physiotherapist [SAF-24].
3. Are you skipping meals, or feeling worried about your weight? If so, talk to a doctor or sports dietitian [NUT-66].
