# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

I'd change most of this plan. You can still build muscle, but at 16 some of it is the wrong way to do it.

**What I'd change**
- **Maxing out every week:** stop. Adolescent guidance is to focus on technique, not on the weight lifted, and that means no weekly max testing [POP-49]. Without a coach or spotter, a max bench is also where injuries happen.
- **4 days a week:** go to 3 non-consecutive days [POP-84]. Four is only for older teens in competitive sport, and only with someone monitoring them [POP-86]. Lifting alone, you'd have no one doing that.
- **Creatine and pre-workout:** skip both. Sports nutrition guidance for teen athletes is food first and no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. One expert group says creatine might be considered under supervision, but its authors have industry ties, so I don't act on it [NUT-64]. The evidence I have gives no figure for pre-workout in teens, and I'd avoid it.
- **5,000 calories:** the evidence doesn't give a safe calorie target or weight-gain rate for growing teens, so I can't back that number [NUT-62]. As my own judgement, eat three real meals plus snacks and a bit more than you do now. Muscle needs enough total energy and regular meals first. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg/day is enough, which is roughly 90 g for you. Spread it over about 5 meals or snacks, about 19 g each [NUT-62].
- **Sleep:** aim for 8-10 hours [MON-32].
- **Parent or guardian:** tell one what you're doing. Ideally train where a qualified coach or gym staff can check your technique, since supervised training is the safe setup at your age [POP-47].

**Week plan (full body, 3 days, for example Mon / Wed / Fri)**

| Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bench press or push-ups | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 (RPE 7-8) | Use a spotter or safety bars. Pause the bar on your chest. |
| Row (dumbbell or cable) | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Balances all the pressing. |
| Squat or goblet squat | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Your legs are missing from your current routine. |
| Romanian deadlift | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Light, hips back, flat back. |
| Overhead press | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |
| Curls | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Last, not first. |

- **Rest:** about 1 minute between sets, 2-3 minutes after the heavier exercises [POP-85].
- **Effort:** stop with 2-3 reps still in the tank. You don't need to go to failure [STR-05].
- **Progression:** when you hit 12 reps on every set at that effort, add a small amount of weight and go back to 8 reps [STR-35].
- **Purpose of the week:** build technique and steady muscle gain with room to recover.
- **If you miss a day:** don't double up. Do just the bench, row and squat for 2 sets each.

**Questions that would help me tailor this**
1. How long have you been lifting, and what weights do you use for bench and curls?
2. Any pain right now (where, and how strong out of 10)? If you have chest pain, dizziness or fainting, or a joint that gives way or can't bear weight, stop lifting and get medical advice today.
3. Any heart, blood pressure or other health condition, or any medication?
4. Are you skipping meals, or worried about your weight? If so, talk to a doctor or sports dietitian.
5. Have you been told to train only under supervision?
