# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Response

Short version: the goal is fine, but three of the four ideas (maxing out weekly, 5,000 kcal, creatine plus pre-workout) would work against you at 16. Here's a safer way to get bigger. The IDs like [POP-84] point to the sources behind each rule.

**What I'd change**

- **Stop maxing out every week.** For teenagers the guidance is no weekly max testing. The focus is technique, with moderate loads: 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to 80% of your max [POP-82, POP-47]. Maxing every week adds injury risk and doesn't build muscle faster.
- **Lift 3 days a week, not 4.** For teens, the guidance is 2-3 non-consecutive days [POP-84]. More than that needs someone monitoring you [POP-86], and you don't have a coach. Feeling recovered doesn't change this. Full-body sessions also train more than bench and curls alone.
- **Food first, no supplements.** Sports dietitian guidance for teen athletes is to meet needs from normal food and not use performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63].
  - **Creatine:** experts disagree. One consensus view says no. A minority view says it might be acceptable for serious, supervised young athletes [POP-53, NUT-64]. That decision belongs with a doctor or dietitian and your parents.
  - **Pre-workout:** the evidence on caffeine and similar products covers adults, not teens. These products can also wreck sleep, and you need 8-10 hours [MON-32].
- **5,000 kcal.** The evidence does not give a safe calorie figure or gain rate for growing teens. As my own judgement, I'd skip the big jump. Eat regular meals with a bit extra, and watch the trend over a few weeks. The extra mostly just adds fat if training is the limit.
- **Protein:** about 1.5 g/kg per day is enough for teen athletes, as long as total food is adequate [NUT-62]. For you that's about 93 g, which normal meals can cover.

**Example week (full-body, 3 non-consecutive days)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon / Wed / Fri | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Light warm-up sets first. Technique over load [POP-49]. |
| | Bench press | 3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safety arms. |
| | Row (cable or dumbbell) | 3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Balances all the pressing. |
| | Overhead press or push-ups | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |
| | Biceps curls | 2 x 10-12 | 2-3 | Curls go last. |

2-3 RIR means stopping with 2-3 reps still in the tank [STR-05].

- **Purpose of the week:** learn clean technique and build a base.
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range on every set at the target effort, add a small amount of weight next time [STR-35].
- **Missed a session:** don't double up. Do a short version of 2 exercises (bench and a row, 2 sets each) and carry on.

**For safety**

- Lifting without supervision isn't recommended for teens [POP-47]. A coach, a school or gym trainer, or a parent who can watch your form would help a lot. Please involve a parent or guardian in this plan.
- If you're feeling pressure about your size, or you're tempted by steroids or "bodybuilding" products, talk to a doctor with a parent. That's normal to bring up, and nobody will judge it [POP-50].

**Questions that would help me tune this**

1. How long have you been lifting, and what do you bench for sets of 8?
2. Any pain or injuries? Any chest pain, dizziness or fainting, or a heart or other health condition?
3. Are you eating 3 meals a day plus snacks now, and what's your usual weight trend?
