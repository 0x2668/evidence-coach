# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

Short version: eat plenty of food, skip the creatine and pre-workout, stop maxing out weekly, and get someone to supervise your lifting. Nothing in your message is a medical red flag.

**Calories (5,000 a day)**
- The evidence I work from gives no calorie target or safe gain rate for growing teenagers, so I won't make up a number. 5,000 is a big jump, and I'd treat it as a guess.
- Total food is what limits muscle gain at your age, and extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle [NUT-62]. Eat regular meals, include a snack or two, and stay with mostly normal food.
- Protein of about 1.5 g/kg a day is enough for teen athletes if total energy is adequate [NUT-62]. For you that's about 93 g a day, or about 19 g at each of 5 eating occasions.
- Watch the trend, not the scale each morning. If you're gaining steadily and your lifts are going up, you're eating enough. If you skip meals, feel anxious about weight or body shape, or feel unwell, see a doctor or sports dietitian.

**Creatine and pre-workout**
- Food first, no performance supplements for teenage athletes [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Legal supplements are often contaminated, and for most young athletes they add little beyond puberty, good training and good food [POP-51, POP-52].
- Creatine is genuinely contested for teens. One position says it may be considered only with supervision and serious training, but it conflicts with the other sources and its authors have industry ties [POP-53, NUT-64]. I don't recommend it for you now.
- My sources have no figures for caffeine in teens, and pre-workouts are a contamination risk, so I'd leave them out. Sleep, food and consistency will do more for you.

**Weekly maxing out**
- For teens, lifting is about learning technique, not how much you lift. It's safe and effective when supervised, and most youth lifting injuries are accidents with dropped weights or bad technique [POP-47, POP-49, POP-77].
- The recommended dose is 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to 80% of your max. Heavy phases above 85% come only after more training age and sound technique [POP-81, POP-82, POP-83].
- Lifting alone without a coach and testing maxes each week goes against that. Weekly maxes also add fatigue without building muscle. Ask a coach, PE teacher or experienced adult at the gym to supervise, and use safeties or a spotter on bench.

**A plan to start with**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon, Wed, Fri (or any 3 non-consecutive days) | Full body: squat or leg press, bench press, row or pulldown, overhead press, Romanian deadlift or hip hinge, curls, a trunk exercise | 2-4 x 6-12 each | RPE 7-8 (2-3 RIR) [STR-05, STR-29] | Rest about 1 min between sets, 2-3 min on heavier lifts [POP-84, POP-85]. Warm up first. |

- **Purpose:** build technique and size across all major muscle groups, not just chest and arms [STR-01].
- **Progression:** use double progression. When you hit the top of the rep range at 2-3 RIR on every set, add a small amount of weight next time [STR-35].
- **Missed session:** do a short version, 1 or 2 sets of your main lifts [BEH-28].
- **Frequency:** youth guidance says 2-3 days a week on non-consecutive days [POP-84]. Four days isn't clearly harmful, but it's more than the guidance. For the 4th day, play a sport or do easy cardio.

**Questions for you**
1. How long have you been lifting, and how is your technique on bench? Has anyone checked it?
2. Do you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, another chronic condition or medication, a bone or joint problem that exercise makes worse, or been told to exercise only under supervision? If yes to any, see a doctor before increasing training.
3. Do you have any pain right now? If so, where, and how strong is it out of 10?
4. Is a parent, school or gym adult able to supervise you?

If your bulking goal is partly driven by worry about how you look, or if you're ever tempted by steroids or "bodybuilding" products, talk to a doctor, ideally with a parent involved [POP-50].
