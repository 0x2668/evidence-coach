# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

Short version: your goal is fine, but the plan has three problems. Weekly maxing is the riskiest part, and the 5,000 kcal, creatine and pre-workout don't fit at 16.

**What I'd change**

1. **Stop maxing out.** For teens, the consensus is to learn technique first and work at up to about 80% of your max for 6-12 reps. Heavy sets of 6 or fewer above 85% belong to people with more training experience and good technique, and the guidelines say that work should be supervised [POP-81, POP-82, POP-83, POP-49]. Weekly max attempts add injury risk and don't build muscle faster. Lifting without a coach is the other weak spot. Ask a PE teacher, a coach at your gym or a trainer to check your bench and squat technique, even for one session. Teen lifting is considered safe and effective when someone teaches technique [POP-47]. Lifting does not stunt growth [POP-48].

2. **Food first, and not 5,000 kcal.** The evidence I have gives no calorie target for a growing teen who wants to gain muscle, so this part is my judgement. Add a moderate amount to what you eat now, with regular meals, instead of jumping to 5,000. Muscle needs enough total energy, and extra protein won't work without it [NUT-62]. For protein, about 1.5 g/kg/day is enough for teen athletes, which is roughly 93 g/day for you. Spread it as about 0.3 g/kg (around 19 g) over 5 eating occasions [NUT-62].

3. **Skip the pre-workout and creatine for now.**
   - Pre-workouts are mostly caffeine. The caffeine evidence I have is for adults only [NUT-55], and the adolescent evidence is missing from my sources [NUT-147 gap]. Sleep matters a lot at your age, so I wouldn't risk it.
   - Creatine is contested. Most expert bodies say young athletes shouldn't use performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. A minority view says it can be considered under supervision [NUT-64, POP-53]. That decision belongs to a doctor or dietitian and your parents.
   - For most teens, the big gains come from growing, regular training and enough food.

**A safer 4-day week (first week)**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squat or leg press, bench press, row, plank | 2-3 x 8-12 each | RPE 7-8 (2-3 RIR) | Light warm-up sets first. Start at or below 60% of your max if technique is new [POP-81]. |
| 2 | Romanian deadlift, overhead press, pulldown or pull-up, curls | 2-3 x 8-12 each | RPE 7-8 | Curls are a finisher here, not the main lift. |
| 3 | Rest or easy activity | - | - | Walking, sport or cycling counts toward your 60 min/day [POP-09]. |
| 4 | Same as Day 1 | 2-3 x 8-12 each | RPE 7-8 | Try for a rep or two more than Day 1. |
| 5 | Same as Day 2 | 2-3 x 8-12 each | RPE 7-8 | - |

- **Purpose:** build technique and muscle across the whole body, not just bench and curls. Hitting every major muscle group at least twice a week works [STR-01].
- **Progression:** use double progression. Reach the top of the rep range (12) for all sets at 2-3 reps left in the tank, then add a small amount of weight and go back to 8 [STR-35, STR-13].
- **Missed a session?** Don't stack it onto the next one. Do a 20-minute minimum version: 1 squat, 1 press and 1 row for 2 sets each [BEH-28].

**Two questions for you**

- Any pain? If it's above 5/10, or doesn't settle by the next morning, tell me and see a physio or doctor [SAF-30].
- Is the push to bulk coming partly from how you feel about your body, or from friends or social media? That's fine either way. If it's weighing on you, a doctor or sports dietitian is a good person to talk to, ideally with a parent. The same goes if anyone suggests steroids or "bodybuilding" products: please talk to a doctor first [POP-50, POP-53].

If you tell me your current eating pattern and which lifts you do now, I can tighten the plan.
