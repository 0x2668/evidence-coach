# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short version:** keep the goal of gaining muscle, but drop the 5,000 kcal, the pre-workout, the creatine and the weekly maxing out. Build the base first. The base is food, technique and a balanced routine, and it will get you further.

**What to change**

- **Weekly max tests.** Stop them. For lifters your age the focus is learning technique, not the load on the bar [POP-49]. Work at up to about 80% of your max, in sets of 6-12 reps [POP-81, POP-82]. Hard maxing without a coach is the riskiest part of your plan. Lifting your age group is safe and doesn't stunt growth when it's supervised and technique is taught [POP-47, POP-48]. So try to get someone to watch you, such as a school PE teacher, a gym instructor or a club coach.
- **5,000 kcal.** I have no evidence-based calorie target for a growing teenager, so I won't invent one. Eat regular meals, about 5 eating occasions a day, with enough total food. Without enough energy, extra protein won't build muscle [NUT-62].
- **Protein.** About 1.5 g/kg a day is enough for teen athletes, which is about 93 g for you. That's roughly 19 g at each of 5 meals [NUT-62]. Real food covers it.
- **Pre-workout and creatine.** I won't recommend either. Guidance for teen athletes is food first and no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Creatine is contested. One expert group says it might be considered for serious, supervised young athletes, and others advise against it [POP-53]. If you still want to consider it, that's a decision for a doctor or dietitian and your parents. Pre-workouts also vary a lot in what they contain, and I have no evidence on their safety for under-18s.

**A safer week**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upper A: bench press, row, overhead press, curls | 2-3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 | First weeks at 60% of your max or lighter [POP-81]. Focus on controlled form. |
| 2 | Lower: squat or leg press, Romanian deadlift, calf raise, plank | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Your current routine has no leg work, so this adds it. |
| 3 | Rest, or a walk or sport | - | - | |
| 4 | Upper B: incline press, pull-up or pulldown, lateral raise, triceps | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |
| 5 | Lower or full body, light | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |

- **Purpose of the week:** learn good technique and cover the whole body, not just chest and arms.
- **Progression:** when you reach 12 reps on all sets with 2-3 reps still in reserve, add a small amount of weight [STR-35, STR-13].
- **If you miss a session:** do 2 exercises for 2 sets each and carry on.
- **Expect gradual change.** You're still growing, so muscle comes from food, sleep and consistent training over months.

**Please check a few things**

1. Do any of these apply: heart condition or high blood pressure, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, another chronic condition or regular medication, a joint or bone problem that gets worse with exercise, or been told to exercise only under supervision? If yes to any, see a doctor before training harder [SAF-04].
2. Any pain from the weekly maxing, and where? Pain above 5/10, or pain that isn't gone by the next morning, means see a physio or GP [SAF-30, SAF-24].
3. Can you get someone to supervise you or check your form?
4. Is the main reason you want to bulk wanting to feel stronger, or are worries about how you look part of it? Both are fine. If it's driven by body image, or you're ever tempted by steroids or "bodybuilding" products, talk to a doctor, with a parent involved [POP-50]. We can also talk it through here.
