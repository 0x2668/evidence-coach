# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short version:** train for technique and steady progress, not for maxes. Skip the pre-workout and creatine, and eat regular meals. At 16 your biggest gains come from growing up, consistent training and enough food, and no plan makes that "as fast as possible".

**Your three ideas**

- **5,000 kcal/day:** the evidence I have gives no calorie target for growing teenagers who want to build muscle, so I won't invent one. Muscle gain needs enough total energy from regular meals, and extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle [NUT-62]. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg/day is enough, which is about 93 g/day for you. Spread it over about 5 eating occasions at roughly 19 g each [NUT-62]. Raise your food gradually and check progress over weeks. A sports dietitian can set a calorie number for you.
- **Pre-workout:** I can't recommend it. The caffeine evidence I have is in adults only, so there is no adolescent dose I can give you [NUT-55]. Supplement products can also be contaminated, and young athletes are advised to avoid performance supplements [NUT-63]. It can also hurt your sleep, which is when you recover (my judgement).
- **Creatine:** experts disagree. One position stand says it may be considered for serious, supervised young athletes [NUT-64]. The paediatric and dietitian guidance says food first and no supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. I side with the cautious view for you. That decision belongs with a doctor or dietitian and your parents, not with me.

**Weekly maxing**

Test maxes rarely, if at all. For teenagers, lifting should centre on learning technique, not on how much you lift [POP-49]. Guidance for youth lifting is to start at 1-2 sets at no more than 60% of your max. Once your technique is solid, you can build to about 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to 80% [POP-81, POP-82, POP-83]. Maxing out every week also adds risk with no coach watching. Resistance training is considered safe for teenagers when it is supervised and technique is taught [POP-47], and there is no evidence it stunts growth [POP-48]. So please get a qualified person to watch you. A school PE teacher, a club coach or a gym trainer would all work.

**A better week (4 days)**

Bench and curls alone leave out your legs and back. This plan covers every major muscle group.

| Day | Session / exercise | Sets x reps | RIR (RPE) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squat or leg press, bench press, row, plank | 2-3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 (RPE 7-8) | Light warm-up sets first. Stay well under your max. |
| 2 | Romanian deadlift (light), overhead press, pull-down or pull-up variation, curls | 2-3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 | Keep the back neutral. |
| 3 | Rest or easy activity | - | - | Walk, play sport, or ride a bike. |
| 4 | Repeat Day 1 with small changes | 2-3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 | Same technique focus. |
| 5 | Repeat Day 2 with small changes | 2-3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 | |

- **Purpose of the week:** build technique and a base across all muscle groups, with plenty of recovery.
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range at 2-3 reps in reserve, add a small amount of weight (2-10%) [STR-13, STR-35]. People misjudge reps in reserve by about 1 rep, so stay conservative [STR-32].
- **If you miss a session:** don't double up. Do a short version with one exercise each for legs, push and pull, then pick up from your last completed level.
- **Extra activity:** aim for about 60 minutes of activity per day on average, including some bone- and muscle-strengthening on at least 3 days [POP-09].

**Check these**

- Have you got any chest pain, dizziness, fainting or a heart or joint problem? If yes, see a doctor before increasing the load.
- Is any pain above 5/10 during or after lifting, or not gone by the next morning? If so, reduce the load. If it lasts a few weeks, see a physiotherapist [SAF-24, SAF-30].
- If worries about your body or size are driving the "as fast as possible" part, talk to a doctor with a parent involved. Steroids and similar products are never a safe shortcut, and I can't advise on them [POP-50].

Two questions would help me tune this. How long have you been lifting, and how many meals do you eat on a normal day?
