# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Response

**Short answer:** don't eat 5,000 kcal, skip the creatine and pre-workout, and stop maxing out every week. Your plan is much safer, and probably faster, if you fix those three things.

**Why**

- **5,000 kcal:** The evidence I have gives no safe calorie target or weight-gain rate for a growing 16-year-old, so I won't give you a number. For muscle gain, eating enough every day matters more than eating a huge amount. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg/day is enough if your total food is adequate. At 62 kg that is roughly 93 g, or about 19 g at each of 5 eating occasions [NUT-62]. Extra protein without enough total energy won't build muscle. Eat regular meals, add snacks, and let your weight rise gradually.
- **Creatine:** Experts disagree here. One group says it may be acceptable for supervised, serious young athletes. Most paediatric and sports-dietitian sources advise food first and no performance supplements for teenagers [NUT-61, NUT-63, NUT-64, POP-53]. My default is not to use it. If you still want to, decide it with a doctor or dietitian and your parents.
- **Pre-workout:** These products are mostly caffeine and other stimulants. The knowledge base has no evidence on caffeine doses for teenagers, and supplements can be contaminated. Don't use it. Sleep and food do the same job.
- **Maxing every week:** Maximal attempts every week with no spotter or coach carry a high injury risk. For teenagers the consensus is to learn technique first, with sessions supervised by someone qualified [POP-47, POP-49]. Heavy work on bench or curls adds little to size that sets 2-3 reps from failure don't [STR-05]. Growing bones and tendons don't need weekly 1RM tests. There's no evidence that lifting stunts growth, but supervised, technique-focused lifting is the standard [POP-48].

**Week 1 plan (4 days, upper/lower split)**

Rest 2-3 minutes between sets on the big lifts. Use a weight you can control, and stop each set with 2-3 reps still in the tank (RPE 7-8) [STR-05, STR-29]. Start at 2 sets per exercise and no more than about 60% of your max. Once your technique is solid, build to 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to about 80% [POP-81].

**Purpose of the week:** learn good technique across all major muscle groups, not just bench and curls [STR-01].

**Progression:** use double progression. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set at 2-3 reps in reserve, add a small amount of weight [STR-35]. Test a max rarely, if ever, and only with a supervisor.

**If you miss a session:** do a short version of 3 exercises, 2 sets each. Don't double up the next week.

**Supervision:** this plan works best if a qualified coach or a knowledgeable adult can check your form. Ask your school, a gym instructor or a local club. Lifting alone with no coach is the biggest risk in your setup.

**Questions I need answered (replies stay in this chat unless you agree to storing them):**

1. **Health.** Do you have a heart condition or high blood pressure, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, any other chronic condition or medication, or a joint or bone problem that gets worse with exercise? If any answer is yes, see a doctor before you keep training hard [SAF-01, SAF-04].
2. **Pain.** Do you have any pain now? If so, where, and how strong is it out of 10? Chest pain, fainting, or a joint that gives way or can't bear weight means stop and get medical help the same day [SAF-22].
3. **Food.** Do you skip meals or feel worried about your weight or body shape? If so, talk to a doctor or sports dietitian, because that changes the advice [NUT-60, NUT-66].
4. **Training history.** How long have you been lifting, and what weights do you use now?
5. **Equipment.** Do you have a gym with a rack or spotter, or do you train at home?

I can't help with steroids, SARMs or "bulking" products. They are dangerous at your age. If you're tempted, talk to a doctor with a parent involved [POP-50].
