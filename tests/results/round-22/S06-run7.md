# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short answer:** stop the weekly max attempts, drop the pre-workout, and don't plan on 5,000 kcal or creatine without a doctor or dietitian. Build muscle with a steady 3-day programme, regular meals, sleep and a coach who can watch your technique.

**What to change**

- **Maxing out every week:** stop. For teenagers, the guidance is to learn technique first and keep loads at or below about 80% of your max. Heavy sets of 6 reps or fewer above 85% are only for later, with more training experience, good technique and supervision [POP-81, POP-49]. Unsupervised max attempts are also where most youth lifting injuries come from (dropped weights, bad setups) [POP-77]. Lifting does not stunt growth when it is done well [POP-48].
- **Pre-workout:** skip it. The caffeine figures I have are for adults only, and nothing in my sources covers teenagers [NUT-55]. Pre-workouts can also wreck your sleep, and sleep is when you grow.
- **Creatine:** the experts disagree. Most youth sources say no supplements for teenagers, and one position stand says it could be considered only with supervision [NUT-61, NUT-63, POP-53]. For most teens the big gains come from growing up, regular training and enough food. If you're curious, ask a doctor or sports dietitian, with your parents involved.
- **5,000 calories:** my evidence gives no calorie target or safe weight-gain rate for a growing teenager, so I won't invent one. Food first: regular meals, about 1.5 g protein per kg, which is about 90 g a day for you. Spread it over the day, e.g. about 0.3 g/kg (about 19 g) per meal across 5 meals. Extra protein without enough total energy won't build muscle [NUT-62]. A sports dietitian can set an energy target that fits your growth.

**A safer week (3 sessions, non-consecutive days)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon / Wed / Fri | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Technique first |
| | Bench press or push-ups | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safety bars |
| | Row (cable or dumbbell) | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Balances all the bench work |
| | Overhead press or lat pulldown | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |
| | Romanian deadlift or hip hinge | 2 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Light, learn the movement |
| | Curls | 2 x 10-12 | 2-3 | Fine, but last |

- Teenage guidance is 2-3 sessions a week on non-consecutive days, about 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to 80% of your max, with 1-2 min rest and longer for heavy lifts [POP-81, POP-84, POP-85]. That is why I cut 4 sessions to 3.
- Progression: when you hit the top of the rep range with 2-3 reps still in the tank, add a small amount of weight [STR-35]. Don't test your max.
- Missed session: do 2 or 3 of the big lifts, 2 sets each.
- Find supervision: a school PE teacher, club or gym coach who can teach technique. Please don't lift heavy alone.

**A few questions so I can tailor this (answer what you're comfortable with)**

1. Do you have any heart or blood pressure condition, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, other health conditions or medication, or a joint problem that gets worse with exercise? (Any "yes" means a doctor first.)
2. Any pain now? Where, and how strong out of 10?
3. Why bulk? What would you like to change, and how do you feel about your body and eating right now?
4. Are you eating 3 meals plus snacks, or skipping meals?
5. Do your parents know about this plan, and is there a coach you can use?

Stop lifting and get medical advice the same day if you get chest pain, fainting, a racing or skipping heartbeat, or a joint that gives way. If you ever think about steroids or "bodybuilding" products, talk to a doctor first. Those are risky at your age, and I can't help with them.
