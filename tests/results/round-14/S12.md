## Prompt

Here's my run from yesterday: tests/fixtures/easy_run.gpx. It was supposed to be an easy run. My lab-measured max heart rate is 190. Was it really easy?

## Response

**Probably yes, but heart rate alone can't confirm it.**

From the file (calculated by the script, not Strava):
- 10 km in 60 min, even 6:00/km pace, flat, with the first km at 6:10.
- Average HR was 150 bpm, which is 79% of your 190 max. Peak HR was 162 bpm, or 85%.

**How I read it**
- In the research's 3-zone model, easy (zone 1) is below the first lactate threshold. Percentages of max HR are only rough guides for that [END-12, END-02].
- Elite-skier data put easy-to-steady running at roughly 54-83% of max HR and the moderate zone at 84-88% [END-03]. Your average sits inside the easy band. Your peak just touches the moderate band, which is normal for a brief rise.
- The even pace and flat route suggest you held a steady effort rather than pushing.

**What I can't tell**
- The summary gives only the average and peak HR, not how HR moved over the hour. If HR crept up at the same pace after about 10 minutes, that is cardiovascular drift. It means the effort was getting relatively harder, more so in heat [END-49].
- Wrist HR has measurement error, so treat the numbers as approximate.

**The better check is the talk test** [END-14]. If you could speak in full sentences the whole way (RPE about 10-11 on the 6-20 scale), it was easy. If speaking got uncomfortable, it was in the moderate zone, whatever the HR said.

If you share your thresholds (a lab LT1/VT1, if you have one) or your zone bounds, I can rerun the file for time in each zone. If you'd rather keep easy days easy by feel, I can also build you a plan using the talk test.
