# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Bouldering: stop now. Hangboarding: you can keep a lighter version if your OB agrees. Neither is a reason to quit being active.**

**Bouldering**
- At 31 weeks (third trimester), I'd take bouldering out completely, including low traverses and down-climbing. This is my coaching line, not a fixed rule. The guidance says to avoid fall-prone activities in pregnancy and to ask your obstetric provider about fall risk for climbing [POP-55]. It names no week, and the week-28 cut-off is my judgement.
- Knowing how to fall doesn't remove the risk. That part is also my judgement. Your OB's "keep doing what you're doing" was general advice. Ask her this specific question: "Is falling off a boulder problem, or taking a hard impact, acceptable at my stage?"
- If she explicitly agrees on falls and harness fit, roped climbing is a separate conversation. Until then, don't switch to it.

**Hangboarding**
- The knowledge base has no pregnancy-specific evidence on finger training. Everything below is my judgement, not a rule from the evidence.
- Healthy pregnancy and regular exercise are well supported. Without complications, activity in pregnancy is considered safe and desirable, and the target is at least 150 min/week of moderate activity [POP-35, POP-40]. This is consensus-level evidence. Because you train well above that, a specialist provider should oversee you [POP-10].

| Day | Session | Sets x reps or time | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 non-consecutive days | Hangboard repeaters, lighter | Fewer sets than your usual, no added weight | RPE 6-7 (3+ RIR), stop well before failure | Warm up fingers. Breathe throughout and don't hold your breath. Keep 48-72 h between sessions [CLB-26]. |
| 2 days | Easy wall climbing you can step or climb down from | 30-45 min, Z1 (easy, can talk in full sentences) | RPE 3-4 | Stay low and avoid dynamic moves or jumping off. |
| Daily | Pelvic floor training | Short daily practice | Easy | Get technique instruction from a pelvic floor physiotherapist [POP-36]. |
| 1-2 days | Walking or other easy aerobic work | Build toward the 150 min/week total | Z1-Z2 (can talk) | Stay cool and hydrated. |

The week's purpose is to keep your fingers and general fitness ticking over without fall risk. If you miss a session, skip it. Don't make it up later.

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you have any of these** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before exercise
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions

**Please tell me, or check with your OB:**
- Any placenta previa, preeclampsia, high blood pressure, growth restriction or twins? These can limit you to usual daily activities [POP-37, POP-38].
- Any pain in your fingers, shoulders or elbows? Rate it out of 10 and say whether it's back to baseline the next morning.

The IDs in brackets (like [POP-55]) point to the sources in the skill's reference files.
