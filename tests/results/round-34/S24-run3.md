# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Bouldering: stop, from now until after the birth.** At 31 weeks, I'd take bouldering out completely. That includes low traversing and down-climbing. Your experience with falling doesn't remove the risk. Pregnancy guidance says to avoid fall-prone activities and to ask your obstetric provider specifically about climbing [POP-55]. This is consensus guidance. It doesn't name a week, so the week-28 cut-off is my coaching decision. "Keep doing what you're doing" was a general answer, not an answer about falls. Roped climbing would need her explicit agreement on falls and harness fit.

**Hangboard: the evidence doesn't give a figure for pregnancy.** There's no fall risk, so it's the lesser concern. I found no claim on finger loading or hangboarding in pregnancy. What follows is my judgement, so check it with your OB:
- Ask her directly about hangboarding and about everything else you do. Elite-level training is a reason for specialist supervision [POP-10].
- Keep the hangs well submaximal. Stop with 2-3 reps in reserve instead of near failure [STR-05].
- Cut the volume to about 2 sets per session. The plan below is a conservative starting point, not a studied dose.
- Keep 48-72 h between sessions [CLB-26].
- Stay off a hangboard you'd have to jump down from if you tire. Feet stay on the floor.

**Suggested week (all judgement unless a tag is shown):**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps or time / zone | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hangboard repeaters, light | 2 sets, edge you can hold easily | RPE 6-7 (3-4 RIR) | Half-crimp [CLB-69]; feet on the floor |
| 1 | Pull, push and leg strength, full body | 2-3 sets x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 (2-3 RIR) [STR-05] | Seated or inclined positions; ask your OB about lying on your back, because the evidence doesn't give a figure |
| 3 | Walk, easy cycling or swimming | 30-40 min, Z1 (easy, can talk in full sentences) | RPE 3-4 | 3-zone model [END-02]; build toward 150 min/week of moderate activity in total [POP-35] |
| 4 | Hangboard repeaters, light | As day 1 | As day 1 | |
| 5 | Full-body strength | As day 1 | As day 1 | |
| Daily | Pelvic floor training | Per your instructor | - | With technique instruction [POP-36] |

This week is for keeping your fingers and strength ticking over, not for gaining. Next week, repeat it if you feel normal. Don't add load. If you miss a session, drop it. Don't make it up. The minimum is one walk and one strength session.

**Stop and call your OB or midwife if you notice any of these** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding
- dizziness or faintness
- breathlessness before you start
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions
- fluid leaking from the vagina

**Questions for your OB:** "Is hangboarding fine? Are falls off a boulder a risk at 31 weeks? Is roped climbing with a proper harness an option?"

Claim IDs like [POP-55] point to the sources in the skill's reference files. Most of the evidence here is consensus guidelines. Nothing in it is specific to climbers.
