# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

Short answer: stop bouldering now. Hangboarding can continue only in a reduced form, and only if your OB agrees to that specific plan.

**Bouldering: stop from week 28, and you are at 31.**
- Guidance for pregnancy says to avoid fall-prone activities and to ask your obstetric provider about fall risk if you climb [POP-55, consensus, 2024]. The source gives no week number. Week 28 as the cut-off is my coaching decision.
- Your experience with falling doesn't remove the risk. That part is my judgement. The risk is the fall or impact itself, and a landing at 31 weeks is different from one before pregnancy.
- This includes low traversing and down-climbing. "Keep doing what you're doing" was a general answer. It almost certainly wasn't an answer to "do I take bouldering falls at 31 weeks?"
- Roped climbing is not a swap-in. It needs your OB's explicit agreement on falls and harness fit.

**Hangboard repeaters: possible, but the evidence doesn't give a figure.**
Nothing in the knowledge base covers hangboarding in pregnancy, so this is my judgement.
- First ask your OB specifically about hanging and finger training. Don't rely on the general "keep going".
- If she agrees, keep it submaximal: use the same hang times as now, but use a load well below your usual (for example, add assistance from a pulley or band, or hang on a bigger edge). Hold 3-4 repeater sets, 2 days a week, 48-72 h apart [CLB-26, practitioner dosing]. Stop each set with plenty left, and skip anything near max.
- Always keep your feet on the floor or a box beneath you. Don't do dynamic or feet-off moves, or drops from the bar.
- Pain stays at 5/10 or lower and is back to baseline the next morning [SAF-30]. Stop if you feel unsteady.

**What to do with the bouldering days (judgement):**
- Do easy strength work at 2-3 reps in reserve instead [STR-05]. Pulls, rows, and leg and core work you can tolerate all fit.
- Add daily pelvic floor training with technique instruction [POP-36].
- Add easy aerobic work toward at least 150 min a week of moderate activity [POP-35]. The evidence is strong here (consensus guideline), as long as you have no contraindications [POP-40].

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you have:** vaginal bleeding, dizziness or faintness, shortness of breath before exercising, chest pain, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or swelling, regular painful contractions, or fluid leaking [POP-54]. Call 112 (or your local number) for chest pain or severe breathing difficulty.

**Questions for your OB:**
1. Do you have any contraindications (for example placenta previa, preeclampsia, or twins)? These can limit exercise to everyday activity [POP-37].
2. Is hangboarding with my feet on the floor fine, and at what effort?
3. Is any roped climbing acceptable, and with what harness?
