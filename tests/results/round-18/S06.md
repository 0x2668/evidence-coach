# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

I'd change the plan in four places. None of what you wrote is a red flag, but a few things are riskier than they need to be.

**1. Stop maxing out every week.**
- At 16, the consensus guidance is to work at up to about 80% of your 1-rep max (1RM), in 6-12 reps. Heavier phases of 6 reps or fewer above 85% come later, with more training experience and good technique [POP-81, POP-82, POP-83].
- Max attempts without a spotter or coach are where most youth lifting injuries happen (dropped weights, bad setup) [POP-77]. They also don't build muscle faster than sets in the 6-12 range. For size, any load from about 30% to 100% of your max works if the sets end close to failure [STR-06].
- Lifting is fine for growth. There's no evidence it stunts growth [POP-48]. The advice is to have someone qualified supervise and teach technique [POP-47]. Ask a PE teacher, a gym instructor or an experienced lifter you trust to watch your bench.

**2. Don't take creatine or pre-workout.**
- For teens, the main sports-nutrition and paediatric sources say food first and no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63, POP-51]. One position (ISSN) says creatine may be considered for supervised, serious young athletes. Experts disagree on this, but I side with the majority. Creatine is also not what limits your progress right now.
- Supplements can be contaminated. The evidence has no safe dose for pre-workout in teens, and it often contains lots of caffeine, which can wreck sleep.

**3. Don't aim for 5,000 kcal.**
- The evidence gives no calorie figure for a growing teen who wants to bulk, so I won't invent one. Regular meals with enough total energy are the precondition for gaining muscle [NUT-62].
- Protein: about 1.5 g/kg/day is enough [NUT-62]. For you that is about 93 g, split over roughly 5 eating occasions of about 0.3 g/kg (about 19 g each). Extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle.
- My own judgement, not from the evidence: eat a bit more than you do now, and don't force 5,000 kcal. If your weight doesn't rise slowly over a few weeks, add a snack or two. A sports dietitian can set exact numbers.

**4. Balance the training.** Bench and curls alone won't do it. Cover all the major muscle groups [STR-01], with these youth guidelines:

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon / Wed / Fri (non-consecutive) | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 / 2-3 RIR | Technique first; about 1 min rest, 2-3 min on heavy lifts [POP-84, POP-85] |
| | Bench press or push-ups | 2-3 x 6-12 | RPE 7-8 / 2-3 RIR | Start at 60% 1RM or less, up to 80% once the form is solid [POP-81] |
| | Row or pull-up variation | 2-3 x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 / 2-3 RIR | Equal pulling and pushing |
| | Romanian deadlift or hip hinge | 2-3 x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 / 2-3 RIR | Light, with a clean back |
| | Curls | 2 x 10-12 | RPE 8 / 2 RIR | Fine, but they come last |

- **Why:** You build muscle from consistent training in the middle range, with enough food and sleep. You don't need to test your max.
- **Progression:** When you hit the top of the rep range at 2 reps in reserve, add 2-10% load next time (double progression) [STR-35, STR-13]. Youth guidelines suggest 2-3 sessions a week, so I cut your 4 days to 3. The extra day mostly adds fatigue.
- **Missed session:** Do a short version with 1 set each of squat, bench and row.

**Questions for you** (a few answers change the plan):
1. How long have you been lifting, and can you describe how your bench setup and spotting work now?
2. Any "yes" to these? A heart condition or high blood pressure, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, a bone or joint problem that exercise makes worse, or a doctor who told you to exercise only under supervision. If yes, check with a doctor before pushing harder.
3. Any pain right now? Where, how strong out of 10, and is it gone by the next morning?
4. What does a normal day of eating look like, and do you ever skip meals?
5. How much sleep do you get?

If you're thinking about steroids, prohormones or anything from the "bodybuilding" shelf, or if weight or how you look is stressing you out, talk to a doctor, ideally with a parent involved [POP-50, POP-53]. I can't advise on those, but I'm glad to keep helping with the training side.
