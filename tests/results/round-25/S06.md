# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Drop to 3 full-body sessions a week, stop the weekly maxes, skip the 5,000 calories and the supplements, and find a qualified coach.** You can absolutely build muscle at 16, and supervised lifting doesn't stunt growth [POP-48]. It just won't be as fast as you'd like. The IDs in brackets point to the sources.

**What to change**
- **Maxing:** stop testing every week. At your age, technique comes before load [POP-49], and most teen lifting injuries are preventable accidents, like dropped weights [POP-77].
- **Coach:** the safety evidence comes from supervised training, so find a qualified coach and ask a parent or guardian to help [POP-47]. Until then, stay at the light loads below and have someone spot your bench.
- **Calories:** the evidence gives no calorie or weight-gain figure for growing teens. My judgement is that 5,000 a day won't build muscle faster than your training allows, and much of the extra would likely become fat. You do need enough food, though [NUT-62], so a doctor or sports dietitian can set a target with you and a parent.
- **Food for now:**
  - Eat regularly, around 5 times a day, and don't skip meals.
  - Aim for about 93 g of protein a day, or roughly 19 g each time you eat [NUT-62].
  - Include carbs and protein right after training, and foods with calcium, vitamin D and iron [NUT-63].
- **Creatine:** I don't recommend it. Experts disagree: most sources advise against supplements for teens, while one group with industry ties allows creatine in serious, supervised athletes [NUT-61, NUT-63, NUT-64]. That decision belongs with a doctor or dietitian and your parents [POP-53].
- **Pre-workout:** skip it too. Teens are advised against performance supplements, and contamination is a common concern with legal ones [NUT-63, POP-51]. The evidence I have gives no safe caffeine dose for your age.
- **Sleep:** aim for 8-10 hours a night [MON-32].

**Your week:** do this same session on Monday, Wednesday and Friday [POP-84]. The evidence sets the 2 sets and 60% limit; 10 reps and the exercise choice are my judgement.

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Squat or leg press | 2 x 10 | 4+ (my judgement) | Warm up with a lighter set; up to 60% 1RM |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Romanian deadlift | 2 x 10 | 4+ | Light; ask your coach to check your back position |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Bench press | 2 x 10 | 4+ | Up to 60% of your recent max; use a spotter |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Row | 2 x 10 | 4+ | Balances out the bench |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Curl | 1-2 x 10 | 4+ | Do these last |

Rest about 1 minute between sets, or 2-3 minutes for heavier or more technical lifts [POP-85]. For new lifts, pick a weight that feels easy.

- **Purpose:** build clean technique and cover your whole body, not just chest and arms.
- **Next week:** work up towards 12 clean reps on every set, keeping the load within the 60% limit.
- **Once a coach approves your technique:** move to 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to 80% 1RM, adding weight only after you hit 12 reps on every set with 2-3 reps still left [POP-81, POP-82, STR-35].
- **If you miss a session:** don't double up. For a busy day, do 2 sets each of squat, row and bench.

**Before you increase anything, a few questions:**
1. Has a doctor ever told you that you have a heart condition or high blood pressure, or that you should only exercise under supervision? Do you get chest pain, dizziness or blackouts? Do you have another chronic condition, take prescribed medication for one, or have a bone, joint or soft-tissue problem that activity makes worse? If any answer is yes, see a doctor before training harder [SAF-04].
2. Do you have any pain from maxing, especially in your shoulders, elbows or wrists? Where is it, how strong out of 10, and is it gone by the next morning?
3. What's making you want to bulk fast: a sport, how you look, or something else? There's no wrong answer. If worry about your body is a big part of it, a doctor is a good person to talk to, along with a parent [POP-50].
4. Are you skipping meals, or stressed about your weight or food? If so, a doctor or sports dietitian can help [NUT-66].
5. Do you have access to a coach, a school gym teacher or a gym with qualified staff?
