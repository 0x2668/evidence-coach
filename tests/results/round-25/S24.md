# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Bouldering: I'd stop anything you could fall from, at least until your OB has answered the specific question below. Hangboarding can continue in a lighter, feet-on-the-floor form.** No evidence here looks directly at pregnant climbers, so these specific changes are my judgement, based on general pregnancy guidance.

**Why change anything**
- **Falls:** ACOG, the US obstetric college, says to avoid fall-prone activities and anything with an abdominal-impact risk. It also says climbers should ask their obstetric provider about fall risk before continuing [POP-55]. The claim IDs in brackets point to the sources.
- **Your experience:** 12 years of skill makes a controlled fall more likely, and it clearly counts for something. But it can't guarantee a clean landing, or protect your abdomen if you land badly or hit a hold or another climber.
- **Your OB's advice:** "Keep doing what you're doing" is a good sign, and staying active is safe and encouraged if you have no complications [POP-40]. But she may not realise that falling onto mats is a routine part of bouldering.

**What to ask her:** "I boulder without a rope and regularly fall or jump about [your height] onto mats, and I also hang from my fingers. Given that, are you happy for me to continue? Is there anything about my pregnancy that changes your answer?" If she knows the details and still says yes, it's a decision for the two of you, but I'd still keep falls off the table.

**Suggested week until you've spoken to her**

| Day | Session | Volume | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low traversing and movement drills | Your usual session or shorter | Comfortable | Feet low enough to step down at any time; no jumps, big dynos or topping out |
| 2 | Brisk walk, cycling on a stationary bike or swimming | Your choice | Moderate; you can still talk | Counts towards the weekly total below |
| 3 | Hangboard, feet on the floor | Up to 3 sets of 4 x 10 s hangs, 5 s rest between hangs, 1 min between sets | Light strain; never near failure | Half-crimp, step off rather than drop |
| 4 | Low traversing, or the same aerobic option as day 2 | Your choice | Comfortable to moderate | Skip it if you're tired |
| Daily | Pelvic floor exercises | The evidence does not give a figure | Easy | Ideally get the technique taught by a pelvic health physio [POP-36] |

- **Purpose of the week:** keep your fitness, finger conditioning and movement skills with no fall risk.
- **Progression:** none for now. Don't add load or sets, and cut back further whenever it feels harder.
- **If you miss a session:** don't make it up. A short walk and your pelvic floor exercises are enough.

**How the numbers hold up**
- **Hangboard:** the protocol is a standard repeater format, cut to its lowest set count and with far less load [CLB-06, CLB-08]. Those trials weren't in pregnant climbers.
- **Load in pregnancy:** the evidence gives no safe finger-loading or effort limit, so I'm being deliberately conservative.
- **Finger strength:** one session a week maintains gains in non-pregnant climbers, so you shouldn't lose much [CLB-07, CLB-13, CLB-26, CLB-35, CLB-60].
- **Weekly total:** aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity [POP-35]. This is well established.
- **Top-roping:** the evidence doesn't cover harness use or swings late in pregnancy, so ask your OB before trying it as a replacement.
- **Extra support:** guidance suggests pregnant athletes be supervised by a specialist healthcare provider, so ask your OB who that could be [POP-10].

**When to stop and call your OB**
- **Stop exercising and call if you have any of these [POP-54]:**
  - vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
  - regular painful contractions
  - dizziness or faintness
  - shortness of breath before exercise
  - chest pain
  - headache
  - muscle weakness
  - calf pain or swelling
- **After a fall or blow to your belly:** call the same day, even if you feel fine. This is my judgement rather than a figure from the evidence.

**Two things I need to know**
1. Has your OB flagged any complications, such as placenta previa, high blood pressure, preeclampsia, a short cervix, growth restriction, twins or a previous preterm birth? Some of these rule out strenuous exercise, including this plan, and others need a specific conversation with her first [POP-37, POP-38, POP-39].
2. Do your fingers, shoulders or anything else hurt when you hang or climb?
