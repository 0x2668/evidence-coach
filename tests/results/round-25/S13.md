# S13: Starting strength at 68 with osteoporosis

## Prompt

Ich bin 68, Frau, und habe seit zwei Jahren die Diagnose Osteoporose. Mein Hausarzt sagt, ich soll mehr Sport machen, hat aber nichts Genaues gesagt. Bisher gehe ich nur spazieren. Vor einem Jahr bin ich einmal gestürzt. Was soll ich konkret trainieren?

## Response

Walking alone is not enough: it has no proven effect on falls [POP-34]. You need strength, balance and later, depending on your history, some impact training. Exercise is unlikely to cause fractures [POP-66]. The figures come from consensus statements on osteoporosis and falls prevention. The IDs in brackets point to the sources.

Please read the questions below the table first. They decide whether you can start with this plan. It deliberately leaves out jumps and stamping for now.

## Your first week

Do the exercises on three days, with one day off in between.

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps or time / zone | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon, Fri | Sit-to-stand from a chair [POP-27] | 1 x 8-12 | At least 5 RIR* | Use a stable chair and only use your hands if you need to. |
| Mon, Fri | Hip hinge [POP-70] | 1 x 8-12 | At least 5 RIR* | Hold on to a chair back and push your bottom backwards, keeping your back long. Don't add weight yet. |
| Mon, Fri | Push-ups against the wall* | 1 x 8-12 | At least 5 RIR* | Keep your body straight and stand about one step away from the wall. |
| Mon, Fri | Seated rowing with a light resistance band* | 1 x 8-12 | At least 5 RIR* | Sit upright. Alternative: bend forward from the hips and row with light water bottles. |
| Mon, Fri | Heel raises* | 1 x 8-12 | At least 5 RIR* | Hold on to the kitchen counter. |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Stepping onto a low step or stair [POP-27] | 1 x 8-12 per leg | At least 5 RIR* | Hold the handrail. |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Standing with one foot directly in front of the other [POP-27] | 3 x 20 seconds per side* | Should feel slightly wobbly, but safe | Stand at the counter with your hands just above it, and hold on if needed. Later, try standing on one leg. |
| Tue, Sat | Walk, 5 minutes longer than your usual walk [POP-17] | Z1 of the 3-zone model (easy to brisk, can talk in full sentences) | RPE 3-4* | Sturdy shoes and well-lit paths. |
| Thu, Sun | Your usual walk | Z1 (easy, can talk in full sentences) | Easy | Rest day from exercises. |

*The evidence gives no figure or specific exercise here. This is my cautious judgement.

**Purpose of the week:** to learn the movements safely and establish a routine.

**Next steps:** Stay at each level for 2-4 weeks [POP-18], then change one thing at a time:
- **Sets:** Go up to 2 sets, later 3 [POP-22, POP-67].
- **Load:** Then slowly increase the weight until 8-12 repetitions feel properly hard [POP-67].
- **Walking:** Gradually build up to 150 minutes per week of brisk walking [POP-01].
- **Duration:** Keep the program going for at least 12 weeks, and preferably longer [POP-27].

**If time is short:** Do sit-to-stands and the balance exercise for 5-10 minutes*. Do not make up missed sessions.

**Please avoid:** Sit-ups, touching your toes, and lifting with a rounded back. Hinge from the hips instead [POP-70].

**Stop training and get medical advice the same day if you have:**
- Sudden severe back pain or back pain that worsens rapidly [SAF-34]
- Dizziness [SAF-15]

Also tell your doctor about any further fall [POP-28].

A few sessions with a physiotherapist are a good way to check your technique, especially for the hip hinge.

## Five questions for you

**1. Your fall and general condition:**
- Were you injured and needed treatment?
- Were you lying on the floor for an hour or more?
- Did you possibly black out?
- Have you fallen at least twice in the past 12 months?
- Have you noticed unintentional weight loss, exhaustion, weakness or slower walking?

*If you answer yes to any of the first four, or have several of the signs in the last point:* see your GP for a falls risk assessment [POP-28, POP-30, POP-31]. Until then, stick to your usual walks and only do the exercises with support, without increasing them.\* If you may have blacked out, or feel dizzy now, see question 3.

**2. Fractures:** Have you ever had a vertebral fracture, or several fractures from minor incidents?

- *If yes:* Get individual advice from a physiotherapist before increasing strength or impact training. Walk no more intensely than a brisk pace [POP-69].
- *If no:* We can add moderate impact on most days, such as stamping, low jumps and hops, building up to at least 50 per session [POP-68].

**3. Current symptoms:** Do you have any of these?
- Dizziness or fainting
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath with light exertion
- A racing, skipping or fluttering heartbeat
- Swollen ankles
- Calf pain when walking
- Unusual tiredness

*If yes:* No training. Get medical advice today [SAF-15, SAF-20]. For tight or squeezing chest pain that spreads to your arm, neck or jaw, or severe shortness of breath, stop and call 112 [SAF-17, SAF-21].

**4. Other health conditions:**
- Do you have a heart condition or high blood pressure?
- Do you have another chronic condition, or take medication for one?
- Does activity make a bone, joint or soft-tissue problem worse?
- Have you been told to exercise only under supervision?

*If yes:* Tell me which, and check with your GP before increasing your activity [SAF-01, SAF-04]. Please also ask them what training is suitable for you specifically.

**5. Your everyday life:**
- What do you want to be able to do safely again, or keep doing?
- How many days and minutes per week can you realistically manage?
- How long is your usual walk?
- What equipment do you have, such as a resistance band, dumbbells or stairs with a handrail?
- What do you enjoy?

Once I have your answers, I can tailor the plan to you.
