# S25: Long-event hyponatremia question

## Prompt

My sister just finished her first marathon in 5:40. She drank at every aid station because she was scared of dehydrating. Now she's vomiting, has a bad headache and seems confused. Her friends say to give her a sports drink and let her sleep it off. What should we do?

## Response

**Call an ambulance now: 112 (999 in the UK). If you are still at the race, also get the medical tent or race medics, but don't let that delay the call.** Do not let her sleep it off.

Confusion and vomiting after a long endurance event are an emergency [SAF-49]. She may have exercise-associated hyponatremia, which means her blood sodium has dropped too low, usually from drinking more than she sweated [NUT-30]. Her slow finish and drinking at every stop fit that pattern, but only a blood test can confirm it [SAF-48]. It can also look like heat illness, so she needs medical staff either way, and it can get worse quickly [SAF-50].

**Tell the operator:**

"She just finished a marathon in 5:40, drank at every aid station, and now has vomiting, a bad headache and confusion. We're worried about hyponatremia." Then follow what they tell you. Medics need to know about the drinking, so repeat it when they arrive.

**Until help arrives:**
- **No more fluids:** don't give her water or a sports drink [SAF-48]. Sports drinks are mostly water and usually don't contain enough sodium to fix this [NUT-20]. If the operator or medics tell you otherwise, follow them.
- **Sleep:** don't let her sleep it off. Stay with her and keep checking whether she is alert and making sense. Being hard to wake is a sign she is getting worse, so tell the operator straight away.
- **Vomiting or drowsiness (my judgement, not from the coaching evidence):** if she's drowsy or vomiting, lay her on her side so she doesn't choke.
- **If she feels hot or is behaving strangely (heat illness can look the same):** tell the operator and start cooling her, for example by moving her to the shade, removing extra layers and using cold water or ice on her skin [SAF-39, SAF-41].
- **If things get worse:** if she has a seizure or collapses, call back or tell the operator immediately. If she stops breathing, start CPR, and the operator will guide you [SAF-19].

Her friends mean well and this is a common instinct, but here the extra fluid could make things worse. The guidance here is firm that symptoms like these need emergency care. The studies behind it come mostly from field and wilderness medicine.
