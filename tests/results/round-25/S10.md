# S10: Running and lifting at 20 weeks of pregnancy

## Prompt

I'm 20 weeks pregnant, healthy pregnancy so far, and before I was running about 30 km a week and lifting twice a week. My midwife said exercise is fine. Can I keep running and lifting, and what should I watch out for?

## Response

**Yes, you can most likely keep doing both.** With no complications, staying active in pregnancy is considered safe and worthwhile, and this is well established [POP-40]. The guideline minimum is 150 minutes of moderate activity a week [POP-35]. Your 30 km is probably around 3 hours, so you're above that but not extreme.

Tell your midwife exactly what you do (30 km of running, two lifting sessions), so that "fine" clearly covers your actual training [POP-37]. Anyone training well above the guideline should have a specialist healthcare provider overseeing it [POP-10], so check with them before adding much more.

The codes in brackets point to the sources.

**Stop and contact your midwife or obstetric provider if you have any of these [POP-54]:**
- vaginal bleeding, or fluid leaking from the vagina
- regular painful contractions
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before you start exercising
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- chest pain

Call 112 (999 in the UK) if chest pain is tight or squeezing or spreads to the arm, neck or jaw, if you have severe trouble breathing, or if someone collapses [SAF-17, SAF-21].

**Conditions that change the advice**

Your pregnancy can change, so tell your midwife about any new diagnosis:
- **Usual daily activities only:** for example preeclampsia, ruptured membranes, premature labour, persistent unexplained bleeding, or placenta previa after 28 weeks [POP-37, POP-38].
- **Discuss with your care provider first:** for example high blood pressure in pregnancy, symptomatic anaemia, a previous preterm birth, recurrent pregnancy loss, or twins after week 28 [POP-37, POP-39].

**Activities to avoid or check [POP-55]**
- **Avoid:** anything with a risk of falls or a blow to the abdomen, hot yoga or Pilates, scuba diving, and activity above 6,000 ft (about 1,800 m) unless you live at altitude.
- **Ask your provider first:** trail running, because of the fall risk.

**Add daily pelvic floor training**

Ask for technique instruction, for example from your midwife or a pelvic floor physiotherapist [POP-36].

**Where the evidence gives no figures**

My sources give no pregnancy-specific numbers for running distance, lifting loads or heart-rate limits. They also don't cover heat beyond hot yoga, or exercising while lying on your back. Ask your midwife about lying on your back. The rest is my own conservative judgement:
- **Running:** keep it easy enough to talk in full sentences, and let pace and distance drop as needed. Aim to maintain fitness rather than build it.
- **Lifting:** keep your usual exercises, stop 3 or more reps short of failure, skip max attempts, and don't hold your breath. Swap any exercise that stops feeling comfortable.
- **Heat:** avoid hard sessions in hot conditions, and drink to thirst.

**Two questions so I can make this more specific:**
1. Are you still running 30 km and lifting twice a week now, or has that dropped since you got pregnant?
2. Do you run on roads or trails, and what are your usual lifts?
