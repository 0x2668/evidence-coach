## Response

**Short answer: don't do the 5,000 calories, the pre-workout or the weekly maxing out. Skip creatine for now too. Your best route to muscle is a different one.**

You're 16 and lifting with no coach, so these are the changes that matter most.

**1. Stop maxing out every week.**
- Heavy single-rep tests are the riskiest thing you can do alone at your age. Most youth lifting injuries come from accidents, like dropped weights and poor technique, that supervision could prevent [POP-77].
- Max tests also don't build much muscle. Work in the 6-12 rep range, stop with about 2-3 reps left in the tank, and add weight only when you hit the top of the rep range [STR-05, STR-35, POP-82]. Technique matters more than the weight on the bar [POP-49].
- Get someone qualified to teach you technique and spot you, such as a coach at a gym or club. Teen lifting should be supervised [POP-47].
- Lifting won't stunt your growth. The evidence shows no link when sessions are supervised [POP-48].

**2. Change the programme.**
- Four days a week of mostly bench and curls leaves out your legs, back and shoulders. Cover all the major muscle groups at least twice a week [STR-01]. Muscle-building volume is a standard of about 10 sets per muscle per week (adult figure) [STR-02].
- For teens, aim for about 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps, up to roughly 80% of your max, on 2-3 non-consecutive days a week [POP-82, POP-84]. A full-body or upper/lower split fits that.
- The sources I have are written for 2-3 days a week. Four days is my judgement, so keep your 4 days only if you recover well: sleep, soreness and mood normal.

**3. Eat more, but not 5,000 calories.**
- Regular meals with enough total energy are what allow muscle gain. Extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle [NUT-62].
- Protein is about 1.5 g/kg a day, so roughly 90-95 g for you. Spread it over about 5 eating occasions of about 0.3 g/kg (roughly 19 g each) [NUT-62].
- The evidence gives no calorie target or weight-gain rate for growing teens. 5,000 kcal is my judgement as far too much, because you'd mostly gain fat. Add a bit to each meal and snack instead, and watch the trend over weeks.
- If you skip meals, worry a lot about your weight, or have a medical issue, see a doctor or sports dietitian [NUT-60, NUT-66].

**4. Supplements.**
- Pre-workout is mostly caffeine. I have no evidence on caffeine for teens, and it can wreck sleep, which is when you grow and recover. Skip it.
- Creatine: the guidance is to avoid performance supplements at your age. One position stand says it might be considered with supervision, but it's a minority view and I don't act on it [NUT-61, NUT-63, NUT-64]. If you still want it, talk to a doctor or sports dietitian with a parent involved [POP-53].
- For most young athletes, growing, good training, food and sleep give more than supplements [POP-51].

Never use steroids, prohormones or SARMs. I won't advise on them, and the risks are real.

**Tell me a few things so I can write a proper plan:**
1. What are your best sets now (weight x reps) on bench and curls?
2. What equipment do you have, and how many minutes per session?
3. Is there a gym staff member, teacher or club coach who could watch your technique?
4. Do you have any pain, injuries, or heart or health conditions? Have you ever had chest pain, dizziness or fainting during exercise? If yes, see a doctor before training hard.
5. Roughly how much do you eat and sleep now?

I'm also curious what "bulk" means to you: more strength, more size, or how you look? Any of those is fine. It just helps me aim the plan.
