# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** climbing in pregnancy can continue, but I'd stop bouldering falls now, at least until your OB has answered a specific question about them. Hangboarding can probably continue at a lower intensity. Your OB's "keep doing what you're doing" is a good sign: it suggests she hasn't found a reason for you to stop exercising. But she may not know that bouldering means falling onto a mat every session, so ask her about that directly.

## Why bouldering is the part to change

- Exercise in pregnancy without complications is safe and recommended. Keeping up activities you already do is encouraged [POP-40]. The evidence for this is strong.
- The same guidance says to avoid activities with a fall risk or a risk of a blow to the abdomen. It names climbers specifically and says to ask the obstetric provider about fall risk before continuing [POP-55]. Twelve years of practice falling lowers the chance of a bad landing. It doesn't stop a knee or a mat edge from hitting your bump, or a fall you didn't plan.
- There is no research on pregnant climbers. The advice above comes from general pregnancy guidance, not climbing studies.

## Hangboard repeaters

The evidence gives no load or effort limit for strength training in pregnancy, and doesn't cover hangboarding at all. So the changes below are my judgement and should be read as conservative:

- Keep 2 sessions a week with 48–72 h between them, as now [CLB-26].
- Hang well short of failure, not "near failure on the last rep". Take weight off with your feet on a chair or with a pulley, or drop a set.
- Breathe out during each hang rather than holding your breath.
- Stop the session if you feel any of the warning signs listed below.

## Suggested week until you've spoken to your OB (my judgement)

| Day | Session / exercise | Sets x reps or time | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Hangboard repeaters (half-crimp) | 2–3 sets of 4 x 10 s on / 5 s off, 1 min between sets | About 3 reps' worth left in reserve, never near failure | Feet-assisted or pulley-assisted. Warm up on the wall first. |
| Tue | Climbing with no falls | 45–60 min | Moderate (you can talk) | Traversing, problems well below your limit, down-climb every problem, no jumping off, no dynamic moves |
| Thu | Hangboard repeaters | as Mon | as Mon | Remove weight if Monday felt harder than usual |
| Sat | Climbing with no falls, or a walk, swim or bike | 45–60 min | Moderate | Aim for at least 150 min/week of moderate activity in total [POP-35] |
| Daily | Pelvic floor exercises | a few minutes | Easy | Recommended daily, ideally with a physio teaching the technique [POP-36] |

**Purpose:** keep your fitness and finger strength without risking a fall or a blow to the abdomen.
**Progression:** none for now. Hold this level or reduce it as your pregnancy goes on.
**If a session is missed:** don't make it up. Do a 20-minute easy traverse or a walk.

## Questions to take to your OB

1. "In bouldering I fall 2–4 m onto mats several times per session, and sometimes land on my back or knees. Is that acceptable at 31+ weeks?"
2. "Is roped climbing with a harness OK for me, and how should the harness fit?" There is no evidence on harness types in pregnancy.
3. "Is there anything in my pregnancy that should limit hard exercise?" For example a low-lying placenta, high blood pressure, a short cervix or twins [POP-37, POP-38]. If she has mentioned any of these, wait for her answer before training as above.

Because you train well above the general guideline, pregnancy guidance recommends supervision by a specialist healthcare provider [POP-10]. A physiotherapist who specialises in pregnancy would be a good fit.

## Stop and contact your OB if you get any of these

Vaginal bleeding, dizziness or faintness, shortness of breath before you start, chest pain, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or swelling, regular painful contractions, or fluid leaking from the vagina [POP-54].
