# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** staying active this far along is well supported, and your OB's "keep going" fits the guidelines. The part they single out is falling. Hangboarding has no pregnancy-specific evidence either way, so I'd keep it but take the intensity down.

The codes in brackets, like [POP-55], point to the sources if you want to look them up.

**Bouldering: the falls are the issue, not the climbing**
- ACOG (the US obstetricians' college) says to avoid activities where falls are likely or the belly can take an impact. It names climbers specifically and says to ask your obstetric provider about fall risk before carrying on [POP-55]. Good falling technique cuts injury risk to you, but even a controlled landing on a mat still jars your belly.
- "Keep doing what you're doing" may not have pictured jumping or falling from 3–4 m. It's worth asking her directly: *"I boulder, so I land on a mat from up to [height] a couple of times a session. Is that OK, or should I stop falling?"*
- Until she answers, here is what I'd do (my judgement; the evidence doesn't set a safe fall height): **no falling and no jumping off.** Stay on problems you can reliably climb back down. Skip dynos, big moves you might fail, and moves that press your belly into the wall.

**Hangboarding**
- The pregnancy evidence doesn't give limits on load or effort [gap in the knowledge base], so this part is judgement. Keep doing repeaters, but drop the load until you finish every set with a clear margin and not close to failure. Breathe through each hang. Feet-on hangs are a good lighter option [CLB-16].
- Keep two sessions a week with 48–72 h between them [CLB-26].

**Interim week (my judgement, until your OB has answered the fall question)**

| Day | Session | Volume | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Bouldering, climbing down every problem | 45–60 min | Moderate: you can still talk | Never top out anything you can't climb back down; no jumps or dynos |
| Tue | Hangboard repeaters (7 s on / 3 s off) | Fewer sets than you do now | Finish with a clear margin | Less weight or one foot on; half-crimp or open hand |
| Thu | Bouldering, as Monday | 45–60 min | Moderate | Same rules |
| Fri | Hangboard, as Tuesday | Same | Same | At least 48 h after Tuesday |
| Daily | Pelvic floor exercises | A few minutes | — | Well established for pregnancy; best with a physio's instruction [POP-36] |

Purpose: keep fitness and finger strength without the impact risk. Next week, don't add load; hold steady or reduce as the pregnancy goes on. Busy week: one low bouldering session plus a walk is enough.

**Stop and call your OB if** you get vaginal bleeding, dizziness or faintness, breathlessness before you start, chest pain, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or swelling, regular painful contractions, or fluid leaking [POP-54].

**Questions for you:**
1. Has your OB mentioned any complication, such as placenta previa, preeclampsia or high blood pressure, risk of preterm labour, twins, or anaemia? Any of these changes what's safe, so check with her before we go on [POP-37].
2. What else do you do in a week (walking, running, gym), and roughly how many minutes? The goal is at least 150 min of moderate activity a week [POP-35]. If you train far above that, the guidelines suggest a specialist healthcare provider oversees it [POP-10].
3. What's your repeater protocol now: edge size, added or removed weight, and number of sets? Then I can give you a specific lighter version.
4. Do you have access to top-rope or auto-belay? The evidence doesn't cover harness fit late in pregnancy, so that's another question for your OB if you want to rope-climb instead.
