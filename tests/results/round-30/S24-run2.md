# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** keep training, but stop bouldering until your OB has answered one specific question: is the fall risk OK for you now? Hangboarding can likely continue at lower intensity with your feet on the floor.

**Why bouldering is the part to change**
- The pregnancy guidelines are clear that exercise is safe and worth doing if there are no complications [POP-40, POP-35]. They also say to avoid activities with a fall risk or a risk of a blow to the abdomen. The ACOG guidance names climbers specifically: ask your obstetric provider about fall risk before you carry on [POP-55]. This comes from a consensus guideline. It's not a guess.
- "Keep doing what you're doing" is good general advice, but your OB may not have pictured repeated drops onto a mat. Ask her directly: *"I boulder and fall from up to about 4 m onto pads, sometimes landing on my back or awkwardly. At 31 weeks, is that OK?"*
- Knowing how to fall cuts the risk but doesn't remove it. Your balance and centre of mass are changing week by week, and you can't control every fall. That's my judgement, not something a study measured.
- No study looks at climbing in pregnancy at all, so everything beyond the fall-risk point is coaching judgement.

**Suggested changes until you've spoken to your OB** (my judgement where marked)

| Day | Session | Volume | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Low traversing or movement drills on easy terrain | 30-45 min | Easy to moderate, can talk in full sentences | Judgement: feet low enough that you can always step down, never jump or drop. Keeps your skill and climbing time without falls. |
| Day 2 | Hangboard repeaters with feet on the floor or a chair | 2-3 sets of 4 x 10 s on / 5 s off, 1 min between sets | Light to moderate strain, well short of failure | Your current protocol takes the last rep close to failure [CLB-06]. Judgement: take enough weight through your feet that you finish each set with plenty in reserve. The evidence gives no load limits for pregnancy. Avoid holding your breath and straining. |
| Day 3-4 | Walking, cycling on a trainer, swimming, or general strength | Toward 150 min/week moderate in total | Can talk in full sentences | The pregnancy guideline target [POP-35]. |
| Daily | Pelvic floor training | Short, daily | — | Recommended through pregnancy, ideally with technique instruction from a pelvic health physio [POP-36]. |

If a session gets skipped, a 20-minute walk plus pelvic floor work is a good enough minimum.

**Two more things**
- You've trained hard for 12 years. For pregnant athletes and anyone training well above the general guideline, the WHO recommends supervision by a specialist healthcare provider [POP-10]. A sports physician or a physio who works with pregnant athletes can review your climbing much better than either of us can.
- **Stop and contact your OB** if you get any of these: vaginal bleeding, dizziness or feeling faint, being short of breath before you start, chest pain, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or swelling, regular painful contractions, or fluid leaking [POP-54].

Two questions so I can be more specific:
1. Has your OB mentioned any complications, such as placenta position, blood pressure, twins or a short cervix? Any of these changes what's safe [POP-38, POP-39].
2. Do you also rope climb, or is it bouldering only? That tells me what to suggest if your OB rules out falls.
