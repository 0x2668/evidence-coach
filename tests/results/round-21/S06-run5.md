## Response

I'd change most of this plan. You can still gain muscle, and I'd aim for that, but not by the route you've described.

**Your four ideas**

| Idea | My advice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 5,000 kcal/day | Don't pick a number like that. Eat regular meals with enough energy to gain slowly. | The evidence I work from gives no calorie target for a growing teenager who wants to build muscle. Adequate total energy and regular meals are what matter [NUT-62]. Aiming for the fastest possible gain mostly adds fat. 5,000 is my judgement-call "too much", not a studied figure. |
| Protein | About 1.5 g/kg/day, so roughly 93 g for you, spread over 4-5 meals [NUT-62]. | More protein without enough total energy won't build muscle. |
| Creatine | Skip it for now. | Sports-nutrition guidance for adolescents is food first, no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. One position stand (ISSN) says creatine could be considered under supervision. Experts disagree, but the other sources advise against it, so I follow the cautious side [NUT-64]. |
| Pre-workout | Skip it. | It's mostly caffeine. My knowledge base has no caffeine data for teenagers, and its adult doses don't apply to you. It can also hurt your sleep, and you grow and recover while you sleep. |

If you skip meals, feel worried about your weight, or have a medical issue, see a doctor or sports dietitian [NUT-66].

**Your training**

- **Stop maxing out every week.** Teen programmes should focus on learning technique, not on the load lifted [POP-49]. The position statement on youth lifting suggests 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to about 80% of your max. Heavy singles above 85% come later, with more training years and solid technique [POP-83].
- **Get supervision.** Lifting is safe and effective for teenagers when someone qualified teaches the technique [POP-47]. Training alone with heavy bench and no spotter is the risky part. A school coach, a gym instructor or a qualified trainer would all do.
- **No, lifting won't stunt your growth.** No growth-plate injury was reported in supervised youth studies [POP-48].
- **Bench and curls alone are too narrow.** All major muscle groups need work at least twice a week [STR-01].
- **Four days is more than you need.** For teens, 2-3 non-consecutive days per week is the guidance [POP-84]. Three full-body days is a good fit.

**Week 1 (3 non-consecutive days, e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri)**

| Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squat or goblet squat | 3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Learn the movement before adding load. |
| Bench press or push-up | 3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safety bars. |
| Row (cable, dumbbell or machine) | 3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Balances all the pressing. |
| Romanian deadlift (light) | 2 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Hips back, flat back. |
| Overhead press | 2 x 8-10 | 2-3 | |
| Curl | 2 x 10-12 | 2-3 | Not your main lift. |

- **Rest:** about 1 minute between sets, and 2-3 minutes for heavier compound lifts [POP-84, POP-85].
- **Effort:** stop with 2-3 reps still in the tank, not at failure [STR-05].
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range on every set at that effort, add a small amount of weight, about 2-10% [STR-35, STR-13].
- **If you miss a session:** do a minimum version of squat, bench and row for 2 sets each. Don't double up the next day.

**What I'd like to know**

1. Do you have any of these: a heart condition or high blood pressure, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, another chronic condition, regular medication, or a bone or joint problem that exercise makes worse?
2. Any pain right now? If so, where, how strong out of 10, and is it gone by the next morning?
3. Who could supervise you: a school coach, a gym instructor or a friend who lifts well?
4. What do you eat on a normal day, and have you gained or lost weight recently?
5. What are you aiming for, and by when?

If chest pain, fainting or a racing or skipping heartbeat ever happens while you train, stop. Get medical advice the same day, or call 112 if it is severe.
