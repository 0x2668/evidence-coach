# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

Short answer: I'd change three of the four things you're planning. Lifting four times a week is fine, but 5,000 calories, the pre-workout and weekly maxing out aren't good ideas at 16.

**1. Food comes first, with no 5,000-calorie target**
- The evidence I work from gives no calorie target or weight-gain rate for growing teenagers who want to bulk [NUT-62]. I won't invent a number, and 5,000 is probably far more than you need. As a judgement call, a modest, steady increase in what you eat works better than a huge jump.
- What does have support is regular meals with enough total energy. Extra protein won't build muscle if you aren't eating enough overall [NUT-62].
- Protein: about 1.5 g/kg a day is enough for teen athletes, which is about 93 g for you. Spread it over roughly 5 eating occasions at about 0.3 g/kg each, or about 19 g per meal [NUT-62].
- Aim for steady weight gain over months, not as fast as possible. Muscle grows at a limited rate, and the extra gain from forcing food is mostly fat.

**2. Skip the creatine and the pre-workout for now**
- Sports dietitian and pediatric sources advise that adolescents get what they need from food and don't use performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Pre-workouts also often contain a lot of caffeine, and the evidence I have on caffeine covers adults only [NUT-55].
- Creatine is the one point where experts disagree. A minority view says it might be considered for supervised, serious young athletes. The other sources say no, so my default is to wait [NUT-64, NUT-61]. Supplements can also be contaminated [POP-51].

**3. Stop maxing out every week**
- Max attempts without a spotter or coach are the riskiest thing you listed. Most youth lifting injuries come from accidents like dropped weights, and supervision prevents many of them [POP-77].
- For teens, the focus should be technique, not the weight on the bar [POP-49]. Lifting is safe for teens and doesn't stunt growth when it's supervised and well taught [POP-47, POP-48].
- Muscle growth doesn't need maxing. Most sets should end with about 2-3 reps left in the tank [STR-05].

**A safer week (judgement, built on the rules above)**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Upper: bench, row, overhead press, curls | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 reps in reserve (RPE 7-8) | Use a spotter or safety bars on bench [POP-77] |
| Wed | Lower: squat or leg press, Romanian deadlift, calf raises | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 reps in reserve | Your plan has no leg work |
| Fri | Full body: pull-ups or pulldown, push-ups or dumbbell press, lunges, core | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 reps in reserve | Keep loads at or below about 80% of your max [POP-81] |

- Guidance for youth is 2-3 sessions a week on non-consecutive days [POP-84]. Four is not forbidden, so a 4th light technique day is an option if you want it, but I'd start with three.
- Rest about 1 minute between sets, and 2-3 minutes on heavy or technical lifts [POP-84].
- When you reach the top of the rep range with 2-3 reps still left, add a small amount of weight (double progression) [STR-35].
- If you miss a day, do a short 20-minute version of the same session and don't double up later.
- Try to find a supervised setting, such as a school gym, a club or a coach, because a teen shouldn't train unsupervised [POP-49].

**Questions for you (answers decide how hard I can push)**

2. Do you have any current pain? If so, where, and how strong out of 10?

4. What's your current typical day of eating, and do you ever skip meals?
5. Do you have a gym with a spotter, and what equipment can you use?

If you're worried about your body, or you're thinking about steroids or prohormones, talk to a doctor, ideally with a parent involved [POP-50, POP-53]. I can't advise on those, and I'm not judging you for asking.
