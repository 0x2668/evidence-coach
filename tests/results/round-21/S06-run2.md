# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Response

**Short answer:** don't max out weekly, don't start at 5,000 calories, and hold off on the supplements. Your training is the bigger fix. I'm treating you as an athlete training on your own.

**What I'd change, and why**

- **Stop weekly max attempts.** For teenagers the focus is learning technique, not the weight on the bar [POP-49]. Consensus says to build up to 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to about 80% of your 1RM. Heavy phases of 6 reps or fewer above 85% come only after more training age and sound technique [POP-81, POP-82]. Maxing out alone is also where injuries happen. Most youth lifting injuries are dropped weights and equipment mistakes that supervision prevents [POP-77]. Lifting does not stunt growth [POP-48].
- **Get someone to supervise.** Teen lifting should be supervised by a qualified person who teaches technique [POP-47]. A gym coach, a school PE or strength teacher, or a club coach would do. At minimum, have a spotter on bench.
- **Train 3 days a week, not 4.** The youth guidance is 2-3 non-consecutive days [POP-84]. That's still plenty for muscle gain.
- **Eat enough, but there's no calorie target I can give you.** The evidence I use has no energy figure for growing teenagers who want to build muscle, and 5,000 is a guess. A sports dietitian or doctor can set a number for your growth stage. Until then, eat regular meals with enough total food, because protein without enough energy won't build muscle [NUT-62]. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg/day is enough for teen athletes, which is about 93 g for you. That's roughly 19 g at each of 5 eating occasions [NUT-62].
- **Skip the pre-workout.** The caffeine evidence is in adults. I have no data for teens, and sleep matters a lot for growth and recovery. Teen guidance says food first, with no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Pre-workouts are also often poorly labelled products, and supplement contamination is a recognised concern [POP-51].
- **Creatine is contested.** One expert body says it might be considered for supervised, serious teen lifters [POP-53]. Pediatric guidance says supplements add little beyond puberty, training and food [POP-51, POP-52]. I wouldn't start it. If you're curious, ask a doctor or dietitian, with your parents involved.
- **"As fast as possible" is mostly set by puberty, sleep, food and consistent training.** Nothing in a tub speeds it up much [POP-52].

**Starter week (3 days, non-consecutive, for example Mon / Wed / Fri)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 3 | Warm-up: 5 min easy cardio, then 1-2 light sets of the first lift | - | - | Technique first |
| All 3 | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | You said bench and curls dominate, so legs are the gap |
| All 3 | Bench press (or push-ups, dumbbells) | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Spotter or safety arms; at most 60% of your 1RM for 2 weeks |
| All 3 | Row or lat pulldown | 2-3 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Match your pushing with pulling |
| All 3 | Overhead press or incline dumbbell | 2 x 8-10 | 2-3 | Alternate between days |
| 1-2 days | Biceps curls, plus a plank or core | 2 x 10-12 | 2-3 | Curls are fine as an extra, not the main work |

- **Purpose:** learn technique and build a base.
- **Rest:** about 1 min between sets, 2-3 min on heavier lifts [POP-85].
- **Progression:** the effort target is about 2-3 reps left in the tank, not failure [STR-05]. Start at 2 sets for 2 weeks, with loads at 60% of 1RM or less [POP-81]. Then go to 3 sets. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add a small amount of weight [STR-35, STR-13].
- **Missed day:** don't double up. Do one short session (squat, bench, row, 2 sets each) and carry on.

**Questions for you**

1. **Health:** have you been told you have a heart condition or high blood pressure, or do you get chest pain, dizziness or fainting? Do you have a bone or joint problem that exercise makes worse? If yes to any, see a doctor before lifting more.
2. **Pain:** do you have any pain now? If so, where is it, how bad is it out of 10, and is it back to normal by the next morning?
3. **Meals:** are you eating 3 regular meals a day now? Is worry about your weight or body shape driving the bulk? If so, a doctor or dietitian is the right first stop. Please don't take steroids or "muscle" products. If you're tempted, talk to a doctor with a parent involved [POP-50].
