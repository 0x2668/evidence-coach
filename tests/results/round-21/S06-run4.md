# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short version:** don't go to 5,000 kcal, skip the creatine and pre-workout, and stop testing your max every week. You can still gain muscle well without those.

**Why**

- **Weekly maxing:** it is the riskiest part of what you described. In youth lifting, most injuries come from accidents like dropped weights and poor technique, and the guidance is to build technique, not to chase the heaviest load [POP-49, POP-77]. Heavy singles on bench with no spotter are the worst case. Max attempts also don't build muscle faster than sets stopped 2-3 reps short of failure [STR-05, STR-28]. This is well established.
- **Supervision:** the consensus is that lifting is safe and worthwhile for teens, with qualified supervision and technique teaching [POP-47, POP-78]. You have no coach, so I'd try to fix that. A school PE teacher, a gym instructor or a qualified youth coach can check your bench and curl form a few times. Always lift with a spotter or in a rack with safety bars.
- **5,000 kcal:** the evidence I have gives no calorie target or safe gain rate for growing teens [NUT-62]. This is my judgement: a jump that large is mostly fat and discomfort. Eating regular meals with enough food, and about 1.5 g/kg protein (about 90 g/day for you, spread over the day), is what supports muscle gain [NUT-62]. Extra protein without enough total energy won't build muscle.
- **Creatine:** expert bodies disagree here, so I'd skip it. Sports Dietitians Australia and a 2021 review say no performance supplements for adolescents, while one ISSN view allows creatine under supervision [NUT-61, NUT-63, NUT-64]. This is contested, and there is no data on caffeine in teens.
- **Pre-workout:** it is mostly high-dose caffeine, which can wreck sleep and cause anxiety. Supplements can also contain undeclared substances [NUT-52, NUT-55]. Sleep is a much better "pre-workout" for you.

**Your week (my suggestion, using youth guidelines)**

Train 3 non-consecutive days, not 4. Youth guidance is 2-3 sessions a week [POP-84].

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon / Wed / Fri | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 (2-3 RIR) | You didn't mention legs, so I've added them. Skipping them leaves out the biggest muscles. |
| | Bench press | 2-3 x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 (2-3 RIR) | Use a spotter or safety bars. |
| | Row or pull-up | 2-3 x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 | Balances all the pressing. |
| | Overhead press or incline push-up | 2 x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 | |
| | Biceps curl | 2 x 10-12 | RPE 8-9 | The last set can end close to failure. |

- **Loads:** stay at or below about 80% of your max (roughly the 8-12 rep range). Rest 1-3 minutes between sets [POP-82, POP-84]. If your technique isn't solid, start lighter at 60% or less [POP-81].
- **Progression:** when you hit 12 reps on every set at 2-3 RIR, add a small amount of weight (2-10%) next session [STR-35, STR-13].
- **Missed session:** do one set of each exercise (about 20 minutes) and carry on. Don't stack sessions.
- **Fourth day:** if you want it, make it a walk, sport or play, not more lifting.

**A few questions so I can tailor this (reply when you can)**

1. Have you ever had chest pain, dizziness or fainting, or has a doctor said you have a heart, blood pressure or other health condition? If yes, see a doctor before training harder [SAF-04].
2. Any pain or injury right now, especially in the shoulders, elbows or wrists?
3. Do you have access to a rack with safety bars or a person who can spot you?
4. What does a normal day of eating look like, and are you hungry enough to eat more?
5. How long have you been lifting, and what are your current bench and curl weights for 8-10 reps?

If you ever feel pressure to change your body, or you're tempted by "stronger" products like prohormones or steroids, talk to a doctor. Those carry real health risks for someone still growing [POP-50]. Wanting to get bigger is completely normal. At 16 you are still growing, so steady training and food will take you further than it seems.
