# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short version:** don't do the 5,000 calories, the creatine, the pre-workout or the weekly maxing. You can still build muscle quickly with food, sensible training and sleep.

**What I'd change**

| Your plan | What I'd do instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 5,000 kcal/day | Eat regular meals, and add one or two extra snacks if you're not gaining. Aim for about 93 g protein a day (1.5 g/kg x 62 kg), spread over about 5 eating occasions. | Enough total energy with regular meals is what lets you gain muscle. Extra protein without enough energy won't build it [NUT-62]. The evidence has no calorie figure or safe gain rate for growing teens, so I won't invent one. 5,000 is a guess, and a big jump will mostly add fat. |
| Creatine | Skip it for now. | Experts disagree. Most guidance for teens says food first and no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. One minority position allows it only for serious, supervised young athletes [NUT-64]. If you still want it, talk to a doctor or sports dietitian with a parent involved [POP-53]. |
| Pre-workout | Skip it. | Teens aren't advised to use performance supplements [NUT-63]. The evidence I have on caffeine covers adults only. Many pre-workouts are mostly caffeine and can wreck your sleep, and sleep is when you recover. |
| Maxing out every week | Stop testing 1-rep maxes. Use about 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps, at no more than about 80% of your max [POP-81, POP-82]. | At your age the aim is good technique, not the heaviest load [POP-49]. Weekly maxes carry high injury risk and teach you little. |
| 4 days of mostly bench and curls | Train 3 non-consecutive days a week and cover your whole body: legs, back, chest, shoulders and arms [POP-84, STR-01]. | Bench and curls alone leave out most of your muscle, and extra days add little. Stop each set with 2-3 reps left in the tank, not at failure [STR-05]. Add weight only when you hit the top of your rep range [STR-35]. |

**The biggest gap is that you're lifting with no coach.** Resistance training is safe for teens and doesn't stunt growth [POP-48]. That holds when someone qualified supervises and teaches technique [POP-47]. Ask a school PE teacher, a gym instructor or a qualified coach to check your form on a few lifts and spot you on bench. Ask a parent to help find one.

**Questions for you:**
1. What do you want out of this: strength, size, a look, a sport? Why now?
2. Do you have any pain or injuries, especially in the shoulders, elbows or wrists?
3. Do any of the PAR-Q+ screening questions apply to you? These are:
   - a heart condition or high blood pressure
   - chest pain
   - dizziness or fainting
   - another chronic condition
   - medication for a chronic condition
   - a bone or joint problem that exercise makes worse
   - being told to exercise only under supervision
4. Have you skipped meals or felt worried about your weight or body?

I'm not judging any of this. I ask because wanting to bulk as fast as possible sometimes goes with pressure about how you look. If you've been tempted by steroids or "bodybuilding" products, I can't advise on those. A doctor is the right person to talk to, and I can explain the health risks.

If you answer these, I can write you a 3-day full-body week in a table.
