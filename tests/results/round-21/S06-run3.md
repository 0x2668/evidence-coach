# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short answer:** Don't max out every week, don't start at 5,000 kcal, and skip the pre-workout and creatine for now. You can still build muscle well at 16 with the setup below.

**1. Stop weekly max attempts [POP-81, POP-49, POP-48]**
- The youth guidelines say to progress from 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to 80% of your max. Loads above 85% for 6 reps or fewer come later, once you have more training years and solid technique, and with supervision.
- Maxing out alone, with no spotter and no coach, is the riskiest way to train. Heavy bench alone is where most of the danger is.
- Lifting is safe for teenagers, and it does not stunt growth [POP-48]. The problem is unsupervised max attempts, not lifting itself.
- Heavy singles also aren't needed for size. Sets of 6-12 stopped about 2-3 reps before failure build muscle just as well [STR-05].

**2. Food: a big surplus isn't the plan**
- The evidence I have gives no safe calorie target for growing teenagers who want to gain muscle, so I won't invent a number. 5,000 kcal is a guess, and I'd treat it as too much. Most of the extra would likely become fat.
- What the evidence does say: eat regular meals, enough in total, with protein spread across about 5 eating occasions. Aim for about 1.5 g/kg/day, which is roughly 93 g for you [NUT-62]. Extra protein without enough total food won't build muscle.
- Muscle at your age comes mostly from growing up, steady training and enough food [NUT-61]. Slow and steady works better than as fast as possible.
- A sports dietitian or your doctor can give you a calorie target that fits your growth. I'd suggest that over a number from me.

**3. Creatine and pre-workout [NUT-61, NUT-63, NUT-64, POP-53]**
- This is contested. Pediatric and sports-dietitian bodies advise teens to skip performance supplements. One minority position, from authors with industry ties, says creatine might be okay for serious, supervised athletes.
- My default is no. If you still want creatine, talk to a doctor and your parents first.
- My sources give no safe amount of caffeine for teens, and pre-workouts often contain a lot of it. Skip it. Sleep and food will do more for you.

**4. A safer structure for your 4 days [POP-81, STR-05, STR-35]**

| Day | Focus | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upper: bench, row, overhead press | 2-4 x 6-12 each | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safety bars |
| 2 | Lower and core: squat or leg press, hinge | 2-4 x 6-12 | 2-3 | Learn the technique first |
| 3 | Upper: incline press, pull-up or pulldown, curls | 2-4 x 6-12 | 2-3 | Curls are fine, but back and legs matter as much |
| 4 | Full body, lighter | 2-3 x 8-12 | 3 | Technique day |

- RIR means reps in reserve, how many reps you could still do. RIR 2-3 means you stop with 2-3 left. That equals RPE 7-8 [STR-29].
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range at the target effort, add a small amount of weight [STR-35]. Your best weight will rise without maxing out.
- **Missed day:** do a short version with 1-2 main lifts, so a busy week doesn't derail you [BEH-28].
- **Coaching:** ask a school PE teacher, a qualified coach or a gym staff member to check your form on bench, squat and hinge. The guidelines call for supervised youth lifting, and I can't watch you lift.

**A few questions to tailor this**
1. How long have you been lifting, and what do you bench and curl now?
2. How's your eating now: regular meals, or skipping some?
3. Is the bulk about size, or are you unhappy with your body? Either answer is fine. I ask because body-image worries are worth talking through with a doctor [POP-50].
4. Any injuries or pain? Any chest pain, dizziness or fainting when you train? If yes, see a doctor before training hard.
5. Are you tempted by steroids or "bodybuilding" products? I can't advise on those. If you are, please talk to a doctor.
