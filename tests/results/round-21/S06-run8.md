# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

You're right to want to build muscle, but I'd change most of this plan. For a 16-year-old, three parts of it aren't supported by the evidence.

**What to drop**
- **Pre-workout.** The knowledge base has no evidence on caffeine for teens. Its caffeine figures are for adults, and sleep and anxiety effects vary [NUT-55]. Sports Dietitians Australia and a 2021 review advise against performance supplements for young athletes [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Pre-workouts are also often contaminated or mislabelled [POP-51].
- **Creatine.** Experts disagree here. The American Academy of Pediatrics says supplements add little for most young athletes [POP-51, POP-52]. The International Society of Sports Nutrition says creatine might be considered, but only with supervised training and a balanced diet [POP-53], and its authors have industry ties. With no coach, I'd skip it.
- **Max testing every week.** Youth guidelines have you train at up to about 80% of your 1-rep max. Heavy sets above 85% come only after you have more training experience and sound technique [POP-81 to POP-83]. I'd put weekly max attempts down to unsupervised lifting. Most youth lifting injuries are accidents, like dropped weights, and a spotter and supervision prevent them [POP-77]. Resistance training doesn't stunt growth, but it should be supervised [POP-47, POP-48]. Most of your sets should end with 2-3 reps left in the tank, not at your limit [STR-05].

**Food**
- The evidence gives no calorie target for a growing teen, so I won't endorse 5,000. That is my judgement, not a researched figure. Jumping straight there mostly adds fat.
- What the evidence does say is that enough total energy from regular meals is what lets you build muscle. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg/day is enough, which is about 93 g for you, spread over around 5 eating occasions [NUT-62]. Extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle.
- A sensible start is to add one or two solid meals or snacks a day, such as milk, eggs, rice, bread, peanut butter or yoghurt. Then watch your weight over 3-4 weeks and adjust.

**A safer plan**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 3, 5 (not consecutive) | Squat or leg press | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Start light and learn the movement [POP-49, POP-81] |
| | Bench press or push-up | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safety bars |
| | Row | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Back work balances all the bench |
| | Romanian deadlift or hip hinge | 2 x 8-10 | 2-3 | |
| | Overhead press, then curls | 2 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |

- **Why three days.** Youth guidance says 2-3 sessions a week on non-consecutive days [POP-84]. Your four days isn't in the evidence, so this is the conservative choice. Covering all the major muscle groups matters more than bench and curls [STR-01].
- **Rest and progression.** Rest about 1 minute between sets, and 2-3 minutes on the heavy ones [POP-84]. Add a rep each week. Once you hit the top of the range with 2-3 reps left, add 2-10% load [STR-35, STR-13].
- **Missed session.** Do a short version with one set of each exercise.

**Questions for you**
1. Do you have any heart condition, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, or a joint or bone problem that gets worse with exercise? If you do, see a doctor before you keep lifting.
2. Does anything hurt right now? If so, where, and how bad out of 10?
3. Can you train somewhere with a spotter or a supervisor, such as a school gym teacher, a friend who knows how to spot, or a gym with staff? If not, stick to machines and push-ups.
4. Are you skipping meals, or very worried about your weight or how you look? If so, talk to a doctor or sports dietitian.

I also don't advise on steroids or similar products. If you're ever tempted, talk to a doctor first.
