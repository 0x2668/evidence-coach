## Response

# Client R: analysis of last week and plan for next week

**Bottom line:** the 21 km run is the main problem, and heavy legs plus 6 h sleep argue against adding load. Next week should absorb that run, with fewer hard sessions and a long run back at 14 km. This plan is provisional until the red-flag questions below are answered.

## Analysis

| Finding | Numbers | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Long-run spike** | 21 km vs 14 km, +50% (limit 15.4 km) | This falls in the 30-100% spike band, with an overuse-injury rate ratio of 1.52 (95% CI 1.16-2.00) [END-33, END-34; single cohort study]. It is an association, not a prediction for R. |
| **Long run was not easy** | 5:05/km, RPE 7, target pace 4:59/km | That is about 2% slower than race pace for almost the full distance (1:46:45). |
| **"Easy" run was not easy** | 158 of 186 bpm = 85% HRmax, RPE 6 | This is about 3-zone Z2 by the rough bands (84-88%), not Z1, though those bands come from elite skiers [END-03, END-12; expert opinion]. Heart-rate reading: wrist or chest? [MON-22] |
| **Too little easy running** | Only Sunday may be easy, and it has no HR or RPE | Tue, Wed, Thu and Sat were all moderate to hard, whereas most training should be easy [END-01, END-07; contested on distribution, not on this]. |
| **Squat too close to failure** | 4 x 6 at RPE 9 = 1 RIR | Main lifts should mostly be 2-4 RIR, and 2-3 sets capture most of the strength gain [STR-31, STR-03; expert opinion, consensus]. |
| **Short sleep** | About 6 h vs a 7 h+ adult target | Expect about 7.6% lower performance after a night of 6 h or less; morning sessions were largely unaffected [MON-31, MON-37, MON-38; consensus, meta-analysis]. |

**What went well:** two rest days, strength work on a separate day from the intervals [END-54], and heavy loading kept in the plan.

**Heavy legs:** this fits the load and short sleep, but one week can't establish the cause. Self-report outweighs device data when they disagree [MON-18]. If it lasts for weeks despite less training, refer him [SAF-60, SAF-61].

**The 1:45 target:** I can't judge it from this data. The 5:05/km run is encouraging, but 800 m reps can't be used to estimate critical speed, and there is no HR for that run [END-19]. Recent race or time-trial results would help.

**Don't reset the baseline:** read literally, the 10% rule now allows 23.1 km, because 21 km is in the 30-day window. I would not use that, and would keep 14 km as the base (my judgement). Consecutive 10% steps can also be too much [MON-12].

## Check before Tuesday

- **If any of these, he stops training and gets medical advice today:**
  - dark or cola-coloured urine since Saturday, or muscle pain out of proportion, with swelling or weakness [SAF-51, SAF-52]
  - dizziness or fainting, breathlessness on mild exertion, ankle swelling, calf pain on walking, or unusually high fatigue [SAF-15]
  - sudden unusual breathlessness, chest pain with nausea, or a racing, slow, skipping or fluttering heartbeat [SAF-20]
  - a joint that can't bear weight, is badly swollen or misshapen, locks or gives way, or is hot and red with fever [SAF-22, SAF-23, SAF-26]
- **If he has either of these, refer him soon (GP, physio or sports physician) and adapt training meanwhile:**
  - pain that has not improved within a few weeks [SAF-24]
  - localised bone pain: pause the running progression and get it assessed [END-39]
- **Any other pain:** continue only if it stays at or below 5/10 and is back to baseline by the next morning [SAF-30].

**Week's purpose:** absorb the 21 km spike and restore a real contrast between hard and easy days. That means about 36 km of running vs 45 km last week (the size of the cut is my judgement) [MON-03, MON-05].

**Evidence limits:**
- The 4 x 800 m, the 14 km and the 154 bpm cap are my conservative choices, not figures from the evidence.
- The knowledge base has no zone paces for R without a critical-speed test.
- Many of these studies are in younger or trained groups. The long-run data, however, are a good fit: mean age 45.8, 78% men.

**Progression, if check-ins are normal and he is pain-free:**
- **Long run:** add about 1.5 km a week, staying within 10% of the longest run since the spike (my judgement). The knowledge base gives no half-marathon long-run target, so I would aim for 18-20 km by week 8.
- **Strength:** build towards 2 sessions a week with 3 x 6 squats at RPE 8 [END-53]. Keep them on separate days from hard runs, or more than 3 h after [END-54].
- **Taper:** use weeks 9-10, cutting volume by about 41-60% and keeping intensity and frequency [END-50; meta-analysis].

**If sessions are missed:** do not stack them. The minimum week is the long run, one easy run and one short strength session [BEH-28].
