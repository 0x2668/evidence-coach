# S10: Running and lifting at 20 weeks of pregnancy

## Prompt

I'm 20 weeks pregnant, healthy pregnancy so far, and before I was running about 30 km a week and lifting twice a week. My midwife said exercise is fine. Can I keep running and lifting, and what should I watch out for?

## Response

**Yes, you can most likely keep running and lifting.** Without obstetric or medical complications, exercise in pregnancy is considered safe and desirable, and that is well established. The guideline minimum is 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, which you probably already exceed.

**Check with your midwife**

"Exercise is fine" may not have meant your actual training, so tell them the specifics: 30 km a week and lifting twice a week, including your typical loads. Guidance is for specialist supervision if you train well above the guideline, and your provider should agree to what you're doing. Also ask about these, as the evidence I have gives no figures or rules for them:
- how heavy you should lift
- whether you need a heart rate or effort limit
- whether exercises lying on your back are OK
- how to manage heat

**Keep your training safe** (my judgement, given those gaps)
- **Running:** Use the talk test and stay at a pace where you can speak in full sentences. Treat 30 km as a ceiling rather than something to maintain, and cut back without worrying if you feel you need to.
- **Lifting:** Stop a few reps short of failure, skip max attempts, and keep breathing rather than holding your breath. Reduce the weight or swap an exercise if it feels uncomfortable.
- **Pelvic floor:** Do pelvic floor exercises daily, and ask your midwife or a pelvic floor physio to check your technique. This one is a guideline recommendation, not just my judgement.

**Avoid**
- Contact sports or anything with a risk of a blow to the abdomen
- Fall-prone activities, such as downhill skiing, surfing, off-road cycling, gymnastics and horse riding
- Hot yoga or hot Pilates
- Scuba diving and skydiving
- Activity above 6,000 ft (about 1,800 m), unless you live at altitude

If you run on trails, ask your midwife about the fall risk first, or stick to even ground until you've spoken to them.

**Stop exercising and contact your midwife or obstetric provider if you have any of these:**
- Vaginal bleeding
- Fluid leaking from the vagina
- Regular, painful contractions
- Dizziness or feeling faint
- Shortness of breath before you start exercising
- Chest pain
- Headache
- Muscle weakness
- Calf pain or swelling

Call them the same day. Call 112 (999 in the UK) if you have severe chest pain or severe difficulty breathing, or if you collapse.

**When to stop and check again**

If you're ever diagnosed with something like preeclampsia, high blood pressure, a placenta or cervix problem, growth restriction, or anaemia with symptoms, stop and ask your provider before training again. Some of these limit you to normal daily activities, and others mean discussing what's safe first. The same goes for twins or more, though a twin pregnancy may only need that discussion from week 28.
