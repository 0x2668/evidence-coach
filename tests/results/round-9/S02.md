## Response

**You can do this in 16 weeks if you treat finishing as the goal.** The plan peaks at about 65 km a week, with a long run of 32 km or 4 hours, whichever comes first. Your race will probably last around 7.5-9 hours (my rough estimate), so check the cut-off.

There are no controlled trials of ultra preparation. Weekly distances, session designs and climbing targets are my judgement, built around the evidence-based rules below. Much of this research is mostly in men.

## The rules behind it
- **Long runs:** no run goes more than 10% beyond your longest in the previous 30 days, because bigger single-run jumps were linked to more overuse injuries. No rule can guarantee you stay injury-free.
- **Intensity:** nearly all your running should be easy, and long runs should stay in Z1. Amateur runners spent about 86% of a 65 km mountain ultra in this range.
- **Back-to-backs:** these are optional coaching practice and haven't been tested in trials. The usual 48-64 km over two days doesn't fit your hours, so I capped them at about 41-42 km.
- **Downhill:** keep this going throughout, because the protection can fade after about 9 weeks without it. The best dose for trail runners isn't known.
- **Strength:** two sessions is the low end of the recommended 2-3, chosen to fit your five days.
    - **Weeks 1-4:** use the loads shown in Week 1, adding a third set from Week 3 if you recover well.
    - **Weeks 5-13:** if your form is solid, move the main lifts to 2-3 sets of 6-8 reps at 2-3 RIR. Once you reach 8 reps on all sets, add 2-10% to the load and drop back to 6.
    - **Weeks 14-16:** cut sets, and do no heavy leg work in race week (my judgement).

## Fuelling
- **Long runs:** take 30-60 g carbohydrate per hour, building up gradually. For the race, aim for 150-400 kcal per hour, including 30-50 g carbohydrate and 5-10 g protein, using foods you've already tested.
- **Fluids:** drink to thirst and include a planned sodium source. Weigh yourself before and after long runs to estimate your sweat rate. If you gain weight during a run, you're drinking too much.
- **Daily food:** aim for 1.4-2.0 g/kg of protein, with more carbohydrate around Tuesday and Saturday. Eat enough to match the rising load, and don't try to lose weight during this build.

## When to stop or get checked
- **Pain:** you can continue if it stays at or below 5/10 during and right after a session, and is back to baseline by morning. If not, cut back. If it keeps happening or lasts a few weeks, see a physiotherapist.
- **Get medical assessment soon:** localised bone pain, especially in the foot, means pausing the running build. Stress fractures are more common in women. Also see a doctor or sports dietitian if your periods become irregular or stop.
- **Get medical advice the same day:** this applies to fainting, palpitations, unusual breathlessness, or dark urine after hard exercise.
- **Call 112:** this applies to severe chest pain or severe difficulty breathing at any time, or to confusion, vomiting, a seizure or collapse during or after a long run or the race.
- **Long-term risk:** repeated ultras may carry heart and kidney risks, though the evidence is low quality. It's worth knowing if this becomes a regular habit.
