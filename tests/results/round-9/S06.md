# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Slow this down a little: skip the 5,000 calories, creatine, pre-workout and weekly maxes.** At 16, your biggest gains come from growing, regular training, enough food and sleep. Lifting won't stunt your growth when it's properly supervised, and that's the key part: you need someone to teach you in person. [POP-47, POP-48, POP-52]

## Training
- **Find a qualified coach:** ask a parent to help you find one through your school, a club or a gym. Lifting should be supervised at your age, and technique matters more than the weight for now. [POP-47, POP-49]
- **Stop maxing every week:** you don't need to go to failure to grow, and maxes tell you your strength rather than build it. Many teen lifting injuries are accidents, like dropped weights, so always have a spotter on bench. [STR-05, POP-77]
- **Train your whole body:** bench and curls miss your back and legs. Lift 2-3 times a week with rest days in between, rather than 4. [POP-84, STR-11]

Here's a starting week to go through with the coach. The exercises and rep counts are my judgement, as the evidence only sets the overall limits.

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon, Wed, Fri (or just 2 of these) | Goblet squat | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 RIR (reps left in the tank) | Up to 60% of your max; don't test a max to work this out |
| | Romanian deadlift | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 RIR | Learn with a light weight first |
| | Bench press | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 RIR | Use a spotter |
| | Row | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 RIR | Keep it controlled |
| | Curls (optional) | 1-2 x 10-12 | At least 3 RIR | Finish with these |

- **Purpose of this week:** learn the movements with clean form.
- **Progression:** once the coach says your technique is solid, build to 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps, up to about 80% of your max. Add weight only when you reach the top of the rep range on every set. [POP-81, POP-82, STR-35]
- **If you miss a session:** don't double up. Just carry on from your last completed session, and on a busy day do one set of each exercise.

## Food
- **Calories:** the evidence I have doesn't give a calorie target or weight-gain rate for teens, so I won't give you one. In my judgement, 5,000 a day is far more than your body can turn into muscle, and much of the extra would likely end up as fat. A doctor or sports dietitian can set a number for you. [NUT-62]
- **Meals:** eat regularly and don't skip meals, with about 5 meals and snacks a day. Include carbs and protein throughout the day, especially right after training. [NUT-60, NUT-62]
- **Protein:** about 1.5 g/kg is enough, which is roughly 93 g a day for you, or about 19 g each time you eat. This only helps if you're eating enough overall. [NUT-62]
- **Sleep:** aim for 8-10 hours a night. [MON-32]

## Supplements
- **Creatine:** experts disagree about creatine for teens. One position statement allows it only for young athletes in serious supervised training, but other sources advise against it. I don't recommend it, and that decision belongs with you, your parents and a doctor or sports dietitian. [POP-51, POP-53, NUT-63]
- **Pre-workout:** I don't have evidence on the caffeine in these for teens, so I can't call any dose safe. Supplements can also contain ingredients not on the label, which is one more reason to skip it. [NUT-52]

## A few questions
1. **Experience and support:** how long have you been lifting, and can a parent help you find a coach?
2. **Health:** do any of these apply to you?
   - Heart condition or high blood pressure
   - Chest pain
   - Dizziness or passing out
   - Another long-term condition
   - Prescribed medication for a long-term condition
   - A bone, joint or muscle problem that activity makes worse
   - Being told to exercise only under supervision

   **If you have a racing or skipping heartbeat, dizziness or fainting, or unusual breathlessness, stop training and get medical advice today.** If it's severe chest pain or trouble breathing, or you collapse, call 112 (999 in the UK). For any other yes, see a doctor before training harder.
3. **Pain:** has maxing left you with any pain?
   - **Get medical advice today** if a joint is badly swollen or misshapen, locks or gives way.
   - **See a GP or physiotherapist** if the pain hasn't improved within a few weeks.
4. **Your goal:** what's making you want to gain size quickly? For example, is it a sport, or how you feel about your body? There's no wrong answer. If it's on your mind a lot, or you're skipping meals, a doctor can help.
5. **Products:** are you already taking anything, including products from the gym or online, or has anyone offered you any? If it's steroids, SARMs or "bodybuilding" products, talk to a doctor and involve a parent. They won't get you in trouble with me, and I can explain the risks.
