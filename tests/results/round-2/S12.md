**Mostly easy, but the last part probably wasn't.** You started easy and drifted out of easy by the end, even though your pace didn't change.

| Measure | Heart rate | % of your max (190) |
|---|---|---|
| Whole run average | 150 | 79% |
| First half average | 144 | 76% |
| Second half average | 156 | 82% |

- **Time in each zone:** you spent about 51 of the 60 minutes at or below 158 bpm, the rough top of easy (Z1, about 83% of max). The other 9 minutes or so were in the moderate zone (Z2, 158-167 bpm), and none were above that.
- **Why it rose:** your pace held at 6:00/km on flat ground, yet your heart rate climbed 12 bpm. This is called cardiovascular drift. It's normal on longer steady runs and larger in the heat, but it does mean the effort really was getting harder.

**How sure this is:** these bands come from a study of elite skiers, and percentages of max are only a rough guide for any one person. Your lab max makes the numbers better than a formula would, but your easy limit could still sit above or below 158. The best check is how it felt: if you could still talk in full sentences at the end, it was easy, and if speech was getting uncomfortable, it had moved into moderate.

**Next time:** if you want the whole run easy, let your pace slow in the second half rather than holding 6:00/km. Keep your heart rate under about 158 and stay able to talk in full sentences. That limit is my starting point from those rough bands, so adjust it based on how the talk test goes.

**Check the file:** it's dated 25 October, which is five days ago, not yesterday, so make sure it's the right run.

**Three quick questions to firm this up:**
1. Could you speak in full sentences during the last 10 minutes?
2. Was it warm, or were you tired or short on sleep? Heat in particular would explain more of the rise.
3. Was the heart rate from a chest strap or your wrist? Wrist readings are typically off by about 3%, more for some people and devices, which is about 5 bpm at this effort and could shift some minutes across the 158 line.
