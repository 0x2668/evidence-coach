# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Bouldering: no, stop now.** At 31 weeks it's out entirely, including low traversing and down-climbing. Your 12 years of experience don't change that. Knowing how to fall lowers the chance of a bad landing, but it doesn't remove the risk. Pregnancy guidance says to avoid fall-prone activities [POP-55], and it names climbers specifically. It doesn't give a week number. Week 28 is my coaching cutoff, which is the start of the third trimester.

"Keep doing what you're doing" is a general green light. It probably wasn't an answer to whether you can fall onto a mat. Ask her that exact question. Roped climbing is also off unless she explicitly agrees, both on falls and on how your harness fits.

**Hangboarding: allowed in principle, with caveats.**
- It isn't a fall or impact activity, so the fall rule doesn't rule it out.
- The evidence I have gives no pregnancy-specific loads, effort limits or hangboard guidance. Anything below is my judgement.
- Treat it as a maintenance session, not a training block:
  - Hang well below your usual effort, with plenty of reps left.
  - No max hangs, and no new or harder protocols.
  - Breathe normally and don't hold your breath.
  - Keep your feet on the floor or a step for hang-offs and bail-outs, so you never drop from the bar.
- Keep the usual spacing of about two sessions a week with 48-72 h between them [CLB-26]. That figure is for general climbers, not pregnancy.
- Check this with your OB or a specialist sports provider as well. Women training well above the guideline should be supervised by one [POP-10].

**What to do with the rest of your training:**
- Aim for at least 150 min/week of moderate activity, such as brisk walking, easy cycling or swimming, if your OB has no complications to flag [POP-35].
- Do daily pelvic floor training, ideally with technique instruction [POP-36].
- Keep strength work light, and leave the rest of your climbing out. The evidence gives no loads for pregnancy, so I won't invent any.

**Stop and contact your OB or midwife if you have any of these** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before exercise
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions
- fluid leaking from the vagina

If you have chest pain or severe breathing difficulty, call emergency services (112 in the EU, 999 in the UK, 911 in the US).

If it's useful, I can sketch a low-key weekly layout with the hangboard maintenance and the other activity. The evidence is strongest on avoiding falls and on the stop signs. It's thin on hangboarding in pregnancy.
