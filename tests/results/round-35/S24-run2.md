# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** stop bouldering now. Hangboarding can continue in a reduced, supported form, but check it with your OB first. Claim IDs like [POP-55] point to the sources in the skill's reference files.

**Bouldering**
- At 31 weeks you're in the third trimester. Guidance for pregnancy says to avoid fall-prone activities and to ask the obstetric provider specifically about fall risk for climbing [POP-55]. That includes low traversing and down-climbing.
- Your OB's "keep doing what you're doing" is a general answer. It doesn't say she was asked about falls or about climbing in particular.
- Knowing how to fall doesn't remove the risk. Your 12 years make you better at falling, but they don't change the consequences of a fall now.
- The week-28 cutoff is my coaching decision. The source says to avoid fall-prone activities but doesn't name a week.
- Roped climbing is not a swap-in. Do it only if your OB explicitly agrees about falls and about how a harness fits now.

**Hangboard**
- The evidence does not give a figure or protocol for hangboarding in pregnancy. What follows is my judgement, so please confirm it with your OB.
- Keep your feet on the floor or a chair so you can step off at any time. A drop from the board is a fall risk.
- Use repeaters well below your usual effort and stop each set far from failure. Skip max hangs.
- Cut the volume to a fraction of what you did before. Leave out any hang that needs a shaky or committed grip.
- Do two short sessions a week at most, with 48-72 h between them [CLB-26].

**Questions for your OB**
1. Is climbing and bouldering fine at 31 weeks, given the fall risk?
2. Do I have any contraindication, such as placenta previa, preeclampsia, or a risk of preterm labour? These limit you to usual daily activity [POP-37].

**What to do instead**
- Aim for about 150 min/week of moderate activity [POP-35], such as walking, easy cycling or swimming, if your OB clears it.
- Add easy, controlled strength work, with no hanging or falling.
- Do daily pelvic floor training. A pelvic floor physiotherapist can teach you the technique [POP-36].

**Stop and call your OB or midwife if you have:** vaginal bleeding, dizziness or faintness, shortness of breath before exercising, chest pain, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or swelling, regular painful contractions, or fluid leaking [POP-54].

If you'd like, I can sketch a week of non-climbing sessions around your schedule.
