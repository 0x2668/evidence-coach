# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Bouldering: no, not at 31 weeks. Hangboarding: yes, but in a much reduced form.**

**Bouldering**
- Stop bouldering now, including low traversing and down-climbing. This is a firm instruction, whatever your experience. The week number (28) is my coaching cut-off. The source (ACOG) says to avoid fall-prone activities and gives no week [POP-55].
- Knowing how to fall doesn't remove the risk, because a fall in late pregnancy is a risk to the baby as well as to you. That reasoning is my judgement.
- Your OB's "keep doing what you're doing" was a general answer. Ask her this specific question: "Is a fall from bouldering height acceptable for me now?" [POP-55]. Even a yes from her wouldn't change my advice at this stage.
- Roped climbing would need her explicit agreement on falls and harness fit.

**Hangboarding**
- The evidence I have gives no figures for finger loading in pregnancy. There's no fall risk, and general guidance says activity is safe and desirable without complications [POP-40]. So the plan below is a conservative choice and my judgement.

| Day | Session | Sets and time | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 non-consecutive days (48-72 h apart [CLB-26]) | Easy warm-up, then hangboard repeaters | 2 sets, the starting dose in the hangboard guidance [CLB-09], with the same hold time and rest you already use | Stop with a few clean reps left, never near failure | Half-crimp, no added weight, keep your feet on the floor or a step. Skip any session you feel off. |
| Daily | Pelvic floor training | As taught to you | Easy | Ask your OB or a pelvic-health physio for technique [POP-36] |
| Most days | Moderate activity you can step out of | Build toward 150 min/week in total [POP-35] | Moderate | Talk test: you can speak in sentences. |

- Purpose of the week: keep finger strength and routine without falls or maximal effort.
- If a session is missed, don't make it up. A single 2-set session is enough.
- Any finger pain that exceeds 5/10 or isn't gone by next morning means you stop hangboarding. Anything that persists for weeks goes to a physio [SAF-30, SAF-24].
- Because you train well above the guideline, a specialist provider should oversee your training [POP-10]. Tell your OB the details of your hangboard routine too.

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you have:**
- vaginal bleeding;
- dizziness or faintness;
- shortness of breath before exercise;
- chest pain;
- headache;
- muscle weakness;
- calf pain or swelling;
- regular painful contractions;
- fluid leaking [POP-54].

If you have chest pain or severe breathing difficulty, call 112 (or your local emergency number).

The claim IDs in brackets point to the sources in the skill's reference files. The ACOG and Canadian guidelines behind them are consensus-level, and none of them is specific to climbers.

Has your OB mentioned any complications, such as high blood pressure, placenta position, or a twin pregnancy? Those would change this advice and need her sign-off first [POP-37].
