# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

You can build muscle well at 16, but 5,000 calories, a pre-workout and weekly maxing are the wrong plan. Here is what I'd do instead.

**1. Food first, no supplements**
- Skip the creatine and the pre-workout. Sports dietitian and paediatric bodies advise against performance supplements for teenagers, and these products can be contaminated. The evidence is mixed on creatine in teens: one minority view allows it under supervision, but the other sources disagree. [NUT-61, NUT-63, NUT-64, POP-51]
- Protein: about 1.5 g/kg a day is enough for a teenager who trains, so about 93 g for you. That is roughly 20 g at each of 5 meals or snacks. Extra protein without enough total food won't build muscle. [NUT-62]
- Calories: the research gives no figure for how much a growing teen should eat to gain muscle. I won't give you 5,000 as a target, and I'd treat it as too much to jump to. Start by eating regular meals with no skipped ones, and add a little more food while you watch your weight and energy over a few weeks. That is my judgement, not a researched number. [NUT-62]

**2. Stop maxing every week**
- Teen lifting guidance says to focus on technique, not on how much you lift. Start at no more than 60% of your max and build to 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to 80%. Heavy singles above 85% come only after more training years and good technique. [POP-49, POP-81, POP-82, POP-83]
- Most lifting injuries in teens come from accidents, like dropped weights or no spotter. Max attempts without a coach or spotter are the riskiest thing you're doing. [POP-77]
- Lifting doesn't stunt growth. [POP-48]

**3. A better week (my adaptation of the youth guidance)**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon / Wed / Fri | Full body: bench or push-up, row, squat or leg press, a hinge such as Romanian deadlift, overhead press, curls | 2-3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 reps left in the tank (RPE 7-8) | Rest 1-2 min between sets. Add load only when you hit the top of the reps with good form. [STR-05, STR-35] |

- Youth guidance says 2-3 sessions a week on non-consecutive days, so I'd cut your 4 to 3. [POP-84]
- Train all the major muscle groups, not just chest and arms. [STR-01]
- If you miss a day, do a short version: 1-2 sets of the main lifts.
- Next week, add a rep or a small amount of weight on lifts where it felt easy.

**A few questions so I can fine-tune this:**
- Do you usually eat 3 meals a day, and do you ever skip them?
- Can you lift with a spotter or in a gym with staff?
- Do you have any health condition, pain or injury? Do you feel dizzy or get chest pain when you exercise?
- How long have you been lifting, and what do you bench now?

If you ever feel pushed toward steroids or "bodybuilding" products, or you're worried about your weight or body, talk to a doctor or a sports dietitian. A parent or guardian can come along.
