## Response

**Yes, you can keep running and lifting.** Without complications, staying active in pregnancy is considered safe and desirable (well established). The guideline minimum is 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, and your current training may already meet that.

Because your midwife's OK was general, tell her you run about 30 km a week and lift twice a week, and check she's happy with that. Women training well above the guideline should have specialist supervision, so ask whether she thinks this applies to you.

**What to do**
- **Pelvic floor:** train it daily, and ask your midwife or a pelvic health physio to check your technique. This is the one specific addition the guidelines recommend.
- **Running:** there's no evidence-based km limit or heart rate ceiling for pregnancy in my sources. My suggestion is to keep most runs at a pace where you can talk in full sentences, and cut distance or pace as needed.
- **Lifting:** my sources don't set load limits or say whether to avoid lying on your back. My suggestion is to stop 2-3 reps short of failure, as you would anyway, and reduce the weight or swap any exercise that feels uncomfortable or that your bump gets in the way of. Ask your midwife about exercises done on your back.

**What to avoid**
- Anything with a risk of contact or a blow to the belly.
- Activities with a high risk of falling, such as downhill skiing, surfing, off-road cycling, gymnastics or horse riding.
- Hot yoga or hot Pilates, scuba diving and skydiving.
- Activity above about 6,000 ft (1,800 m), unless you live at altitude.
- If you run on trails, ask your midwife about the fall risk before continuing.

**When to stop**

Stop exercising and contact your midwife or obstetric provider if you have any of these:
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking from the vagina
- Regular painful contractions
- Dizziness or feeling faint
- Shortness of breath before you start exercising
- Chest pain
- Headache
- Muscle weakness
- Calf pain or swelling

Call 112 (999 in the UK) if you collapse, have severe difficulty breathing, or have chest pain that is tight or squeezing or spreads to your arm, neck or jaw.

If your midwife diagnoses a new complication, such as high blood pressure or preeclampsia, pause training and check with her before restarting. Some complications limit you to everyday activity.

**If you'd like me to adjust your training, it would help to know:**
1. Do you run on roads or trails, and do you do any hard sessions?
2. What lifts do you do, and roughly how heavy are they?
3. Is anything uncomfortable at the moment, such as pelvic or back pain, or leaking when you run?
