I worked out the percentages myself. The other figures are from the file.

**What this means:**
- **First 48 minutes or so (81% of the run):** your heart rate stayed at or below 157, which is in the easy range.
- **Last 11 minutes (19%):** it was at 158-162, which is in the moderate range.

The band limits are about 83% and 88% of max. They come from elite skiers and are only approximate for any one person, so 157 isn't an exact line. Having your real max does help, but your easy limit depends on your first threshold, and that varies from person to person.

**Why it rose:** your heart rate climbed 24 beats at the same pace on flat ground. This is called cardiovascular drift, and it's usually larger in the heat. It means the run really did get harder for your body, even though the pace stayed the same.

**Next time:** let effort or heart rate set the limit and accept a slower pace near the end. I'd use about 157 as a rough ceiling, but the talk test is the better check: if you can't speak in full sentences, slow down or take a short walk.

**Two questions to pin this down:**
1. In the last 10 minutes, could you still talk in full sentences? If so, the run was probably fine and the ceiling may be a little low for you.
2. Was it warm, or were you short on sleep, fluids or food? Any of these could explain some of the rise, though the file can't show it.

**Check the file too:** it's dated 25 October, which is five days ago rather than yesterday, so make sure it's the right run. If the heart rate came from a wrist sensor, it may also be a little off.
