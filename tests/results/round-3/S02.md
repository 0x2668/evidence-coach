# 16-week plan for your first 50 km trail ultra

This is a realistic goal from your starting point. Your main limit is the 4-hour Saturday, which caps your long run at about 28-30 km. That's enough to prepare you, but expect the last part of the race to be new territory.

No controlled studies have tested ultra preparation, so weekly km, Wednesday workouts, strength sets and climbing targets are my judgement. The principles (mostly easy running, avoiding long-run jumps, strength work) have better support, though no rule guarantees you stay injury-free.

## Progression rules
- **Long run:** stop at the km limit or at 4 hours, whichever comes first. Don't run more than 10% beyond your longest run of the previous 30 days.
- **Wednesday:** by week 6, move to 4 x 4 min uphill, then build toward 6 x 4 min. From week 9, make every other session 6 x 2 min hard, too hard for comfortable talking.
- **Climbing:** build toward about 40 m of climbing per km on long runs, which matches the race.
- **Downhill:** build from 5 to 15 minutes by week 5, on grades up to about -10%. Keep it in the plan every week, because the protection can fade after about 9 weeks without it. Do your last hard descent at least 5 days before the race.
- **Strength:** use 3 sets from week 2. From week 5, if your technique is solid, move to 6-8 heavy reps on the main lifts. Add load once you reach the top of the rep range at 2-3 RIR.
- **Strength in lighter weeks:** do one session in weeks 11 and 14, one light session in weeks 12 and 15, and none in week 16.
- **Back-to-back weekends:** these are optional, as they're coaching practice rather than tested science. They replace Thursday, and Sunday and Monday stay easy, with Monday's lift light or dropped. They total about 45 km, just under the usual 48-64 km, which is fine.

## Fuelling
- **In training:** on long runs, start at 30 g of carbohydrate per hour and build toward 30-60 g if your gut tolerates it.
- **On race day:** the usual ultra guideline is 150-400 kcal per hour, including 30-50 g of carbohydrate and 5-10 g of protein. Use only what you've practised.
- **Fluids:** drink to thirst and carry a sodium source. Weigh yourself before and after long runs: aim to lose less than 2% of body weight, and never gain weight, which means you're over-drinking.
- **Daily food:** aim for 1.4-2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight, and eat more carbohydrate as training grows. Please don't try to lose weight during this build.

## When to back off or get checked
- **Pain:** you can continue if it stays at or below 5/10 and is back to normal by the next morning. If it goes above that, reduce the load. If that keeps happening or lasts a few weeks, see a physiotherapist.
- **Bone pain:** if you have pain in one spot on a bone, especially the foot, stop running and get it checked. Stress fractures are more common in women who run ultras.
- **Same-day medical advice:** get this for fainting, palpitations, a racing or skipping heartbeat, or unusual breathlessness. Chest pain that is tight or spreads to the arm, neck or jaw means stop and call 112.
- **Low energy signs:** see a doctor or sports dietitian if your periods become irregular or stop, or if you have lasting fatigue.
- **Long-term risk:** repeated ultras may carry some long-term heart and kidney risks. The evidence is low quality, but you should know about it.
