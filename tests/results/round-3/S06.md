# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

Good news: you can build real muscle at your age, and lifting with proper supervision doesn't stunt growth. I'd change most of this plan, though, because **it's the weekly maxing and missing exercises that are holding you back, not too few calories or supplements.**

**Food: skip the 5,000 calories**
- **Calories:** the research doesn't give a calorie or weight-gain target for teens. In my judgement, 5,000 is more than your body can use to build muscle, so much of it would likely be stored as fat.
- **Instead:** eat regular meals and snacks, about 5 times a day. Include carbs and protein each time, especially after training.
- **Protein:** aim for about 93 g a day, or roughly 19 g each time you eat. More won't help unless you're eating enough overall.
- **Other nutrients:** get enough calcium, vitamin D and iron. Only take these as supplements if a doctor or sports dietitian advises it.

**Supplements: leave them out for now**
- **Creatine:** experts disagree on whether it's OK for teens. Most sources I use advise against it, while one says it may be considered with supervised training. That's a decision for you, your parents and a doctor or sports dietitian.
- **Pre-workout:** I wouldn't use it. I don't have good evidence on it for teens, stimulant products add risk during hard training, and supplements can contain ingredients not on the label.

**Training: what to change**
- **Stop maxing out weekly:** your strength can improve without it, and technique matters more than load at this stage.
- **Get a qualified coach:** ask your parents to help you find one to teach your lifts and supervise your sessions. Teen lifting is safest this way, and many injuries are accidents like dropped weights, so always use a spotter on bench.
- **Train 3 days instead of 4:** use non-consecutive days, such as Mon/Wed/Fri.
- **Train your whole body:** each session, include a squat or leg press, a hip hinge, a row or pulldown and a press. Add curls at the end.
- **Build up gradually:** start with 1-2 light sets per exercise while a coach checks your form. Then move to 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps, stopping with 2-3 reps left.
- **Add weight slowly:** when you hit 12 reps on every set, add a small amount and go back to 6-8 reps.
- **Rest and sleep:** rest 1-3 minutes between sets, and aim for 8-10 hours of sleep a night.

**When to get medical advice**
- **Today:** dark or cola-coloured urine after training, muscle pain that seems far too severe or comes with swelling or weakness, or a joint that's badly swollen, locks or gives way. The same goes for dizziness, fainting or a racing or skipping heartbeat.
- **Call 112 (999 in the UK):** tight or squeezing chest pain, chest pain spreading to your arm, neck or jaw, severe trouble breathing or collapse.

**A few questions so I can build your first week:**
1. Do you have any pain when lifting, especially in your shoulders, elbows or back? How bad is it out of 10, and is it gone by the next morning?
2. Do any of these apply to you?
   - a heart condition or high blood pressure
   - chest pain
   - dizziness or fainting
   - a long-term health condition, or medication for one
   - a bone or joint problem that activity makes worse
   - being told to exercise only with supervision

   If so, see a doctor before training harder.
3. What do you eat on a normal day, and do you ever skip meals?
4. What's making you want to gain size quickly? For example, is it a sport, how you feel about your body, or pressure from others?
5. Which days can you train, what equipment do you have, and could a parent help you find a coach?
