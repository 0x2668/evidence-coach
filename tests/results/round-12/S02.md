# 16-week plan: first 50 km trail ultra (about 2,000 m of climbing)

I read the endurance, nutrition and strength references before writing this. Nothing in your answers triggers a red flag or a referral. The plan stays on your 5 days: Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, with Sun and Mon off. If your week is shaped differently, tell me and I'll shift it.

## The main points

- **Four runs a week.** You already run 4 times a week, so the first job is to make those runs longer and hillier. That means frequency first, then volume, then long-run length [END-37].
- **The long run grows by 10% at most.** Each run stays within 10% of your longest run in the previous 30 days [END-34]. Your 15 km therefore becomes about 16, 17.5, 19 km and so on.
- **Almost everything is easy.** Race-day data from amateur ultrarunners shows about 86% of race time below the first ventilatory threshold [END-40]. "Easy" here is Z1 in the 3-zone model: you can speak in full sentences. Use the talk test and RPE first [END-14].
- **Heart rate is a secondary guide.** If you want a number, your estimated HRmax is 208 - 0.7 x 34 = about 184 bpm. That formula is only for people with no measured value [END-15]. The 77% HRmax race average in that study would be about 142 bpm. Treat that as a reference, not a hard cap.
- **Heart rate drifts upward on long runs.** Slow down rather than chase the number [END-49].
- **Hike the steep parts.** Climbing costs more than 5 times the flat energy per metre, and at very steep grades walking is cheaper than running. Judge climbs by effort, not pace [END-46].
- **Two strength sessions a week**, including heavy sets [END-53]. This also reduces injuries [MON-39].
- **Your weekly volume will stay below typical ultra levels.** Experienced ultrarunners average about 66-83 km a week [END-37], and your time budget caps you lower. The evidence for ultra preparation is mostly expert opinion, with no controlled trials of volume or long-run length, so treat all numbers as sensible defaults.
- **The 50 km will take longer than your longest training run.** Your Saturday limit is 4 hours. Race-day pacing therefore needs a deliberately slow start and walking breaks.

- **Back-to-back runs (weeks 10 and 13):**
  - These are common coaching practice but untested in studies. The usual suggestion is about twice before a first 50 km [END-45].
  - Your time limits cap each pair at about 40 km over two days, below the 48-64 km the practitioner source mentions. That is fine for your first ultra.
  - Skip them if you feel any niggle or are very tired. Keep two easy days afterwards (Sun and Mon).
- **Tapering:**
  - About 2 weeks, cutting volume by about 41-60% and keeping intensity and frequency [END-50]. Week 16 is lower than that range only because the race itself is the big session. That is my judgement.
  - The hardest block sits just before the taper.
- **Climbing in training:** the race has about 40 m of climb per km (2,000 m over 50 km, my calculation). Aim for a similar ratio on your long runs as they get longer. If you have no hills nearby, use treadmill incline or stairs for Wednesday.
- **Downhill running:**
  - Keep the short, controlled descents on Wednesdays. They build tolerance and fade after about 9 weeks without them [END-41, END-44].
  - Increase gradually to about 15 min total of descending.
  - Avoid hard, long descents in weeks 12-15. A hard downhill session needs 4-5 days before a key workout or race [END-42, END-43].
- **Strength progression:**
  - Weeks 1-3: 2 sets of 8 at 3 RIR to learn the movements.
  - Weeks 5-12: 3 sets and moderate-to-heavy loads. Heavy sets of about 5-6 reps at 2 RIR should approach 80% or more of your 1RM once your technique is solid [STR-03, STR-05, END-53].
  - Down weeks: cut sets, not just effort.
  - Weeks 13-14: 2 sets to maintain.
  - Week 15: one lighter session.
  - Week 16: nothing heavy for the legs. That timing is my judgement.
  - Keep lifting and hard runs at least 3 h apart [STR-43].

## Fuelling and fluids (practise on every long run)

- **Daily protein:** 1.4-2.0 g/kg/day [NUT-01]. Don't try to lose weight during this build.
- **During runs longer than 60 minutes:**
  - Start at 30-60 g carbohydrate per hour [NUT-13].
  - Go higher only if you've practised it and your stomach tolerates it. Experts disagree about 90 g/h in ultras: ACSM supports it for long events, while ISSN calls it unrealistic beyond about 6 hours [NUT-18, NUT-19].
  - The ISSN ultra position stand suggests 150-400 kcal per hour from varied foods, with more savoury foods as the race gets longer [NUT-18].
- **Fluids:**
  - About 450-750 mL per hour for ultras [NUT-20].
  - Take sodium on runs over 2 hours or if your sweat is salty [NUT-28].
  - Weigh yourself before and after a couple of long runs to estimate your sweat rate [NUT-32].
  - Don't drink more than you lose. Weight gain during a long event means over-drinking.
  - Drinking to thirst versus a plan is contested. A reasonable approach for you: thirst plus a planned sodium source until you know your sweat rate.
- **Gut problems:** keep practising the race-day plan in training [NUT-22]. If problems persist, see a dietitian.

## Safety notes

- **Pain rule:** pain should stay at 5/10 or below during and right after a session, and be back to baseline by next morning [SAF-30]. If it exceeds that or lasts a few weeks, see a physiotherapist.
- **Same-day medical advice:**
  - Localised bone pain, especially in the foot. Stress fractures are more common in women ultrarunners, and you should stop running and get it assessed [END-39].
  - Palpitations, chest symptoms, fainting or unexplained loss of performance [END-38, SAF-20].
- **Irregular or missed periods, or unusually low energy:** see a doctor or sports dietitian. This can be a sign of low energy availability [SAF-64]. I'm not diagnosing anything; it's a warning sign to act on.
- **Illness:** no training with symptoms such as fever or chest pain. Return with a short, easy session [MON-45].
- **Long-term health:** repeated ultra-endurance running may carry cardiovascular and kidney risks. The evidence is low quality, and it's worth knowing before you commit to a second ultra [END-38].
