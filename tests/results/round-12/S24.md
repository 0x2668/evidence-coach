# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** Some climbing and finger training can be reasonable at 31 weeks. Your OB's blessing doesn't yet cover the main risk, which is falling. My sources have no studies on pregnant climbers, so what follows is the general pregnancy guidance plus my own judgement, labelled as such.

**What the evidence says**
- Without obstetric complications, staying active in pregnancy is safe and desirable [POP-40, consensus]. If you train well above the usual guideline, the guidance is to have a specialist healthcare provider supervise you [POP-10].
- Fall-prone activities are on the "avoid" list, and climbers are told to ask their obstetric provider specifically about fall risk [POP-55]. "Keep doing what you're doing" is probably a general green light. Ask her directly: "I fall off boulder problems onto pads. Are you comfortable with that at 31 weeks?" Better still, ask a sports-medicine doctor who sees pregnant athletes.
- I found no evidence on hangboarding in pregnancy. A hangboard isn't a fall risk if you keep your feet on the ground, but I can't give you pregnancy-specific loads.

**Check first**
- Any complication means you need your obstetric provider's clearance before training. That covers placenta previa, preeclampsia, growth restriction, ruptured membranes, persistent bleeding, premature labour, twins or more, and uncontrolled blood pressure or thyroid disease [POP-37, POP-38].
- Others need a conversation with her first. These are gestational hypertension, a previous preterm birth, symptomatic anaemia, and twins after week 28 [POP-37, POP-39].
- Do you have any of these?

**Stop and call your obstetric provider if you have** [POP-54]
- vaginal bleeding
- fluid leaking
- regular painful contractions
- dizziness or faintness
- breathlessness before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling

**Suggested changes (my judgement, not from the evidence)**

| Area | Change |
|---|---|
| Bouldering | Stay well below your max. Use low problems you can downclimb. Skip dynamic, committing or high-topout moves, and anything where a fall could land awkwardly. If you can't fall predictably, step off early. |
| Frequency | Two sessions a week is fine if they feel easy. Drop to one if you feel tired or unsteady. |
| Hangboard | One session a week is enough to maintain strength [CLB-26]. Use feet-on or light hangs only, with no added weight. You're not trying to progress now. |
| Pelvic floor | Train it daily, with technique instruction [POP-36]. |
| Warm-up | Do your usual finger and shoulder warm-up, with progressive hangs on a 15-20 mm edge before any finger work [CLB-49]. |

I'm sceptical that "I know how to fall" fully protects you, because a fall can go differently when your balance and body shape have changed. That's why I'd get her explicit answer on falls. If she or a sports-medicine doctor says no to bouldering, you can keep the hangboard (feet-on), easy movement and strength work.

The evidence has no figures on exertion limits, heat, or lying on your back in pregnancy. Keep effort conversational and stop when it stops feeling good.
