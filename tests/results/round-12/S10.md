# S10: Running and lifting at 20 weeks of pregnancy

## Prompt

I'm 20 weeks pregnant, healthy pregnancy so far, and before I was running about 30 km a week and lifting twice a week. My midwife said exercise is fine. Can I keep running and lifting, and what should I watch out for?

## Response

Yes, you can keep running and lifting. The guidelines say that without obstetric or medical complications, exercise in pregnancy is safe and desirable, and that women should keep up their activities [POP-40]. That is consensus-level evidence (ACOG 2020, Canadian guideline 2019).

**Check with your midwife first (the first two are expert-consensus guidance)**
- Women training well above the guideline of 150 min/week of moderate activity are advised to have a specialist healthcare provider involved [POP-35, POP-10]. At 30 km/week plus lifting, you are in that group.
- Tell your midwife exactly what you do, which is running about 30 km a week and lifting twice a week. Ask them to confirm that no contraindication applies to you [POP-37]. The ones to ask about are:
  - placenta previa after 28 weeks
  - preeclampsia or high blood pressure
  - a short or weak cervix
  - growth restriction
  - persistent bleeding
  - preterm labour
  - twins or more
  - anaemia
  - an eating disorder
- If any of these applies, the advice changes. With an absolute contraindication, only usual daily activity is allowed. With a relative one, you decide together with your midwife [POP-37, POP-38, POP-39].

**Stop and call your midwife or obstetric provider if you have [POP-54]**
- vaginal bleeding
- fluid leaking
- regular painful contractions
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling

For chest pain or severe breathing difficulty, call 112 (999 in the UK) instead.

**Running**
- The knowledge base gives no pregnancy-specific mileage, heart-rate or pace limits. Everything below is my judgement.
- Use the talk test (you can speak in full sentences), and do most runs easy [END-14, END-01]. Wrist heart rate is approximate anyway [MON-14].
- Don't try to build mileage. Hold or gradually reduce, and let how you feel decide. Dropping distance is normal and fine.
- Avoid heat, and drink to thirst. Cool, shaded or indoor runs are better. The guidance names hot yoga and hot Pilates as things to avoid [POP-55].
- Pick even, familiar routes. Avoid trail or surfaces where a fall is likely [POP-55].
- Do daily pelvic floor muscle training. Get technique instruction, ideally from a pelvic floor physiotherapist [POP-36].

**Lifting**
- The knowledge base also gives no pregnancy-specific loads, so these are my judgement.
- Keep your two sessions. Strength work is part of staying active [MON-39].
- Stop sets well short of failure, at about 3 or more reps in reserve (RPE 7 or lower), rather than the usual 2-3 [STR-05, STR-29].
- Hold your current loads or ease them. Don't chase personal bests.
- Pick stable exercises, and skip anything with a real risk of falling or of impact to the abdomen [POP-55].
- If lying flat on your back makes you dizzy or short of breath, switch to an incline. The knowledge base has no figure on this, so follow how you feel.

**Check-ins**
- Pain should stay at or below 5/10 during and right after a session and be gone by the next morning [SAF-30].
- New pelvic, groin or back pain, or leaking urine when you run or lift, is a reason to see a pelvic floor physiotherapist.
- If you have a week where sessions get missed, a short walk plus a light strength session is a fine minimum [BEH-28].

Two questions would let me write a week-by-week plan. How many days a week do you run now, and how long is your longest run? And has anything changed since your midwife cleared you, such as new symptoms, pain or a change in your scans?
