# S08: Personal trainer asks for an analysis of a client's week

## Prompt

I'm a personal trainer. Client "R" (alias), 38, male, half marathon in 10 weeks, target 1:45. His last week:
Mon: rest
Tue: 10 km session with 6 x 800 m at 3:55/km, RPE 8
Wed: 45 min gym, lower body, back squat 4 x 6 at RPE 9
Thu: 8 km easy at 5:20/km, average HR 158 (lab-measured HRmax 186), RPE 6
Fri: rest
Sat: 21 km long run at 5:05/km, RPE 7. His longest run in the previous 30 days was 14 km.
Sun: 6 km easy
Sleep about 6 h a night; he says his legs feel heavy. Please analyse the week and suggest next week.

## Response

# Client R: week analysis and next week

**Summary:** R's week was too hard for a runner in a half-marathon build. The long run was a big single-run jump. He did three hard days in four. His "easy" run wasn't easy by his own numbers, and he's sleeping about 6 h. Heavy legs fit that picture. For next week I'd cut running volume by about 20%, drop the strength load a little and keep one quality session.

## Analysis

**1. Long-run spike (the main finding).**
- The 21 km run was 50% longer than his longest run in the prior 30 days (14 km). The cautious limit is 10% over that, about 15.4 km [END-34, single study; MON-11/12].
- Spikes of this kind went with a higher overuse-injury rate. The hazard ratio was 1.64 for the 10-30% bin and 2.28 above 100%. The knowledge base gives no figure for a 50% spike.
- This is cautious practice, not proven protection [END-30, END-58]. It's done, so the job now is to absorb it.

**2. The long run was close to a race simulation.**
- 21 km at 5:05/km is about 1:46:45 pace at RPE 7. Goal pace for 1:45 is about 4:58/km (my calculation).
- It was only 7 s/km slower than race pace, 10 weeks out. It was not an easy long run.

**3. Intensity distribution is not easy-dominant.**
- Tue was RPE 8, Wed was RPE 9 for the squats and Sat was RPE 7. Thu was labelled easy but was not.
- Most training should be easy [END-01, END-07, consensus-level expert/meta-analytic sources].

**4. The Thursday "easy" run was moderate.**
- Average HR 158 is 85% of his lab HRmax of 186. In the 3-zone model that is the between-threshold zone (zone 2, steady), not zone 1 (easy). The skier bands are 54-83% for zone 1 and 84-88% for zone 2, but they are rough for individuals [END-03, END-12].
- RPE 6 on CR-10 is "hard", not easy. Please confirm he used CR-10 [MON-01].
- Caveats: if the HR came from a wrist sensor it is about ±3% [MON-22]. Drift and heat also raise HR [END-49].
- A high HR for a given pace the day after heavy squats fits fatigue, but it is one reading. Look at the trend.

**5. Strength effort was high.**
- Back squat 4 x 6 at RPE 9 means 1 rep in reserve (RPE 8 = 2 RIR [STR-29]). The default is about 2-3 RIR, not near failure [STR-05, consensus].
- It came the day after intervals, and Thursday's run was the day after that. Separate days is fine for interference [STR-43]. The stacking is the problem.

**6. Sleep and recovery.**
- About 6 h is below the 7 h or more adult target [MON-31, consensus].
- After 6 h or less, performance fell about 7.6% in the meta-analysis [MON-37]. That is likely part of the heavy legs.
- If he uses a watch for sleep, it overestimates total sleep by more than 10% [MON-26]. Real sleep may be lower.

**Load (sRPE = RPE x minutes [MON-01])**

- Running volume was 45 km. Sat alone is roughly 40% of the known sRPE.
- I can't compute monotony without the Sunday and Tuesday figures. Two rest days give good contrast.

## Questions for you to check (at most five)

Red-flag screen, grouped by action:
- **Same-day medical advice if any are true:** unusual breathlessness on mild exertion, dizziness or fainting, a racing, skipping or fluttering heartbeat, ankle swelling, calf pain when walking, or dark or cola-coloured urine after hard exercise [SAF-15, SAF-20, SAF-51].
- **Physio or GP soon if:** localised bone pain (shin or foot) after the long run, or fatigue and falling performance lasting weeks despite less training [SAF-61, END-39]. Any pain should stay at or below 5/10 and be back to baseline by next morning [SAF-30].

Also:
- Is the 6 h sleep by choice, or is it hard to sleep? If he has insomnia or daytime sleepiness despite enough time in bed, refer him to a sleep specialist [MON-36].
- What were his weekly km and longest runs for the 4-6 weeks before this?
- What is his most recent race or hard 5-10 km time? Without it I can't check whether 1:45 is realistic. The 800s don't give a critical-speed estimate, which needs three maximal efforts of 3-15 min [END-19].
- Does he have any injuries, medical conditions or PAR-Q+ "yes" answers? I have no screening on file.

- **Running total:** 36 km, about 20% down from 45 km. The reduction size is my judgement; the knowledge base gives no figure for a run-volume recovery week.
- **Purpose:** absorb the spike, keep one quality session and one strength session, and bring sleep up.
- **Sleep actions:** aim for 7 h or more [MON-31]. Offer a nap under 30 min between 13:00 and 16:00 [MON-34]. Move the Tuesday quality session to morning if he can [MON-37, MON-38].
- **Progression rule:** if all sessions land at or below planned effort and check-ins are normal, return to building. Add 1-2 km a week to the long run, still easy (my judgement, within END-34). Add a second, short strength session, since runners benefit from 2-3 a week including heavy loads [END-53, meta-analysis]. Do not add both changes at once.
- **If check-ins are still worse than normal after this week:** hold the load or cut further before adding anything [STR-30, STR-32].
- **Missed sessions:** do not stack them. The minimum version is 30 min zone 1 plus squats 2 x 6 [BEH-28]. The long run stays.
- **Race timeline:** weeks 9-10 are the taper. Cut volume by about 41-60% and keep intensity and frequency [END-50, meta-analysis].
