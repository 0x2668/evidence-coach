## Response

Yes, you can keep doing both. With a healthy pregnancy, staying active is considered safe and desirable, and this is well established. The target is at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, and your usual training probably already covers that.

Do check that your midwife knows you mean 30 km of running a week plus lifting, not just "exercise" in general. Guidelines also recommend specialist oversight for pregnant athletes or anyone training well above the guideline amount, so ask if you plan to push harder than this.

**Stop and contact your midwife or maternity unit if you get:**
- vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking from the vagina
- regular, painful contractions
- dizziness or feeling faint
- shortness of breath before you've even started exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling

If chest pain is tight or spreads to your arm, neck or jaw, or you have severe trouble breathing or collapse, call 112 (999 in the UK).

**What to avoid:**
- **Impact or falls:** anything with a risk of contact, a blow to the belly or a fall, such as downhill skiing, surfing, off-road cycling or riding.
- **Trails:** if you run on trails, ask your midwife about the fall risk first.
- **Other activities:** hot yoga or hot Pilates, scuba diving and skydiving.
- **Altitude:** activity above 6,000 ft (about 1,800 m), unless you live at altitude.

**What to add:** do pelvic floor exercises every day, and ask your midwife or a pelvic floor physio to check your technique.

**Where the evidence runs out:** the guidance I rely on gives no specific limits for lifting loads, heart rate, lying on your back or overheating. These suggestions are my own cautious judgement:
- **Running:** keep most runs at a pace where you can talk in full sentences, and let pace and distance drop as your body changes.
- **Lifting:** stop with 2-3 reps still in the tank, skip max attempts, and swap any exercise that feels uncomfortable.
- **Lying on your back:** ask your midwife, or use an incline or side-lying version in the meantime.
- **Heat:** train in the cooler part of the day and drink to thirst.

If you'd like, I can adapt your training week. To do that, I'd need to know:
1. Do you run on roads or trails, and how many runs do you do a week?
2. Which lifts do you do, and roughly how heavy?
3. Do you have any pain, leaking or heaviness when you run?
