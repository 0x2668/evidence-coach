# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**I'd stop bouldering now, because it's the part with the real risk, and I'd change your hangboard work to feet-on-the-floor hangs.** You can keep climbing in a form where you don't fall. Studies don't cover pregnant climbers specifically, so this applies general pregnancy guidance, plus my own judgement where noted.

## Why change anything
- **Bouldering:** ACOG, the US obstetricians' college, says to avoid fall-prone activities and anything with an abdominal-impact risk. It doesn't ban climbing, but it tells climbers to ask their obstetric provider about fall risk.
- **Knowing how to fall:** your 12 years of practice help protect your joints, but they can't guarantee how you land, especially when a foot slips unexpectedly. At 31 weeks, I don't think the benefit of bouldering justifies a hard landing, even if the chance is small.
- **Your OB's advice:** "keep doing what you're doing" is a useful general clearance, but your OB may not have pictured repeated ground falls or near-failure hangs. Pregnant athletes and women training well above the guidelines should have specialist healthcare supervision, so it's worth asking directly.

**What to ask:** "I boulder without a rope, with falls onto mats, and I hang my full body weight from my fingers close to failure. Given my pregnancy, are you OK with either of these? Is roped climbing with no falls OK?"

## What to do instead until you've spoken to your OB

| Day | Session / exercise | Sets x reps or time | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Climbing day 1 | Top-rope on easy terrain, or low traversing | Your usual session length, or shorter | You can talk in full sentences | Climbing option only if your OB agrees. Keep the rope snug and use a belayer you trust. When traversing, stay low enough to step down. |
| Climbing day 2 | Same as day 1 | Same | Same | Stop early if you feel off. |
| Once or twice a week | Feet-on-floor hangs on an 18-22 mm edge | About 20 s hangs, a few sets | Light strain in the forearms | Half-crimp, no failure, and don't hold your breath. |
| Twice a week | General strength for major muscle groups, plus shoulder and forearm extensor work | Keep it familiar and don't add load | Well short of failure | Can share a climbing day. |
| Daily | Pelvic floor training | Ideally get the technique from a pelvic floor physio | N/A | This is recommended during pregnancy. |

**How to judge it:**
- **Goal:** the aim is to keep climbing and stay moving, not to get stronger, so don't progress the loads. Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, and count walking or cycling indoors toward it.
- **Busy or tired weeks:** a walk and your pelvic floor work are enough.
- **Finger strength:** you may lose some, and the feet-on hangs aren't proven to maintain it. Fingers can be rebuilt after the birth, and you can make that return gradually, based on how you feel.

**Where this is my judgement:** the set counts, snug rope, no breath-holding and "well short of failure" are my conservative choices. The evidence here gives no load, heart-rate or effort limits for pregnancy, and doesn't cover harness type, so ask your OB or climbing wall about that.

## When to stop
**Stop and contact your OB if you have any of these:**
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking from the vagina
- Regular painful contractions
- Dizziness or faintness
- Shortness of breath before exercise
- Chest pain
- Headache
- Muscle weakness
- Calf pain or swelling

**Check with your OB before continuing** if you've been told you have any of these, because some mean sticking to usual daily activities only:
- Placenta previa
- Preeclampsia or high blood pressure
- An incompetent cervix
- Growth restriction
- Premature labour or a previous preterm birth
- Twins or more
- Symptomatic anaemia
