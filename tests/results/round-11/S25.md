# S25: Long-event hyponatremia question

## Prompt

My sister just finished her first marathon in 5:40. She drank at every aid station because she was scared of dehydrating. Now she's vomiting, has a bad headache and seems confused. Her friends say to give her a sports drink and let her sleep it off. What should we do?

## Response

**Call 112 now (999 in the UK; 911 in the US and Canada).** Vomiting, a bad headache and confusion after a long endurance event are an emergency. Don't wait to see if she improves.

**Tell the operator:** "She just finished a marathon in 5 hours 40, drank at every aid station, and now she's vomiting and confused with a bad headache. We're worried about low blood sodium from drinking too much (hyponatremia)." If there's medical staff at the finish, send someone to get them too, but still call.

**Your friends' advice isn't safe:**
- **Don't let her sleep it off.** Confusion needs assessment now, and if she's asleep, you can't see her getting worse.
- **Don't give her a sports drink, water or other fluids** unless the operator or medics say to. Drinking too much may be part of the problem, and sports drinks contain far less sodium than blood does, so they don't reliably correct it.

**While you wait:**
- Stay with her and keep her still. Don't let her walk or drive anywhere.
- If she's drowsy or vomiting, lay her on her side so she doesn't choke.
- Don't give her any medicine, including painkillers, unless the operator tells you to.
- If she feels hot, is in the heat or has been in the sun, move her somewhere cool, remove extra layers and cool her with cold water or ice while you wait. Tell the operator too, because heat illness can also cause confusion.
- If she has a seizure, passes out or stops breathing normally, tell the operator immediately. If she isn't breathing, start CPR.

**Tell the medics:** roughly how much she drank, when the symptoms started, and any medicines she took, including painkillers during the race. Only they can work out the cause, and they may need to test her blood sodium.
