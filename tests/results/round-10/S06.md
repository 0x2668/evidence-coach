# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Skip the 5,000 calories, creatine and pre-workout, and stop maxing out every week. What will help most is enough regular food, a full-body program and a coach to check your technique.** Muscle gain at 16 comes mostly from growing up, training regularly and eating enough, so you have a lot working in your favor.

## Food and supplements
- **Calories:** The evidence doesn't give a calorie target or a safe rate of weight gain for teens. My judgement is that 5,000 a day would mostly add fat rather than speed up muscle gain. Instead, eat regular meals, don't skip any, and add a snack if your weight isn't moving. A doctor or sports dietitian can set a number if you want one.
- **Protein:** Aim for about 93 g a day (1.5 g/kg), which is enough if you're eating enough overall. For example, have about 19 g at each of 5 meals or snacks. Real food is the priority, especially after training.
- **Creatine:** I don't recommend it for you. Experts disagree: one group says it may be OK for teens in serious supervised training, while others advise against supplements at your age. Talk it through with a doctor or dietitian and your parents.
- **Pre-workout:** I don't recommend this either. The caffeine guidance I have is for adults only, and youth supplements are often contaminated. Stimulant products can also raise the risk of serious muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis) when combined with hard, unfamiliar workouts.

## Training
Weightlifting won't stunt your growth, but teens should train under supervision. Most teen lifting injuries are accidents, like dropped weights or misused equipment, so supervision and spotting prevent a lot of them.

Find a qualified coach at your gym, school or a local club, and ask a parent to help set it up. Bring them this starting plan. Until then, stick to these light weights and have someone spot your bench.

Train 3 days a week, on the same days each week:

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | Load / effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Goblet squat or leg press | 1-2 x 8-12 | Light, smooth reps | Add this: you're currently missing legs |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Romanian deadlift (light) | 1-2 x 8-12 | Light, smooth reps | Have a coach teach this one first |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Bench press | 1-2 x 8-12 | Up to 60% of your max | Always use a spotter |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Row (cable or dumbbell) | 1-2 x 8-12 | Light, smooth reps | Balances out your pressing |
| Mon, Wed, Fri | Curl | 1-2 x 8-12 | Light, smooth reps | Keep these, just at the end |

Rest about 1 minute between sets, or 2-3 minutes for harder lifts. Start each exercise with a lighter warm-up set (my suggestion).

- **Purpose of the first weeks:** Learn good technique and train every major muscle group.
- **Progression:** Once your coach is happy with your technique, move to 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps, working up to 80% of your max. Add weight only when you hit 12 reps on every set with good form.
- **Heavy lifting:** Sets of 6 reps or fewer above 85% of your max come later, in occasional phases, and only with more training experience and solid technique.
- **Missed session:** Don't double up. Do 1 set each of squat, row and bench, then carry on with the next session.

## A few questions for you
1. **Your goal:** What's making you want to bulk fast? Is it sport, how you look, or something else? If your body or weight is on your mind a lot, you're not alone, and a doctor is a good person to talk to.
2. **Pain:**
   - **Get medical advice today if:** a joint can't bear weight, is badly swollen or misshapen, locks or gives way, or is hot and red with a fever.
   - **See a GP or physio if:** you have pain that hasn't improved within a few weeks.
3. **Health:**
   - Do you have a heart condition or high blood pressure?
   - Do you get chest pain?
   - Do you get dizzy or have you ever lost consciousness?
   - Do you have any other chronic condition?
   - Do you take prescribed medication for a chronic condition?
   - Do you have a bone, joint or soft-tissue problem that gets worse with activity?
   - Have you been told to exercise only under supervision?

   If you answer yes to any of these, talk to a doctor before you train harder.
4. **Food and products:** Do you ever skip meals? Have you used any other "bodybuilding" products, or been offered steroids or SARMs? If so, tell a doctor and a parent, because products sold as supplements can secretly contain steroids.
5. **Setup:** What equipment do you have, how long are your sessions, and is there a parent or coach who can help?
