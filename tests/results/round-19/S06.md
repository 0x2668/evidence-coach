# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short answer:** the plan needs three changes. Cut the weekly maxing, drop the creatine and pre-workout, and aim for a modest food surplus instead of 5,000 calories. Here is why, and what to do instead.

**1. Weekly max testing: stop**
- Guidance for teens puts technique first and load second [POP-49]. Heavy sets above 85% of your max are only for later, once you have more training age and sound technique [POP-83].
- The evidence I have doesn't say how often a teen can safely test a max. As my own judgement, maxing out alone, with no spotter, on bench is the riskiest part of your plan. Most youth lifting injuries are accidents, like dropped weights, and supervision prevents many of them [POP-77].
- Lifting without a coach is a gap. Ask a qualified coach at your gym or school to teach you technique and spot your bench, ideally with a parent's knowledge [POP-47].

**2. Creatine and pre-workout: not at 16**
- Sports nutrition bodies for adolescent athletes advise getting nutrients from food and not using performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Pre-workouts are usually caffeine-based. The caffeine evidence I have is for adults only [NUT-55], and I have nothing on teens.
- Creatine research is also in adults. A minority view says it could be considered under supervision, but most sources disagree [NUT-64]. If you still want to ask about it, do so with a doctor or sports dietitian, with a parent involved [POP-53].
- For most teens, supplements add little beyond puberty, solid training and good food [POP-51].

**3. 5,000 calories: too much to start with**
- The evidence gives no calorie target or safe weight-gain rate for a growing teen who wants to bulk [NUT-62]. 5,000 is my guess at too aggressive for a 62 kg lifter, so treat it as a judgement and not a rule.
- What the evidence does support is that enough total energy from regular meals is what lets you build muscle. Protein is about 1.5 g/kg a day, which is about 93 g for you. You can spread it as roughly 0.3 g/kg (about 19 g) across 5 eating occasions [NUT-62]. Extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle [NUT-62].
- Practical step: add a meal or snack to each day, eat three regular meals, and don't skip any. Weigh yourself weekly under the same conditions and aim for slow gain. If the scale doesn't move for 2-3 weeks, add a bit more food. A dietitian can set exact numbers.

**A safer 4-day week (approach, not numbers)**
| Day | Session | Sets x reps | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upper: bench, row, overhead press, curls | 2-4 x 6-12 | RPE 7-8 (2-3 reps left) [STR-05] | Moderate loads, up to about 80% of max [POP-82] |
| 2 | Lower: squat or leg press, hinge, calves | 2-4 x 6-12 | RPE 7-8 | Don't skip legs |
| 3 | Rest | | | |
| 4 | Upper, with more pulling and back work | 2-4 x 6-12 | RPE 7-8 | Balance the bench and curls |
| 5 | Lower and core | 2-4 x 6-12 | RPE 7-8 | |

- **Purpose of the week:** learn the lifts and build muscle with consistent effort, without maxing.
- **Progression:** when you reach the top of the rep range at 2-3 reps left, add a small load [STR-35, STR-13].
- **Missed session:** don't stack it. Do a short version of the upper or lower session next time [BEH-28].
- Youth guidance suggests 2-3 sessions a week on non-consecutive days [POP-84]. You can keep 4 only if recovery is fine, so check sleep and soreness.
- Mostly bench and curls will leave your legs and back behind. Spread the work across the whole body.

**Questions for you (answer what applies)**
- Any of these? **If yes, get medical advice today:** chest pain, fainting or dizziness, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or a joint that gives way. **If yes, see a physio or GP soon:** pain that lasts more than a few weeks.
- Is anything bothering you about your body or weight, or are you using any products "for gains", such as steroids or prohormones? If so, I can't advise on those, but talking to a doctor is a good next step. It's a common worry at 16 and nothing to be embarrassed about [BEH-19].
- Do you have a school or gym coach you could ask to supervise your bench?
