# S25: Long-event hyponatremia question

## Prompt

My sister just finished her first marathon in 5:40. She drank at every aid station because she was scared of dehydrating. Now she's vomiting, has a bad headache and seems confused. Her friends say to give her a sports drink and let her sleep it off. What should we do?

## Response

**Call 112 (EU) or 999 (UK) now. Do not let her sleep it off, and do not give her a sports drink.**

Vomiting, a bad headache and confusion after a long endurance event are an emergency sign [SAF-49]. Her story points to a possible danger from drinking too much, but I can't diagnose it, and only a medical team can tell what it is. Sleep would hide worsening confusion, and more fluid could make a fluid-related problem worse.

**While you wait for the ambulance:**
- Tell the operator what happened: first marathon, 5:40 finish, drank at every aid station, now vomiting, headache and confused.
- Stay with her. Keep her sitting or lying on her side if she is vomiting, so she doesn't choke.
- Give her nothing more to drink or eat until the crew arrives, and follow the operator's instructions.
- If she is at the event, go to the medical tent or ask a marshal to fetch the medics. They are the fastest route.
- Watch for drowsiness that gets deeper, a seizure, collapse or breathing problems. Tell the operator at once if any of these happen.
- Tell the medics how much she drank. It matters for their treatment.

**Why this isn't "just dehydration":** In long events, drinking more than the body needs can cause a dangerous drop in blood sodium. For recreational runners, drinking to thirst is the safe default, and the aim is to avoid losing more than 2% of body mass, not to drink at every station [NUT-24, NUT-31]. Her intention was good. It is a common mistake in a first marathon, and she shouldn't feel blamed.

Once she has been assessed and is recovering, I'm glad to help with a fuelling and drinking plan for her next event.
