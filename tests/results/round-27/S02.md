## How the plan works

- **Zones (3-zone model):**
  - **Z1** is easy: you can talk in full sentences, RPE 10-11 on the 6-20 scale.
  - **Z2** is steady-hard: speech gets uncomfortable, RPE 13-15.
  - **Z3** is hard: you can't talk comfortably. I haven't scheduled any Z3.
  - Go by the talk test and RPE first [END-14]. If you want a heart-rate check, 208 - 0.7 x 34 gives about 184 bpm, but that can be off by 11 bpm or more [END-15]. A hard race effort or a field test would give a better number.
- **Mostly easy:** nearly everything is Z1, with one Z2 session a week. This is a pyramidal mix, which suits recreational runners. Experts disagree on the ideal mix, but not on keeping most training easy [END-01, END-07].
- **Long-run cap:** every run is at most 10% longer than your longest run in the previous 30 days [END-34]. I checked each step in the table below.
- **Three plan choices:**
  - **Weekly cutbacks:** I put a lighter week every 4th week. That is my judgement. The evidence gives no figure for running.
  - **Hill climbing:** the plan builds long runs with about 40 m of climbing per km, which is your race's ratio (2,000 m over 50 km). This is my calculation, not an evidence-based target. The evidence gives no figure for weekly climbing in ultra preparation.
  - **Trail running:** judge effort by RPE, not pace. Power-hike steep climbs, because on very steep grades walking costs less energy than running [END-46].

### Downhill practice

- Start in week 2 with about 5 min of controlled downhill running inside Thursday's run. Build to about 15 min by week 8 [END-41].
- Keep it going through week 14, because the protective effect can fade after about 9 weeks without it [END-44].
- After a hard downhill session, leave 4-5 days before a key run [END-42]. Don't do any in race week.

## Strength (2 sessions a week)

Strength training cuts sports injuries and helps running economy in runners [MON-39, END-53]. Do the run first on days you have both, and keep hard runs at least 3 hours away from a lifting session [END-54]. The exercise choices and set structure are my judgement.

| Session | Exercises | Sets x reps | RIR |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (Tue) | Squat (goblet to barbell), Romanian deadlift, split squat, calf raise, row, plank | Weeks 1-2: 2 x 8. Weeks 3-6: 3 x 6. Week 7 on: 3 x 5 | 3, then 2 |
| B (Wed, after the run) | Step-up with a 3-second lowering, single-leg deadlift, push-up, row or pull-up, side plank | 2 x 8-10 | 3 |

- **Heavy work:** heavy loads help (80% of your one-rep max or more, 2-3 sets) [STR-03]. You get there gradually, as the table shows.
- **Progression:** add load only when you hit the top of the rep range with the target reps in reserve [STR-35].
- **Lighter and taper weeks:** drop to 2 sets [STR-37]. In week 15, do 2 sets of Session A. In race week, do one short light session early in the week, or skip it.

## Fuel and drink

Treat every Saturday run as practice for race day.

- **Carbs:** start at about 30 g/h after the first hour and work up toward 60 g/h as your stomach copes [NUT-13]. The 90 g/h figure is contested and may be unrealistic in a long ultra [NUT-19].
- **Fluids and salt:** drink to thirst and include a sodium source [NUT-21]. Never drink more than you sweat. Weigh yourself before and after one long run to estimate your sweat rate.
- **Food:** ultras usually use varied, calorie-dense food, more savoury as the hours go by [NUT-18].
- **Heat:** if the race is hot, plan 1-2 weeks of heat exposure before it [END-47].

## Pain and safety

- **Pain rule:** pain should stay at or below 5/10 during and after a session and be back to normal by the next morning [SAF-30]. If it goes above that, cut the session. If it lasts a few weeks, see a physiotherapist.
- **Bone pain:** pinpoint bone pain in the foot or shin, or pain that builds up with running, means stop running and see a doctor. Women get stress fractures more often in ultra running [END-39].
- **Eating and periods:** eat enough for the training load. If your periods become irregular or stop, or you feel run down for weeks, see a doctor or sports dietitian. It is common in endurance training and fixable [SAF-64].
- **Same-day medical advice:** chest pain, fainting, or a racing or skipping heartbeat.

## If you miss sessions

- **Minimum week:** keep the Saturday long run, even if shorter, plus one strength session.
- **Don't stack missed sessions.** Restart from the last level you completed.
- **After a gap:** if you skip long runs for about a month, the 10% cap resets from your new longest run, so rebuild from there.
