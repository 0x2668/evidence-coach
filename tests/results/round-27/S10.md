# S10: Running and lifting at 20 weeks of pregnancy

## Prompt

I'm 20 weeks pregnant, healthy pregnancy so far, and before I was running about 30 km a week and lifting twice a week. My midwife said exercise is fine. Can I keep running and lifting, and what should I watch out for?

## Response

Yes, you can keep running and lifting. Pregnancy without complications is no reason to stop, and guidelines encourage people to continue safe activity [POP-40]. This is consensus-level evidence (ACOG). The IDs in brackets point to the sources in this skill's evidence tables.

**One check first.** Your running is probably well above the general guideline of at least 150 min/week of moderate activity [POP-35]. Pregnant athletes training well above that should be supervised by a specialist healthcare provider [POP-10]. Tell your midwife your actual weekly running volume, and ask whether an obstetric provider should also be involved [POP-37]. If they've already heard "30 km a week and lifting twice" and said it's fine, you're set.

**What to do (my judgement where noted):**
- **Running:** the evidence does not give a pregnancy-specific distance or pace ceiling. As a conservative default, don't go above your current volume. Keep most runs easy enough to talk in full sentences [END-14], and drop distance or take walk breaks when it feels harder than usual. Take extra care with heat and hydration.
- **Lifting:** the evidence does not give pregnancy-specific loads. Two full-body sessions a week is a sound base [STR-01]. As a conservative default, stop each set with about 3 or more reps left in the tank, avoid going to failure, and skip straining or breath-holding. Choose exercises where a fall or a bar on your abdomen is not a risk.
- **Pelvic floor:** do daily pelvic floor muscle training, ideally after being taught the technique [POP-36].
- **Avoid:** contact or abdominal-impact activities, fall-prone ones (off-road cycling, downhill skiing, gymnastics, riding) and hot yoga or Pilates [POP-55]. If you run trails, ask your provider about fall risk.

**Stop exercising and contact your midwife or obstetric provider if you notice** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions
- fluid leaking from the vagina

For chest pain, severe breathing difficulty or collapse, call 112 instead.
