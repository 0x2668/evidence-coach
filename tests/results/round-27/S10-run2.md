# S10: Running and lifting at 20 weeks of pregnancy

I'm 20 weeks pregnant, healthy pregnancy so far, and before I was running about 30 km a week and lifting twice a week. My midwife said exercise is fine. Can I keep running and lifting, and what should I watch out for?

## Response

**Yes, you can most likely keep running and lifting.** Without obstetric or medical complications, exercise in pregnancy is considered safe and desirable, and people are encouraged to continue [POP-40]. These IDs point to the sources in the skill's evidence tables. The evidence here comes from guidelines (consensus level), which is strong, but it doesn't give figures for running distance or lifting loads.

**Check with your midwife or obstetric provider**
- You run about 30 km a week, which is well above the 150 min/week moderate-activity guideline [POP-35]. Guidelines say pregnant athletes training well above that should be supervised by a specialist healthcare provider [POP-10]. Tell your midwife your actual numbers, since "exercise is fine" may have been said with a lighter routine in mind.
- Confirm that you have no contraindication, such as bleeding, placenta problems, high blood pressure or preeclampsia, a short cervix, growth restriction or twins. Any of these limits you to usual daily activity [POP-37]. If one applies, don't train hard until your provider agrees.
- If you run trails or on uneven ground, ask about fall risk. Fall-prone and contact activities are the ones to avoid [POP-55].

**Stop and contact your provider if you notice** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions

**Suggested approach (my judgement, since the evidence gives no figures here)**
- **Running:** Use the talk test and keep an easy to moderate effort, with no pushing for pace. Don't try to build mileage. Staying at or below your recent weekly distance is reasonable, and cutting back as it gets uncomfortable is fine. Drink to thirst and avoid hot hours.
- **Lifting:** Keep your two sessions. Stay about 2-3 reps from failure, which is the normal default [STR-05]. Lower the load or range if something feels wrong. Avoid exercises with fall or impact risk.
- **Positions, heat and heart-rate limits:** The evidence I have gives no figures on lying on your back, heat, heart-rate or effort ceilings, or lifting loads in pregnancy. Ask your midwife for these.
- **Pelvic floor:** Daily pelvic floor training, ideally with technique instruction, is recommended [POP-36].

**Pain:** Aches during or after a session should stay at or below 5/10 and be gone by the next morning [SAF-30]. If they don't, cut back and ask your provider or a physiotherapist.
