# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** The evidence doesn't give a clear yes or no for bouldering at 31 weeks, and I'd change the bouldering more than the hangboarding. Claim IDs like [POP-55] point to the sources in the skill's evidence tables.

**Bouldering**
- Guidelines say exercise in pregnancy is safe and desirable without complications. That's well-established, consensus-level evidence [POP-40]. It also says to avoid fall-prone activities, and that climbers should ask their obstetric provider about fall risk [POP-55].
- The evidence here has no studies of pregnant climbers, so there is no figure for how risky bouldering falls are late in pregnancy [POP-55].
- Your OB's "keep doing what you're doing" may not have covered falls. Ask her directly: "I boulder twice a week and fall onto pads. Is that OK now, and do you want me to stop at a certain week?" Her answer, given your 12 years' experience, should decide this. Your fall skills lower the risk but don't remove it.

**Hangboard repeaters**
- The evidence has no pregnancy-specific guidance on finger loading, so this is my judgement. A hangboard has no fall risk, so it's the lower-concern part.
- Keep it submaximal, with no added weight and no maximal hangs. Stop sets well before failure, keep the usual 48-72 h between sessions, and don't chase new numbers [CLB-26].
- Use a comfortable breathing pattern and don't hold your breath. Stand up slowly after any floor work.

**Until you've spoken to your OB (my judgement, not an evidence figure)**
| Day | Session | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Easy climbing on the wall: routes well below your limit, or low boulders with controlled down-climbing | Easy, can talk in full sentences | No dynamic moves, no jumping off, no hard problems |
| 2 | Hangboard, submaximal repeaters | Easy-moderate, stop well short of failure | No added weight |
| 3 | Walking or easy strength work, plus daily pelvic floor training [POP-36] | Easy | |

This keeps the climbing and cuts the fall exposure. If a session is missed, don't make it up. Do the walking and pelvic floor work.

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you notice any of these** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
- dizziness or faintness
- shortness of breath before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling
- regular painful contractions

Please also check these with your OB, because some conditions limit you to usual daily activity [POP-37]:
- Do you have a complication such as placenta previa, preeclampsia or growth restriction?
- Is this a twin pregnancy?
- Do you have high blood pressure?

If any of those apply, hold off on both bouldering and hangboarding until she clears it. Also tell your OB that you train well above the guideline, as athletes are advised to have specialist supervision [POP-10].
