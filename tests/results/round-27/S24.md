# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** the evidence supports staying active at 31 weeks, but it doesn't clearly clear bouldering. Hangboarding is less of a concern than the falling. I'd keep the hangboard in a reduced form and change the bouldering.

**Why bouldering is the open question**
- Guidelines say exercise in pregnancy is safe and desirable when there are no obstetric or medical complications [POP-40, consensus]. That covers your hangboard sessions in principle.
- The same guidance lists fall-prone activities as ones to avoid. For climbers specifically, it says to ask the obstetric provider about fall risk [POP-55, consensus]. "Keep doing what you're doing" is a general answer, not a reply to that question.
- Knowing how to fall lowers your risk but doesn't remove it. The evidence here doesn't give a figure for it. I also have no evidence on hangboard loads in pregnancy, or on joint looseness, so the changes below are my judgement.

**Questions for your OB (and midwife, if you have one)**
- "I fall onto a mat several times a session. Is that acceptable for me, given my placenta position and how this pregnancy is going?" Placenta previa after week 28 limits you to usual daily activity [POP-37].
- "Do I have any condition (blood pressure, growth, cervix, twins, anaemia) that makes strenuous exercise a problem?" [POP-37, POP-39]

Pregnant athletes should have a specialist provider overseeing their training [POP-10], so I'd get a clear yes or no on falling.

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you notice any of these** [POP-54]:
- vaginal bleeding, or fluid leaking
- regular painful contractions
- dizziness or faintness
- breathlessness before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- unusual muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling

Also contact them after any fall onto your belly or any hard impact, even if you feel fine. This is my judgement, because the sources don't cover it.

**Suggested changes until you hear back (my judgement, not from the evidence base)**

| Session | Change |
|---|---|
| Bouldering, 2x/week | Until your OB answers the fall question, swap it for low-risk climbing: top-rope or easy auto-belay routes well below your limit, or traversing low. Jump down only from heights you'd be happy to land from at any time. |
| Hangboard repeaters | Keep them if your OB is happy, at a clearly lower effort than usual. Stay well short of failure, use a half-crimp, and add no load. |
| Pelvic floor training | Daily, with technique instruction from a pelvic health physio [POP-36]. |
| Easy aerobic activity | Walking or similar, building toward 150 min/week of moderate activity if your OB agrees [POP-35]. |

If a session leaves you more tired than usual, or you're short of breath, drop that session. Stop any finger pain at the first sign. Hangboard sessions should stay 48-72 h apart [CLB-26].

If your OB says falls are fine, you can go back to bouldering. I'd still keep it at a comfortable level, for example below your usual grade.

To tailor this, tell me:
1. Have you had any complications this pregnancy, such as high blood pressure, bleeding, or a low-lying placenta?
2. Is this a single baby?
