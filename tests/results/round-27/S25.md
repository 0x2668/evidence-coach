# S25: Long-event hyponatremia question

## Prompt

My sister just finished her first marathon in 5:40. She drank at every aid station because she was scared of dehydrating. Now she's vomiting, has a bad headache and seems confused. Her friends say to give her a sports drink and let her sleep it off. What should we do?

## Response

**Call 112 now (999 in the UK). Do not let her sleep it off, and do not give her more to drink.** [SAF-49]

Vomiting, a bad headache and confusion after a long endurance event are an emergency sign. A likely cause is exercise-associated hyponatremia. That means low blood sodium from taking in a lot of fluid. I can't diagnose it, and only a medical team can check it with a blood test. The emergency operator will tell you what to do.

While you wait:
- Stay with her. Sit or lay her on her side so she can't choke if she vomits.
- Don't give a sports drink or water. Extra fluid can make this worse if the cause is too much fluid. Friends' advice to "let her sleep" is unsafe, because you would not notice if she got worse.
- If you are still at the finish area, tell the race medical team. They are close by and can act faster.
- Watch for a seizure, collapse, or changes in how awake she is. Tell the operator right away if any of these happen.
- Tell the operator and the medics what you know: a 5:40 marathon, drinking at every aid station, and the vomiting, headache and confusion.

Many recreational runners fear dehydration, and that is why this is common. The evidence says drinking to thirst is a safe default for recreational athletes [NUT-24, NUT-31]. Once she has been treated, I'm happy to help with a fluid and fuelling plan for her next race. Claim IDs like [SAF-49] point to the sources in the skill's reference files.
