## Response

**Short answer: mostly easy, but not fully, and the effort rose during the run.**

**What the file shows** (computed by the script; wrist heart rate and GPS have some error):
- 10 km in 60 min, flat, steady 6:00/km pace.
- Average heart rate 150 (79% of your 190), maximum 162 (85%).
- First half averaged 144 bpm and second half 156 bpm, at the same pace.

**How to read it**
- A heart rate that rises at a steady pace after about 10 minutes is cardiovascular drift. It means the relative intensity was rising, more so in the heat [END-49]. The first half was clearly easy (about 76% of max). The second half was probably closer to steady effort than easy.
- Using the rough %HRmax bands from elite skiers, easy training sits up to about 83% of max, which is 158 bpm for you. About 51 minutes (86%) of the run was below that, and about 9 minutes was above it. These bands are rough for individuals, and the evidence prefers thresholds, RPE and the talk test over fixed percentages [END-12, END-13].
- Your lab max helps, because a formula can be off by 10 beats or more [END-16]. Zones from a lab test are better still. If you have the lab's threshold values (LT1/VT1), use those as the easy ceiling.

**The check that decides it:** could you speak in full sentences through the whole run, including the last 20 minutes? If yes, it was easy. If you were only getting out short phrases, it drifted into the middle zone [END-14, END-66]. The evidence for this check is expert opinion.

**Next time**
- Set a heart-rate ceiling of about 150-155 bpm and let pace slow if the heart rate climbs. Slowing down is the correct response to drift, not a loss of fitness [END-49].
- If the run was hot, expect more drift.
