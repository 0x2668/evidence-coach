# S10: Running and lifting at 20 weeks of pregnancy

## Prompt

I'm 20 weeks pregnant, healthy pregnancy so far, and before I was running about 30 km a week and lifting twice a week. My midwife said exercise is fine. Can I keep running and lifting, and what should I watch out for?

## Response

Yes, you can keep running and lifting. Your midwife has cleared exercise, and for a pregnancy without complications the guidance says activity is safe and worth continuing [POP-40, POP-35; consensus guidelines, so this is well established]. The IDs in brackets point to the sources listed in the skill's evidence tables.

**Two things to know first**
- You run well above the guideline minimum of 150 minutes a week of moderate activity. For pregnant athletes training that far above it, the guidance is to have a specialist healthcare provider involved [POP-10]. Please tell your midwife or obstetrician your actual numbers (30 km a week plus two lifting sessions) and not only "I exercise".
- "Exercise is fine" should mean you have no contraindication. If you have any of the conditions below, check with your obstetric provider before you continue [POP-37, POP-38, POP-39]:
  - bleeding that is persistent and unexplained
  - ruptured membranes or premature labour
  - placenta previa after 28 weeks
  - preeclampsia or growth restriction
  - a weak cervix
  - gestational hypertension
  - a previous preterm birth
  - recurrent pregnancy loss
  - anaemia that causes symptoms
  - twins

**What the evidence gives and doesn't give**
The knowledge base has no figures for running pace or heart-rate ceilings, for lifting loads, for lying on your back, or for heat in pregnancy. So the next bullets are my judgement, not evidence:
- **Running:** Keep it comfortable, at a pace where you can talk in full sentences. Don't chase pace or distance.
- **Reduce volume if it feels like too much:** Cut it if you're more tired than usual or if your usual route has become uncomfortable. Pregnancy changes how hard the same run feels.
- **Lifting:** Keep your current exercises, but use lighter loads than before and stop each set with a few reps in reserve. Skip anything that hurts or that you can't do with control.
- **Pelvic floor:** Do daily pelvic floor muscle training, ideally with a technique check from a physiotherapist or midwife [POP-36].
- **Heat:** Avoid exercising in heat and drink enough. The evidence base gives no pregnancy-specific figure for this.

**Stop exercising and contact your midwife or obstetrician if you have any of these [POP-54]:**
- vaginal bleeding
- fluid leaking from the vagina
- regular painful contractions
- dizziness or faintness
- breathlessness before you start exercising
- chest pain
- headache
- muscle weakness
- calf pain or swelling

Chest pain that is tight or squeezing, severe difficulty breathing, or collapse is an emergency. Call 112 (999 in the UK) [SAF-17, SAF-21].

**Until your next appointment (my judgement)**
Run 3 or 4 easy sessions of about 20 to 40 minutes. Do two full-body strength sessions at lighter loads. Pause anything that brings on a stop sign above.
