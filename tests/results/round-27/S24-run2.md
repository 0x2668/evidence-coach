# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** the evidence doesn't give a clear yes or no for bouldering at 31 weeks. Hangboarding is a different case from bouldering, and the evidence has no figures for it either. Your OB's "keep going" is a good starting point. But I'd ask her one more, specific question before you keep bouldering.

**What the evidence says**
- Without obstetric or medical complications, exercise in pregnancy is safe and desirable, and continuing familiar activity is encouraged [POP-40]. This is consensus-level evidence.
- Pregnant athletes training well above the guideline should be supervised by a specialist healthcare provider [POP-10].
- The ACOG patient guidance lists fall-prone activities to avoid, such as downhill skiing, surfing, off-road cycling and gymnastics. For climbers it says to ask your obstetric provider about fall risk before continuing [POP-55].
- The evidence here has no data on pregnant climbers, and none on loads, effort ceilings or hangboard training in pregnancy. Everything below beyond those points is my judgement, not a researched figure.

**Bouldering**
Your skill at falling lowers the risk but doesn't remove it. At 31 weeks the main extra risks are a changed centre of balance, looser joints, and any fall or impact to the abdomen. Bouldering also has uncontrolled falls onto pads, with no rope to catch you. Your OB probably answered a general "keep exercising" question. So ask her this: "Is it OK for me to keep taking bouldering falls at this stage? Should I stop at a certain week?" Until you've had that answer, my conservative suggestion is:
- Stay on problems well below your limit, and keep the top-outs and drops low.
- Down-climb instead of jumping off.
- Skip dynamic moves, since you can't predict how you'll land.
- Don't boulder alone, and avoid crowded pad areas.
- Keep effort at a level where you can still talk in full sentences.

**Hangboard repeaters**
- The evidence gives no pregnancy-specific rule for hangboarding. It is a controlled, no-fall activity, so the risk is lower than bouldering. I'd treat it as a lower-risk option, and that is my judgement.
- Keep it light. Use the half-crimp rather than the full crimp [CLB-69, CLB-70]. Do fewer sets than usual and stay well short of failure, with no added weight.
- Don't hold your breath or strain during a hang. Breathe out through the effort.
- Keep sessions about 48-72 h apart [CLB-26].
- Joints are looser in pregnancy, so any finger or shoulder twinge counts as a reason to stop. Pop, swelling or pain over a finger pulley means stop and see a hand specialist [CLB-42, CLB-43].

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you have any of these** [POP-54]
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking.
- Dizziness or faintness, or shortness of breath before you start exercising.
- Chest pain.
- Headache.
- Muscle weakness.
- Calf pain or swelling.
- Regular painful contractions.

Chest pain, severe breathlessness or collapse means call 112 (or your local emergency number). If you ever take a fall onto your belly or have a hard impact, get checked the same day even if you feel fine. That is my judgement, not a figure from the sources.

**Suggested week, until your OB answers**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps or time / zone | RPE | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Easy bouldering | 45-60 min, well below your max grades | 3-4/10 (Z1-Z2, can talk in full sentences) | No dynamic moves; down-climb |
| 2 | Pelvic floor work | Daily, with technique instruction | Easy | Daily is recommended [POP-36] |
| 3 | Gentle hangboard, half-crimp | 2 sets of 4 hangs of 10 s, 5 s rest, 1 min between sets | Stop with 3+ reps in reserve | Bodyweight only [CLB-06] |
| 4-5 | Walking or easy aerobic work | 20-30 min | Easy | Builds toward 150 min/week of moderate activity [POP-35] |
| 6 | Optional second easy bouldering session | 45 min | 3-4/10 | Skip if tired |

The aim this week is to stay active at low risk while you get a clear answer on falls. Next week, keep the same load if everything feels normal, and drop to one bouldering session if you feel off. If you miss a session, don't make it up. Do 20 minutes of walking instead.

Two questions would help me tailor this. How is the pregnancy going, in terms of anything your OB has flagged, such as placenta position, blood pressure or growth? And how many sets and what intensity are your repeaters at now? If you want to look up the sources, the IDs in brackets point to the evidence tables in my reference files.
