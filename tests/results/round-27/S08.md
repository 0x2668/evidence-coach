## Response

**Bottom line:** the week was too hard, and the Saturday long run was a load spike. Next week I'd cut volume and effort but keep one short quality session. The plan assumes nothing below is a red flag, so please check the questions in section 4 before he trains.

## 1. Analysis of the week

| **The long run was not easy** | 21 km at 5:05/km is about 1:47 for the distance, only 6 s/km slower than the 1:45 pace of 4:59/km. RPE 7 on CR-10 is not easy running. | [END-01], expert opinion |

His heavy legs fit a spike plus a week with no recovery contrast. One week does not meet the pattern of weeks of falling performance that would point to overtraining. Self-report outweighs device data, so I'd treat the heavy legs as real and act on them [MON-18].

**Race goal.** Last week's data can't tell me whether 1:45 (4:59/km) is realistic, and the evidence gives no conversion from training runs to a half-marathon time. The 800 m reps can't be used for critical speed either, because efforts of 800 m or shorter are excluded. To set zones and judge the goal, I need at least three maximal efforts of different lengths (about 3-15 min), such as 1500 m, 3 km and 5 km. Schedule these for a rested week, not next week [END-19, END-20].

**Purpose:** let the load spike and the hard-heavy week dissipate, keep one dose of intensity, and keep strength in the plan [MON-39].

**Sleep:** aim for 7 h or more, and ask him how much he feels he needs. A nap under 30 min between 13:00 and 16:00 can fill gaps [MON-31, MON-33, MON-34].

**Progression:** if next week's check-ins are normal or better (legs, sleep, mood) and session-RPE for the same work isn't higher, return to about 45 km the week after. Keep the long run at 18 km or less and never more than 10% above the longest run of the last 30 days [END-34]. Add a second short strength session then. Runners are advised 2-3 sessions per week, and he currently does one [END-53]. If check-ins are still worse than normal, hold the week as it is and don't add load [STR-30, STR-32]. For the race, plan a taper over the final 2 weeks that cuts volume by about 41-60% while keeping intensity and frequency [END-50].

**If a session is missed:** don't stack it into the next week. The minimum version is Thursday cut to 3 x 800 m plus the long run, with the easy runs dropped. Two sessions per week can maintain endurance for a while [BEH-28, END-57].

## 3. What you need to do

- **Heart-rate zones:** talk test and RPE first. Thursday's 158 bpm is a data point, not a zone limit.
- **Screening:** I don't see a PAR-Q+ in what you sent. Please confirm he has done one with no "yes" answers [SAF-01, SAF-04].
- **Check-in:** ask for session-RPE for every session and, for the squats, reps in reserve on the last set [MON-01].

## 4. Red-flag questions (please ask in person)

"Heavy legs" alone isn't a red flag. These are the signs I'd check for before he trains next week:

- **Medical advice the same day, no training until then:**
  - Dark or cola-coloured urine after hard exercise, or muscle pain out of proportion with swelling or weakness [SAF-51].
  - Unusual fatigue, dizziness or fainting, breathlessness on mild exertion, ankle swelling, or calf pain on walking [SAF-15].
  - A racing, slow, skipping or fluttering heartbeat [SAF-20].
- **Refer soon (GP or physiotherapist) and keep the lighter week above:**
  - Localised bone pain in the foot or shin during or after running [END-39].
  - Any pain that stays above 5/10, or isn't back to baseline by the next morning [SAF-30].
  - Fatigue and falling performance lasting weeks despite less training [SAF-61, SAF-60].

Three things I'd like from you: his session durations and session-RPE, any recent race or time-trial results, and his typical weekly distance over the previous month (only the longest run, 14 km, is known).
