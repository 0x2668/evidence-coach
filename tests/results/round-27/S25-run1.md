# S25: Long-event hyponatremia question

## Prompt

My sister just finished her first marathon in 5:40. She drank at every aid station because she was scared of dehydrating. Now she's vomiting, has a bad headache and seems confused. Her friends say to give her a sports drink and let her sleep it off. What should we do?

## Response

**Call 112 (999 in the UK) now. Do not give her a sports drink and do not let her sleep it off.** [SAF-49]

Vomiting, a bad headache and confusion after a long endurance event are an emergency sign. Her story fits a known risk: slow runners who drink a lot at every aid station can dilute their blood sodium (exercise-associated hyponatremia). I can't diagnose her, and I don't need to. The signs alone mean she needs emergency care. Sleep could hide that she is getting worse.

**While you wait for the ambulance:**
- Stay with her. Keep her sitting or lying on her side, in case she vomits or gets drowsy.
- Give her no more fluids, not even a sports drink. If she is already drinking, stop. More plain fluid can make this worse.
- Tell the operator what you've told me: marathon in 5:40, drinking at every aid station, now vomiting, headache, confused.
- If there is a medical tent at the finish, send someone to fetch the medics now. They can treat her on site.
- Watch for seizure, collapse or unresponsiveness. If she stops breathing, tell the operator and start CPR as they guide you.
- Do not give painkillers or any other medicine. Follow what the operator advises.

**For the future:** the evidence supports drinking to thirst as a safe default for recreational runners, rather than drinking at every station [NUT-24, NUT-31]. That's for later. Right now, call 112.
