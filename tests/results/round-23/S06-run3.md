# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short version:** I'd change most of this. The plan is more aggressive than the evidence supports for a 16-year-old training alone, and you can still build muscle fast without it.

## What to change

- **Stop maxing out weekly.** Youth guidelines say to work at up to about 80% of your max, in sets of 6-12 reps. Heavy singles or triples above 85% come later, once you've trained for longer and your technique is sound, and with supervision [POP-81, POP-82, POP-83]. Max attempts on bench with no spotter are risky and don't build muscle faster. Use sets that stop about 2-3 reps before failure instead [STR-05].
- **Creatine and pre-workout: skip both for now.** Sports dietitian bodies and a 2021 review advise against performance supplements for adolescents. The evidence is mixed (one expert group allows creatine under supervision), but the cautious position is food first [NUT-61, NUT-63, NUT-64]. I have no evidence on pre-workout in teens, and these products often contain a lot of caffeine and sometimes undeclared ingredients. If you still want creatine, talk to a doctor or sports dietitian first.
- **5,000 kcal/day:** the evidence doesn't give an energy target or a safe rate of weight gain for growing teenagers who want to build muscle [NUT-62]. As my own judgement, jump up gradually instead of straight to 5,000. Add one or two extra meals or snacks to your normal eating, check your weight every 2 weeks, and adjust. Regular meals with enough total energy are what allow muscle gain. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg a day is enough, which is about 93 g for you, spread over 4-5 meals [NUT-62]. Extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle.
- **Frequency:** 2-3 sessions a week on non-consecutive days is the guideline for youth [POP-84]. You're doing 4. I'd drop to 3 full-body sessions and cover more than bench and curls. Legs, back and shoulders will make you look and perform better than arms and chest alone.

## Week 1 plan (3 full-body days, e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri)

| Exercise | Sets x reps | RPE / RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squat (goblet or barbell) | 3 x 8-10 | RPE 7-8 (2-3 RIR) | Rest 2-3 min on heavier sets [POP-85] |
| Bench press (or dumbbell press) | 3 x 8-10 | RPE 7-8 | Use a spotter or safety bars |
| Row (cable, dumbbell or barbell) | 3 x 8-12 | RPE 7-8 | Rest about 1 min on lighter sets |
| Romanian deadlift | 2 x 8-10 | RPE 7 | Light, with strict technique |
| Overhead press | 2 x 8-10 | RPE 7-8 | |
| Curls | 2 x 10-12 | RPE 8 | Add these last |

- **Purpose of the week:** build sound technique and a steady routine at moderate effort.
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range at 2-3 reps in reserve on all sets, add a small amount of weight (2-10%) [STR-35, STR-13]. Keep using weights around 60-80% of your max.
- **Missed session:** do a 20-minute version of squat, press and row, 2 sets each. Don't double up the next day.
- **Technique:** get someone who knows lifting to look at your form once, such as a PE teacher or a coach at the gym.

## A few questions so I can tailor this
1. What do your current working weights look like for bench and squat, and how long have you been lifting?
2. Do you have any pain or injuries right now, and have you ever been told to limit exercise?
3. Are you skipping meals or worried about your weight or body in any way? If so, a doctor or sports dietitian is the right person to talk to.
4. What equipment do you have, and how many minutes per session?

**Stop and get medical advice** if you get chest pain, dizziness or fainting, or joint pain that doesn't settle by the next morning.
