# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short version:** skip the 5,000 kcal, the pre-workout and the weekly maxing. Train 4 days a week on a balanced plan and eat enough, regular meals. At 16 that works better than the plan you described, and it's safer.

**Your three ideas**

- **Weekly max attempts:** stop these for now. Youth guidance says to start at no more than 60% of your 1RM, then progress to about 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to 80% 1RM. Heavy singles above 85% come only later, with more training years and solid technique [POP-81, POP-82, POP-83]. Lifting is safe and does not stunt growth [POP-48], but the guidance assumes supervision, and most youth lifting injuries are accidents like dropped weights [POP-77]. Maxing bench alone, with no spotter, is the riskiest thing you listed.
- **5,000 kcal a day:** the evidence I have gives no calorie target for growing teenagers who want to gain muscle, so I won't invent one [NUT-62]. A sudden jump to 5,000 mostly adds fat. Eat regular meals with protein at each, and increase gradually. A good protein target is about 1.5 g/kg/day, which is roughly 90 g for you. Extra protein doesn't build muscle without enough total energy [NUT-62]. A sports dietitian can set a calorie number for you.
- **Creatine and pre-workout:** I don't recommend either at your age. Sports-dietitian and paediatric guidance says teens should get nutrition from food, not performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63]. Supplements are often contaminated, and pre-workouts are high in caffeine, which hurts sleep. Experts disagree on creatine for teens: one group allows it under supervision [NUT-64], but I'd leave that decision to a doctor or dietitian with your parents. Sleep and food will do more for you.

**A safer 4-day week (provisional, until I know more)**

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upper: bench or machine press, row, overhead press, curl | 3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 | Use a spotter or safety arms on bench. |
| 2 | Lower: squat or leg press, Romanian deadlift, calf raise | 3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 | Technique first. |
| 3 | Rest or easy activity | - | - | - |
| 4 | Upper: incline press, pull-up or pulldown, lateral raise, triceps | 3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 | Add as many pulls as pushes. |
| 5 | Lower and core | 3 x 8-12 each | 2-3 | - |

- **Purpose:** build technique and muscle across the whole body, not just chest and arms. Bench and curls alone leave your back and legs behind.
- **Progression:** when you hit 12 reps on every set with about 2 reps left, add 2-5% weight. Keep the top set well under 80% of your max [STR-35, STR-13, POP-81].
- **Missed session:** do a short version of 2 exercises x 3 sets.

**A few questions**

1. Do you have any heart condition or high blood pressure, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, another chronic condition, prescribed medication, or a bone or joint problem that gets worse when you're active? Has a doctor ever told you to exercise only with supervision? If any of these is a "yes", get medical advice before you train harder.
2. Do you have any pain, and where? If it's above 5/10 or isn't gone by the next morning, tell me.
3. Can an adult, a school PE teacher or a gym staff member check your technique at least in the first weeks? Do your parents know about your plan?
4. Why does gaining weight fast matter to you: a sport, or how you feel about your body? If it's mostly about feeling too small, talking to a doctor or dietitian can really help. Fast bulking can also be hard on your body.

If you're ever tempted by steroids or "prohormone" products, talk to a doctor first. I can't advise on those, and the risks are real for a teenager.

Tell me your sport, if you play one, your current lifts, and how many days you can really train. Then I'll tune the plan.
