# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

**Short answer:** don't start with 5,000 calories, creatine or a pre-workout, and stop maxing out. Make the training and food solid first. At 16 you have a lot of natural growth ahead of you, so that is the faster route.

**What I'd change, and why**

- **Stop weekly max attempts.** Youth lifting should be supervised, and the focus is technique, not the weight on the bar [POP-47, POP-49]. Heavy work above 85% of your max is only for people with more training age and sound technique [POP-83]. Maxing alone on bench with no spotter is the riskiest thing in your week. You can build strength and size with sets of 6-12 reps at up to about 80% of your max [POP-81, POP-82].
- **Food: skip the 5,000.** The evidence gives no calorie figure for teenagers who want to gain muscle [NUT-62], so this part is my judgement. Eat regular meals and add a bit more to each one, then watch your weight over 3-4 weeks and adjust. Total energy is what lets muscle grow, and extra protein without enough food won't build it [NUT-62].
- **Protein:** about 1.5 g/kg/day is enough for teen athletes, which is about 90 g/day for you. Spread it over about 5 eating occasions of roughly 19 g each [NUT-62].
- **Pre-workout:** I'd skip it. It's mostly caffeine, and the evidence has nothing on caffeine in teenagers [NUT-55 covers adults only]. Food first, no performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63].
- **Creatine:** experts disagree. The ISSN says it may be considered for serious, supervised young athletes [POP-53, NUT-64]. The paediatric and sports-dietitian sources advise against supplements at your age [NUT-61, NUT-63]. I don't recommend it. If you still want it, ask a doctor or sports dietitian, and involve your parents.

**A safer 4-day week** (until you have someone to teach and check your technique)

| Day | Session | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upper: bench, row, overhead press, curls | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Light enough to keep clean form [STR-31] |
| 2 | Lower: squat or leg press, hinge, calf | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Don't skip legs |
| 3 | Rest or easy sport | | | |
| 4 | Upper: incline press, pull-down or pull-up, lateral raise, triceps | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |
| 5 | Lower and core | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |

- **Purpose:** learn the lifts and build a base, with every set stopping 2-3 reps before failure [STR-05].
- **Progression:** when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets, add a small amount of weight [STR-35].
- **If you miss a session:** do just the first two exercises, and don't stack missed sessions onto the next week.
- **Starting loads:** if you're unsure, start at no more than about 60% of your max for 1-2 sets and build up over a few weeks [POP-81].

**Questions for you**

1. Any chest pain, dizziness or fainting, a heart or other health condition, or any joint or bone pain when you train? If you get chest pain or faint, stop and get medical help the same day [SAF-15].
2. Who could supervise you? A school or gym coach, a PE teacher or a physio would all work.
3. Why do you want to gain quickly? If it's about how you look, or you're thinking about steroids or other "bodybuilding" products, please talk to a doctor with a parent involved. I can't advise on those [POP-50, SAF-76].
4. Do you eat regular meals now? If you skip meals or worry a lot about your weight, see a doctor or sports dietitian [NUT-62, NUT-66].
