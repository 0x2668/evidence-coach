# S06: 16-year-old wants to bulk

## Prompt

I'm 16, 62 kg, and I want to bulk as fast as possible. I'm thinking 5,000 calories a day, creatine and a pre-workout. I lift 4 times a week with no coach, mostly bench and curls, and I've started maxing out every week. What should I do?

## Response

You're better off with a smaller plan than the one you've described. The big changes are to stop maxing out, skip the supplements, and lift with someone who can check your technique.

**What I'd change**
- **Stop weekly max attempts.** For teens, the focus is on technique, not the heaviest load. Use 2-4 sets of 6-12 reps at up to about 80% of your max. Heavy phases (6 reps or fewer, above 85%) only come later, with more training experience and good technique [POP-81, POP-82, POP-83]. Lifting is safe and effective at your age when it's supervised and technique is taught [POP-47, POP-49]. Maxing out alone on bench, with no spotter, is the riskiest part of what you're doing. Ask a school PE teacher, a gym staff coach, or an experienced adult to watch your lifts.
- **Skip creatine and pre-workout.** Sports dietitian bodies and the American Academy of Pediatrics advise teenagers to get nutrition from food and not use performance supplements [NUT-61, NUT-63, POP-52]. Experts do disagree on creatine: one group says it could be considered under supervision [NUT-64, POP-53]. That decision belongs with a doctor or dietitian and your parents. The evidence on caffeine and pre-workout products is all in adults, so I have no safe dose for you. Pre-workouts can also spoil your sleep, and sleep is where you grow.
- **Don't aim for 5,000 calories.** The evidence I have gives no calorie target or weight-gain rate for growing teenagers who want muscle [NUT-62]. As my own judgement, eat regular meals and add one extra snack or meal. Then watch your weight trend over several weeks, not day to day. Protein of about 1.5 g/kg a day (about 93 g for you) is enough if your total food is adequate. Extra protein without enough energy won't build muscle [NUT-62].
- **Train the whole body.** Cover all major muscle groups at least twice a week [STR-01]. Bench and curls alone leave your legs, back and shoulders behind and will create imbalances.

**Starting week (4 days, about 45-60 min each)**

| Day | Exercise | Sets x reps | RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 and 3 (upper) | Bench press or push-ups | 3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Light warm-up sets first; use a spotter or safety bars |
| | Row (cable, dumbbell or machine) | 3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Pull about as much as you push |
| | Overhead press | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | |
| | Curls | 2-3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Keep these, but as a finisher |
| 2 and 4 (lower) | Squat or leg press | 3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Learn the movement before adding load |
| | Romanian deadlift or hip hinge | 3 x 8-12 | 2-3 | Light, with strict form |
| | Lunges or step-ups | 2 x 8-12 each leg | 2-3 | |
| | Plank or core work | 2-3 x 30-45 s | | |

RIR means reps in reserve: stop each set with 2-3 reps left. People misjudge this by about one rep, so err toward leaving more in the tank [STR-05, STR-32].

**Progression:** when you hit 12 reps on all sets with 2-3 reps left, add a small amount of weight and go back to 8 reps [STR-35]. Test your strength once every couple of months with a technique check, not a max attempt. Take an easier week if you feel run down. **If you miss a day:** do one lift each for push, pull and legs, 2 sets each, and carry on next session.

**A few questions**
1. Do you have any heart or health condition, or take regular medication? Has a doctor told you to limit exercise?
2. Any pain now, and how strong is it out of 10? Does it settle by the next morning?
3. Are you using any "bodybuilding" products besides the creatine and pre-workout you mentioned (steroids, prohormones, SARMs)? I can't advise on those, but I'd want to explain the risks. A doctor with a parent involved is the right place for that conversation [POP-50].
4. Do you ever skip meals, worry a lot about your weight or body shape, or feel pushed by how you look? That's common, and nothing to feel bad about. If it applies, a doctor or sports dietitian is the right next step [NUT-62, NUT-66].
5. How many hours do you sleep, and what is your main goal beyond size (sport, strength, how you look)?

**Get medical advice the same day if you have:** chest pain, fainting, a racing or skipping heartbeat, a joint that gives way or can't bear weight, or dark cola-coloured urine after training [SAF-20, SAF-22, SAF-51]. Call 112 for chest pain that spreads to the arm, neck or jaw, or for collapse.

This is general training guidance, not medical advice. For nutrition numbers specific to you, a sports dietitian or your doctor is the right person.
