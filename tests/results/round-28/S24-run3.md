# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** the hangboard is the easier part to defend. Bouldering falls are the real question, and your OB's "keep doing what you're doing" may not have been a decision about falls. The guidance I work from (ACOG, 2024, consensus level [POP-55]) lists fall-prone activities as ones to avoid or check. It specifically says climbers should ask their obstetric provider about fall risk. Your 12 years of experience lower the chance of a bad fall but don't remove it. The evidence I have doesn't give a safe height, number of sessions or load for pregnant climbers. Everything below that isn't cited is my judgement.

**Before your next session**
- Ask your OB the specific question: "I fall onto pads several times a session. Is that acceptable at 31 weeks?" Also confirm you have no obstetric or medical complication, since that is what the green light depends on [POP-37, POP-40]. Pregnant athletes are meant to be supervised by a specialist provider [POP-10].
- Know the stop signs. If any of these happen, stop and contact your obstetric provider [POP-54]:
  - vaginal bleeding
  - dizziness or faintness
  - breathlessness before you start exercising
  - chest pain
  - headache
  - muscle weakness
  - calf pain or swelling
  - regular painful contractions
  - fluid leaking from the vagina
- After any hard fall or impact to your belly, call your obstetric provider. I can't judge that for you.

**How I'd change things (judgement, not trial data)**

| Part | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bouldering, 2x/week | Keep it to 1-2 sessions. Use low problems, and down-climb or step off instead of dropping. Skip topouts, dynos, jumps and committing moves. Avoid crowded mats. | Fewer and softer falls matter more than your technique |
| Hangboard, up to 2x/week | Keep 48-72 h between sessions [CLB-26]. Use bodyweight or assisted only (no added weight), half-crimp, and 2 sets instead of the usual 3-5 [CLB-05, CLB-09]. Stop well short of failure. | Fingers are not a fall risk. The evidence gives no pregnancy-specific load limits, so I'd stay conservative. |
| Strength and pelvic floor | Add daily pelvic floor training with technique instruction [POP-36]. Add easy general strength work. | Well supported in pregnancy |
| Total activity | The guideline floor is at least 150 min/week of moderate activity, if you have no contraindications [POP-35]. | You can meet this with easier sessions |

**What the evidence doesn't cover:** there are no claims on heart-rate or effort ceilings, lying on your back, or joint looseness in pregnancy. Use comfort and the talk test as your guide, and ask your OB or a pelvic-health physio about the rest.

**Missed sessions:** don't make them up. A short hangboard session or an easy walk is enough.

If your OB hears "fall onto pads" and still says go ahead, that is a better-informed green light than the one you have now. If you'd like, I can draft a weekly plan around her answer. Claim IDs like [POP-55] point to sources you can look up in the skill's reference files.
