# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

I can't give you a clear "yes, it's fine" for bouldering at 31 weeks. Hangboarding is a different question.

**Bouldering: ask your OB a more specific question.**
- The evidence supports staying active in pregnancy when there are no complications. Activity is "safe and desirable" [POP-40, consensus].
- The same guidance lists fall-prone activities to avoid and says climbers should ask their obstetric provider about fall risk before continuing [POP-55, consensus].
- "Keep doing what you're doing" probably wasn't an answer to that question. Your skill at falling is real, but it can't control an awkward landing, a foot rolling on a mat, or another climber dropping on you. At 31 weeks, with your centre of gravity and joint laxity changed, the stakes of a bad fall are also higher.
- Evidence on climbing in pregnancy is thin. The knowledge base has nothing specific to climbers, so this is a judgement call, not a settled rule.

Ask your OB something like: "I boulder twice a week and could fall onto a mat. Is that acceptable for me, given my pregnancy?" Also tell her you train well above the usual guideline. Pregnant athletes in that position should be supervised by a specialist provider [POP-10].

**Until you have that answer, as my judgement:**
- Switch to top-rope or auto-belay routes well below your limit, or to easy bouldering you can down-climb. Skip dynamic moves and anything where you'd jump off.
- Drop to 1-2 sessions a week. Keep effort comfortable: you should be able to talk in full sentences.
- Avoid crowded boulder areas and stay clear of the fall zones of other climbers.

**Hangboarding: lower fall risk, so it's more adjustable.**
- Fall risk is the main concern the guidance raises, and hanging doesn't carry it, though you still need your OB's agreement on the training as a whole [POP-37].
- Your joints and tendons may tolerate load differently now. The knowledge base has no figures for finger training in pregnancy, so treat the following as judgement.
- Cut volume to roughly half your usual sets and use a bigger, easier hold with your feet supported or on the ground. Stay well short of failure.
- Keep 48-72 h between sessions [CLB-26, practitioner dosing].
- Stop that day if you feel a pop, sharp pain over a finger pulley, or swelling [CLB-42].

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you have any of these** [POP-54]:
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking.
- Dizziness or faintness.
- Shortness of breath before you start exercising.
- Chest pain, or a headache that won't go away.
- Muscle weakness.
- Calf pain or swelling.
- Regular painful contractions.

Call 112 for chest pain that is squeezing or spreads, severe difficulty breathing, or collapse. Any fall onto your abdomen, or a hard fall of any kind, means calling your OB the same day, even if you feel fine. That last point is my judgement, since the knowledge base gives no rule for it.

**Two other things:**
- Do daily pelvic floor training, with proper technique instruction [POP-36].
- Check that your OB has no complications on file, such as placenta previa, preeclampsia or twins. Any of these would change the answer completely [POP-37, POP-38].

The IDs in brackets point to the sources in the skill's reference files. Most of this guidance is consensus-level, but it is general. It isn't specific to climbing.

If you tell me what your OB says and how you'd like to split your week, I can write a lighter weekly plan.
