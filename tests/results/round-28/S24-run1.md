# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Short answer:** I can't tell you that bouldering is fine at 31 weeks, and the evidence doesn't say hangboarding is a problem. Your OB's "keep doing what you're doing" probably didn't cover falling, so ask her about that specifically.

**Bouldering**
- Without complications, exercise in pregnancy is considered safe and desirable. This is well established, from the ACOG and Canadian guidelines [POP-40, POP-35].
- Those guidelines also list fall-prone activities to avoid, and they say climbers should ask their obstetric provider about fall risk [POP-55]. Knowing how to fall helps, but at 31 weeks a fall onto the abdomen or a hard landing is the risk that matters. I haven't seen evidence that skill removes it.
- The evidence here has no data on pregnant climbers. Everything below the guideline level is my judgement.
- Ask your OB this directly: "I fall off boulders onto mats. Is that acceptable for me, given my pregnancy?" Ask for a yes or no on falls, not on exercise in general. Also ask about your placenta position, and whether you have any contraindications [POP-37].

**Hangboard repeaters**
- The evidence does not give a figure or a rule for finger training in pregnancy. This is my judgement, not a guideline.
- Keep it submaximal. Use an assisted hang (feet or pulley support) or a lighter load, and leave 3 or more reps in reserve.
- Stop sets well before failure and don't hold your breath.
- Skip it entirely on days when you feel dizzy or unwell.
- The usual 48-72 h gap between hangboard sessions still applies [CLB-26].

**Suggested changes (my judgement, pending your OB's answer on falls)**

| Day | Session | Dose | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Easy bouldering or top-rope-style traversing | 45-60 min | Easy, you can talk in full sentences | Low problems only. Down-climb instead of jumping. Avoid dynamic or committing moves. |
| 2 | Hangboard, submaximal | 3-4 easy sets, assisted | 3+ reps in reserve | Skip on any tired or dizzy day. |
| 3 | Pelvic floor training plus easy strength or mobility | Daily pelvic floor work [POP-36] | Easy | Squats, rows and hip work are fine. Avoid lying flat on your back for long. |

Overall, aim for at least 150 min/week of moderate activity [POP-35]. Your climbing counts towards that, but you don't need to chase it.

Week to week, hold the load flat. Don't add volume or intensity. If you miss a session, do the easy traverse only.

**Stop and contact your obstetric provider if you have any of these** [POP-54]:
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
- Dizziness or faintness, or breathlessness before you start
- Chest pain
- Headache
- Muscle weakness
- Calf pain or swelling
- Regular painful contractions

Also contact her after any fall or impact to the belly, even if you feel fine. That last point is my advice, not a quote from the guidelines.

Claim IDs like [POP-55] point to the sources in the skill's reference files. The pregnancy guidance is consensus-level, but it is not specific to climbing.
