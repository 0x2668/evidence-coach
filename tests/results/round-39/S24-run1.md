# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

Bouldering should stop now. Hangboarding can probably continue in an easier form: hang with your feet on the floor, well short of your limit.

**Bouldering: stop for the rest of the pregnancy.** That includes low traversing and down-climbing. Pregnancy guidelines say to avoid activities where a fall is likely. They say climbers should ask their obstetric provider specifically about fall risk before continuing [POP-55]. Twelve years of falling well doesn't change this. The concern isn't your technique. It's a fall or an awkward landing affecting your belly. No source names a week for stopping. Making the cut-off the third trimester, which you're already in, is my coaching decision. Don't switch to roped climbing either, unless your OB explicitly agrees after you've talked about falls and harness fit.

**What to ask your OB.** "Keep doing what you're doing" is a general all-clear. It doesn't answer the fall question. Ask her directly: "I boulder, which involves falling and jumping off from about 3-4 m. Is that acceptable at 31 weeks? Would roped climbing in a harness be OK?" Guidelines also suggest that pregnant athletes training a lot be followed by a specialist healthcare provider [POP-10]. Ask whether she'd recommend one.

**Hangboard: keep it, but easier.** The evidence doesn't cover hangboarding in pregnancy, so this is my judgement:

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Hang with feet on the floor or a step, taking part of your weight | You can never drop. Feet-on hangs still build finger strength, though that evidence is weak [CLB-11, CLB-12] |
| Keep the effort moderate: no hangs to failure or near it | Moderate effort is what the guidelines support. The evidence doesn't give an upper limit in pregnancy, so stay well below it |
| Use a half-crimp, not a full crimp | It loads the finger pulleys less [CLB-69, CLB-70] |
| Set the board so that letting go means standing up, not dropping down | No falls, even small ones |
| Breathe steadily, don't hold your breath | My judgement; the evidence doesn't cover this |

**Keep doing:**
- At least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity, which is well established as safe and helpful in an uncomplicated pregnancy [POP-35, POP-40]. Walking, a stationary bike, swimming and gym work you can control all count.
- Daily pelvic floor exercises, ideally after a physiotherapist has checked your technique [POP-36].

**Stop and call your OB or maternity unit** if you have any of these: vaginal bleeding, dizziness or feeling faint, breathlessness before you start, chest pain, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or swelling, regular painful contractions, or fluid leaking [POP-54].

If you'd like a weekly plan that keeps your fingers and fitness going until birth, a few quick questions:
1. Has your OB mentioned any complications, such as high blood pressure, placenta position, bleeding, or signs of early labour?
2. What else do you do besides climbing (gym, running, cycling, swimming)?
3. What are your current repeater sessions: edge size, how long you hang and rest, number of sets, and any added or removed weight?
4. How many days a week and how many minutes per session do you have?
