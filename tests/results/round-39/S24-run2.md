# S24: Non-obvious: third trimester climber who wants to keep bouldering

## Prompt

I'm 31 weeks pregnant and I've climbed for 12 years. I still boulder twice a week because I know how to fall, and I do hangboard repeaters. My OB said "keep doing what you're doing" but she doesn't climb. Is bouldering and hangboarding OK this far along, and how should I change things?

## Response

**Bouldering: stop now.** From week 28 onward, bouldering is out completely. That includes low traversing and down-climbing, and it doesn't change with 12 years of experience or with your OB's general "keep doing what you're doing." Pregnancy guidelines say to avoid activities where falls are likely [POP-55]. They also say climbers should ask their obstetric provider specifically about fall risk, and a general go-ahead doesn't answer that question. Knowing how to fall makes falls less likely to go wrong, but it doesn't make them safe for your baby. The guideline doesn't give a week number; using week 28 as the cut-off is my coaching decision.

**Hangboarding: the evidence doesn't give pregnancy figures.** The guidelines I work from don't cover loads, effort limits or finger training during pregnancy. My conservative suggestion, which is my judgement and not a published figure:
- Keep your feet on: put them on a chair or box and take some weight off your hands. Don't step or drop down from height to start or finish a hang.
- Leave clear reserve. Your repeaters are designed to get near failure on the last set, so stop well before that and keep effort moderate.
- Drop maximal efforts and testing until after the birth. This is a maintenance phase, not a phase for building strength.

**Questions to ask your OB:**
1. "Is the fall risk in climbing OK for me, and would roped climbing be acceptable, including how a harness fits over the bump?" Don't do roped climbing unless she explicitly says yes to both [POP-55].
2. "Do I have any reason to limit hard exercise, such as placenta position, blood pressure or signs of preterm labour?" [POP-37]
3. Training at an athlete's level during pregnancy should be supervised by a specialist provider [POP-10]. If you can, ask for a referral to a sports medicine doctor or a physiotherapist who works with pregnant women.

**What you can keep or add:**
- At least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity [POP-35], such as brisk walking, a stationary bike or swimming.
- Light strength work for your legs, back and upper body, with no fall risk.
- Daily pelvic floor training, ideally after a physio shows you the technique [POP-36].

**Stop and call your obstetric provider if you get any of these during or after exercise** [POP-54]: vaginal bleeding, dizziness or feeling faint, breathlessness before you even start, chest pain, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or swelling, regular painful contractions, or fluid leaking from the vagina. If the bleeding is heavy, or the chest pain is severe or spreads to your arm, neck or jaw, call your emergency number (112 in the EU).

Giving up bouldering after 12 years is a real loss, and it's only for a few months. The finger strength you keep now will make getting back to climbing after the birth easier. The codes in brackets, like [POP-55], point to the sources behind each recommendation if you want to check them.
