# Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms and abbreviations this skill uses. Translate them into the person's language; keep the abbreviation itself (RIR, FTP) as it is, because that is what apps and plans show. Numbers here come from the knowledge base; the IDs in brackets point to it.

## Effort and strength training

- **Rep, set**: one repetition of an exercise; a set is a group of reps done without a break. "3 x 8" means 3 sets of 8 reps.
- **RIR (reps in reserve)**: how many more reps you could have done when you stopped the set. RIR 2 = stop when you could still do 2 more with good form. RIR 0 = you could not do one more (failure). Most working sets sit at about 2-3 RIR [STR-05]. People tend to underestimate it by about 1 rep, so a set at "RIR 2" may really be RIR 3 [STR-32].
- **RPE (rating of perceived exertion)**: how hard something felt, as a number. In strength training it uses the RIR-based 1-10 scale: RPE 8 = 2 RIR, RPE 10 = nothing left [STR-29]. For a whole session the coach uses the 0-10 CR-10 scale (0 rest, 10 maximal) [MON-01]; some endurance sources use the older 6-20 Borg scale [END-14]. The plan says which scale it means.
- **Failure**: the point where you cannot complete another rep with good form. Not required for progress [STR-05].
- **1RM (one-rep max)**: the heaviest load you can lift once. "80% 1RM" means 80% of that load. **6-15 RM** means a load you could lift 6 to 15 times. Testing a true 1RM is not needed; loads are set and corrected by RIR [STR-52].
- **Volume**: how much work you do, usually counted as hard sets per muscle group per week.
- **Double progression**: keep the same load until you reach the top of the rep range on all sets at the target RIR, then add load and start again from the bottom of the range [STR-35].
- **Deload**: a planned easier week (fewer sets, more RIR) to shed fatigue, not a week off [STR-36, STR-37].
- **Periodization, block**: planning training in phases with different aims, loads or volumes. A block is one phase of a few weeks.
- **Autoregulation**: adjusting the day's load to how you perform, for example by RIR or bar speed, instead of fixed percentages [STR-42].
- **Superset, drop set, rest-pause**: time savers. Superset: two exercises back to back. Drop set: lower the weight and continue the set. Rest-pause: a short pause, then a few more reps [STR-24].
- **Hypertrophy**: muscle growth.
- **Concurrent training**: doing strength and endurance training in the same programme.
- **Warm-up sets**: lighter sets of the exercise you are about to do [STR-54].
- **DOMS (delayed-onset muscle soreness)**: normal muscle soreness one to three days after unfamiliar or hard training. Pain out of proportion to the session, swelling or weakness is not DOMS, and dark urine within a day of hard exercise needs a doctor the same day [SAF-51, SAF-52].

## Endurance

- **Easy, steady, hard (3-zone model)**: the coach's default intensity scale. Zone 1 (easy): you can talk in full sentences. Zone 2 (steady): talking becomes uncomfortable. Zone 3 (hard): you cannot talk comfortably [END-02, END-14].
- **Z1-Z5 (5-zone schemes)**: zones in watches and apps. Their Z1-Z2 = easy, Z3 = steady, Z4-Z5 = hard. "Zone 2" means easy in a 5-zone app but steady in the 3-zone model, so the plan says which one it uses [END-02, END-03].
- **Talk test**: judging intensity by how easily you can speak. Works without any device [END-14].
- **HR, HRmax**: heart rate in beats per minute; HRmax is the highest your heart reaches in an all-out effort. A formula estimate can be off by about 11 beats or more, so a measured value is better [END-15].
- **Resting HR, heart-rate reserve (Karvonen)**: resting HR is your heart rate at rest; heart-rate reserve is HRmax minus resting HR, used to set zones when only heart rate is known [END-17].
- **Threshold (LT1/VT1, LT2/VT2)**: the two points where breathing and blood lactate change. LT1/VT1 is the top of easy; LT2/VT2 is roughly the hardest pace you can hold for a long time [END-02].
- **Critical speed (CS), critical power**: a runner's (or rider's) threshold estimated from several maximal efforts of 3-15 minutes. It can be held for only about 20-30 minutes [END-19, END-18].
- **FTP (functional threshold power)**: in cycling, 95% of your average power in a 20-minute all-out test. A useful marker for power zones, not an exact threshold; riders in one study held it for only about 34 minutes, not an hour [END-23, END-62].
- **W/kg**: power in watts divided by body weight.
- **VO2max**: the most oxygen your body can use; a measure of aerobic fitness. Raised by both easy training and intervals [END-27].
- **Intervals**: hard efforts separated by easy recovery, for example "6 x 800 m".
- **Tempo or threshold run**: a sustained run at a steady, comfortably hard pace, just below critical speed [END-18].
- **Long run**: the week's longest run. Each run stays within 10% of the longest run of the previous 30 days [END-34].
- **Run-walk**: alternating short running and walking intervals, for example 1 min each, to start running safely [END-31].
- **Polarized, pyramidal**: two ways of spreading intensity. Both keep most training easy; pyramidal adds a bit more steady work. Neither is clearly better [END-07, END-08].
- **Taper**: cutting training volume before a key event, usually over about 2 weeks, while keeping some intensity [END-50].
- **Cadence**: steps per minute (running) or pedal revolutions per minute (cycling).
- **Elevation gain (D+)**: the total climbing on a route, in metres.

## Monitoring and recovery

- **sRPE (session RPE)**: session load. Rate the whole session 0-10 and multiply by its minutes: 60 minutes at RPE 5 = 300 units [MON-01].
- **AU (arbitrary units)**: the unit of sRPE load; only comparable with your own numbers.
- **Monotony and strain**: monotony shows whether all days are similarly hard; strain is weekly load multiplied by monotony. A cue to add contrast between hard and easy days, not an injury predictor [MON-03].
- **ACWR (acute:chronic workload ratio)**: this week's load divided by the average of recent weeks. Contested; the coach does not use it to predict injury [MON-07, MON-08].
- **Spike**: a sudden jump in load, such as a run much longer than usual. The coach flags these [MON-05, MON-11].
- **HRV (heart-rate variability)**: variation in time between heartbeats, measured by watches in the morning. Compare it with your own 7-day average, not with other people or a single day [MON-14, MON-24].
- **RMSSD**: the usual HRV number shown by apps [MON-14].
- **Check-in**: a short weekly review of what you did, how it felt and what got in the way [MON-19].

## Nutrition

- **g/kg**: grams per kilogram of body weight per day. For example, protein 1.6 g/kg for a 70 kg person = about 112 g a day [NUT-03].
- **g/h**: grams of carbohydrate per hour during a long event, from drinks, gels or food [NUT-13].
- **Gut training**: practising eating and drinking during training so the stomach tolerates it on race day [NUT-23].
- **Energy availability, RED-S/REDs**: energy availability is the energy left for the body after training. Too little for too long harms health and performance (relative energy deficiency in sport); this needs a doctor or dietitian, not the coach [NUT-36, SAF-64].
- **Hyponatremia**: too little sodium in the blood, usually from drinking far more than you sweat during a long event. Confusion or vomiting after an event is an emergency [NUT-30, SAF-49].

## Climbing

- **Hangboard**: a board with edges for finger training; "hangs" are timed holds on it.
- **Max hangs**: short, heavy hangs for finger strength [CLB-05].
- **Repeaters, intermittent hangs**: repeated short hangs with short rests, for strength-endurance [CLB-06].
- **Half-crimp, full crimp, open hand**: grip positions. The half-crimp is the usual hangboard grip; the full crimp loads the finger pulleys most [CLB-05, CLB-69].
- **Pulley (A2)**: a band of tissue that holds a finger tendon against the bone; the most common finger injury in climbers. A pop with pain and swelling needs a doctor [CLB-42].
- **Campus board**: a board climbed with the hands only. Advanced adults only; no feet-off or dynamic campusing under 18 [CLB-34].
- **Growth spurt**: the fast-growth years (about 13-15); finger training stays light then [CLB-37].

## Safety and planning

- **PAR-Q+**: a short health questionnaire before starting exercise. A YES answer leads to follow-up questions and may mean checking with a doctor or exercise professional first; it does not diagnose [SAF-01, SAF-03, SAF-04].
- **Red flag**: a symptom that needs medical help, from "call 112 now" to "see a doctor or physiotherapist".
- **Pain 5/10 rule**: training may continue with changes if pain during and right after stays at or below 5 out of 10 and is back to baseline by the next morning [SAF-30].
- **Contested**: experts disagree; the coach gives a practical default and says so.
- **Evidence level**: how strong the support is: consensus, meta-analysis, single study or expert opinion.
- **Claim ID**: a code such as [STR-05] that points to the source in the skill's reference files.
